## The story of the period

### The licence notices, from missing to enforced

At 6:00 AM the release build carried no licence notices at all. That included the MPL-2.0 text (the Mozilla licence) that certifi needs; certifi is the list of trusted web certificates we bundle. Idris had found the gap, and ADR-003 (our decision record on bundled packages) says the text must ship. At 6:33 AM I ruled that the rule stands: the files go in by Day 10, the screen by Day 12, and the Day 13 TestFlight ask waits on both.

The team then did it all before 9:00 AM. At 6:44 AM Odalys's licence check began failing any app that lacks the files or carries a copy one byte off (72 tests pass; it catches 31 of 31 mutants, meaning deliberately broken copies of the code that a good test must catch). At 6:52 AM Idris added the files to the app and the check to every build. Pernille reviewed it and passed it. At 8:56 AM Idris put a plain Acknowledgements screen in the app, with the full licence texts. That was six days ahead of its Day 12 date.

### Widgets: every piece now draws

Idris drew the widget Button and Toggle with no tap, because an earlier ruling (L5 row V1) keeps taps out of v1. He asked me to rule, and at 9:20 AM I ruled that v1 ships them drawn, with no tap until 1.1. Pernille's review found that no test guarded the no-tap rule: broken copies wired to tap actions survived. That went back to Idris (T-0434), and it is done. At 1:14 PM Maren removed the "black holes" from the dark widget card. Those were dark patches where the card drew near-black fills on itself; two new colour tokens fixed them. At 2:44 PM Idris finished the Lock Screen half. 24 shots were taken inside the real widget process, and the full suite is green.

### Web, prompts and projects

Pernille built `pywidget.http`, so scripts can fetch from the web. When GitHub is down, the GitHub widget now shows "Not read yet" where it used to crash. Idris made the app answer `http.status` (Wi-Fi, cellular, wired or none) and proved that HTTPS checks certificates against the app's own list. Pernille also designed `pywidget.prompts`, which lets a script ask the user for text or a number in a native dialog. Idris's review found one High bug in it. A German default of 84.2 would have come back as 842. Pernille fixed it within 16 minutes, with tests for German and French and 29 of 29 mutants caught. She also landed `app.json`, the project file, with 899 host tests green.

### The editor misses its speed bar

At 11:47 AM Idris measured the code editor engine (Runestone with Tree-sitter, a code-parsing library). The bar is 16 ms per keystroke. The first measure came in at p95 29-33 ms, meaning 95% of keystrokes were faster than that, and some runs reached 47 ms. That is a miss, and it is written as one. I granted one day to build an adapter. The re-measure decides on the median of five runs, and the pass and fail lines were written before any number came in. At 5:17 PM the next two gates came back. VoiceOver (the iPhone screen reader) had nothing to read until the adapter made the text field the readable element. The hardware keyboard passes on iPad. The two-window test fails, because the app has no scene manifest yet (T-0478). There were 0 off-palette pixels.

### Pro, and the review loop on it

At 3:19 PM Idris found that Xcode's test store is unreliable in two ways. It kept finished purchases in its "unfinished" list in 9 of 10 solo runs. And it often still lists a declined "Ask to Buy" (a parent's refusal) for over 10 seconds. His tests check our own finish record instead. The declined case is skipped and named, not passed. At 3:55 PM the Pro purchase was done: the Pro screen buys, restores and survives a refund, and a UI test proves it. The price comes only from the App Store.

Then the review loop ran for the last hour. Pernille's review found 0 High and 3 Medium. Idris fixed all three. Maren ruled on the design and wrote the final strings. Pernille's re-review confirmed the fixes and found one new Medium, T-0486: a purchase can reload the widgets before the widget can see the purchase, so a locked card could wait an hour. It blocks TestFlight until it is fixed or ruled acceptable.

### What went wrong

- **Bug of the period: 7 of 103 "green" test runs were not green.** At 4:10 PM Odalys made `xc.sh` (our build-and-test script) fail a run that ran zero tests, or that hid a failure behind an automatic retry. She replayed 103 past runs that had exited green. 7 turned red: 6 had run no tests, and 1 had hidden a failure. Idris's final paywall run is green under the new check.
- **Idris's broken run, 1:31 PM.** His commit 58b447f9 broke a unit test, because that run executed the UI tests only. He found it himself, fixed it in b98b534c, and now runs the full unit suite before any commit that adds app files. It is the same lesson as the 7 runs: a pass is only as good as what actually ran.
- **My miss, 10:07 AM.** While scoping the Home Screen apps request, I found that native prompts had no build line on the board. I said so on the request and added two tasks.
- **My own commit 57b3a90b used a made-up author email address.** I have noted the right one.
- **The session stop.** At 3:20 PM a work session ran past its 210-minute limit and was stopped. No session summary was written for it; its work shows up as events from 12:03 PM to 3:19 PM. The next session started at 3:22 PM.
- **Hung builds.** At 7:52 AM Odalys made `xc.sh` stop an Xcode build that freezes after it has already finished. The baseline was 7 hangs, costing 3,501 lane-seconds (seconds the single Xcode lane sat blocked) across 200 runs.
