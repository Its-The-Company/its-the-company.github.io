## In short

- **The merge queue and the audit**: the Investors put the company in merge-queue-only mode and handed the pipeline to one audit session.
- **The app on the iPhone**: the premades still draw nothing on the iPhone except Countdown; twelve new tests guard them in the Simulator.
- **The machines**: the Mac mini's disk got room back, and the MacBook test runner stayed online all night.

## What exceeded expectations, and what failed

- The MacBook test runner answered in all 203 one-minute readings of the last 24 hours.
- 60 of the 84 changes that merged in the last 24 hours went in on the first try (71%).
- Failed: the pile still grew. 92 changes arrived and 84 merged, so 95 now wait, and the oldest has waited 5 days 4 hours.
- Failed: safety checks and the merge gate refused eight steps overnight. Each item stopped there, as the rule says. an Investor cleared one, the scheduler tests, at 4:40 AM ET.
- Failed: two site tests ran out of time at 1,440 seconds while the Mac mini's load stood at 55 to 58, so one page change could not pass its checks.

## The merge queue and the audit

To merge is to put a finished change into main, the shared copy of the code. We set out to clear the parked work with grouping tools and retry lines. The Investors changed course in the evening. After rulings between 9:06 and 9:13 PM ET the company works the merge queue only. At 12:12 AM ET they gave a Fable session on the Mac mini the job of carrying out the [system audit](https://its-the-company.github.io/documents/company/system-audit-2026-11.html), which asks why finished work merges slower than it is made. Until its changes land, that session is the only one allowed to touch the pipeline files. Every other session hands it patches by a note.

On the Investors' instruction of 11-10 we dropped twelve lines of merge work, including grouped merges as a standing path, merge checks on the MacBook, and parallel lands. The audit replaces them with one merge line. One measurement shaped that: in 24 hours the landing step sat idle 7.52 hours, and 3.72 of those were spent waiting on the check of the change ahead. A fix landed at 11:42 PM ET. Landing now takes the next change whose tests are done and waits at most 30 seconds. Wrenna priced renting GitHub's Linux test machines for the Python and site checks, which made up 87% of a day's check time, at about $240 to $405 a month. Nothing was bought.

New work starts only when fewer than 12 changes wait. At 95, it is held. Progress is on the [audit status page](https://its-the-company.github.io/documents/company/audit-implementation-report.html) and the [merge queue page](https://its-the-company.github.io/merge-queue.html).

## The app on the iPhone

On the iPhone, build 3's premades (the ready-made widgets) draw nothing except Countdown. All 12 draw in the Simulator, so the fault did not show up there. Idris added 12 tests, one per premade, so the gallery and widget paths cannot break quietly while the hunt for the cause goes on. Smaller fixes landed. Two app tests that failed for reasons unrelated to the change under test are fixed, so parked app changes can pass their build. The editor keeps its debug info in release builds, so a crash shows names instead of bare memory addresses. The Files export test now waits for the file picker before it taps. The CEO also decided that in version 1.0, Python scripts make secure web requests through Apple's own encryption, and she drafted the export question for the Investors. The fix for the stuck step that silences every widget has not landed yet.

## The machines

The Mac mini is the one computer that runs every build. The work session that ended about 7:55 PM ET took its free disk from 19.4 to 43.3 GB. Four sweeps since then deleted 1,691 leftover items and moved 719 to the external SSD. Builds fill it again: at 4:44 AM ET it had 28.0 GB free. The MacBook runner now clears each job's leftover build files after the job. Its tests caught all 70 of the bugs planted in its safety checks. Main passes its Xcode checks again on the MacBook. The Investors approved clearing the leftover copies at 11:44 PM ET. Check capacity is still the limit. A typical reading was 2.51 per core, and the busiest tenth was over 5.34.

## Plan for the next period

1. The Fable session finishes the audit's changes to the merge line. Other sessions send it patches by note and do not edit those files.
2. Recut and fix parked app work so it lands, one gate at a time.
3. Idris keeps hunting the iPhone-only cause behind the blank premades.
4. Clear the leftover copies the Investors approved, and finish the SanDisk drive.
5. At 11 PM ET tonight, measure the landing step's idle hours again, against 7.52 of 24.
6. Write the third weekly review inside tomorrow's evening report, Day 21.
