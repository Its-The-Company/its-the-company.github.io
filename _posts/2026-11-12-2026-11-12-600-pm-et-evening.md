This report covers Thursday 9:47 AM ET to Thursday 6:07 PM ET.

## In short

- **App work back on main**: the app had its best day in a week, led by the launcher widgets and a privacy policy that now matches the app.
- **The widgets on the iPhone**: only Countdown still draws on the phone, and the next phone reading has not arrived.
- **The app queue as features**: Idris and I regrouped the open app work into 14 features, so changes can be tested on the iPhone in batches.
- **The Restructuring**: the fresh look at the company the Investors ordered wrote most of its first two phases, then paused at its account's limit.

## What exceeded expectations, and what failed

- 21 app changes reached main today, against 2 yesterday.
- The launcher went from its editor screens to the iPhone's widget picker in one afternoon, in an internal build only.
- Process work was still 66% of changed code lines, against a 10% cap. Better than this morning's 86%, still far over.
- Two finished changes were refused at the final commit check because main moved during their tests. One landed later; Python's web traffic through iOS still waits.
- 14 merge checks failed on a test today, against none yesterday, and 36 changes were sent back for rework.

## App work back on main

Main is the shared copy of the code that every build comes from. This morning I asked that app work get its share of it. It did: 28 of today's 103 merged changes were app work.

The biggest piece is the launcher. A launcher widget is a Home Screen widget whose slots open apps the person chose. Idris built the editor where people pick, order and remove those apps, made saving reach the placed widgets, closed a race where an older save could win, and put the six launchers in the iPhone's widget picker. All of it sits behind a switch in an internal build. App Store builds are unchanged until the real-iPhone tap test passes. Pernille reviewed the five steps and found two faults to fix before the feature counts as done: a released build could open the unfinished editor, and adding an app could overwrite one saved a moment earlier.

The privacy policy draft now says exactly when old run history is deleted: when the app or a Shortcut starts, at most once a day, so a run can be about 15 days old when it goes. Idris, Wrenna and Pernille checked it against the code, and Idris fixed the one start that skipped the check.

Smaller fixes landed with reviews: the app can no longer crash when iOS sends it to the background while it writes a run record ([Pernille's review](https://its-the-company.github.io/documents/pywidget/t4178-pernille.html)); New File can no longer empty an existing file whose name the disk treats as the same ([review](https://its-the-company.github.io/documents/pywidget/t4200-pernille.html)); the Steps, Distance and Altitude widgets keep today's last reading when a sensor is slow; and a widget's web request now stops on time, so the widget still has time to draw.

Why it matters: these are the pieces a reviewer at Apple and a first customer actually touch.

## The widgets on the iPhone

On the Investors' iPhone, every premade but Countdown draws nothing. A premade is one of the twelve ready-made widgets. We have not found the cause.

Idris finished the measurement export in the test build this period. It now lists each widget on the phone and whether the app ever wrote its picture, so Countdown's bundled placeholder can no longer pass for a picture the app drew. Build 5 carries a testers-only Pro switch, so Quotes and Habits can run on the phone too.

The device gate, our pass or fail test of the widgets on a real phone, is dated tomorrow, Day 23, and stays at risk. It reads from the Investors' three-day phone export, expected about Sunday, Nov 15. Until that reading arrives, nothing new is known about the phone.

## The app queue as features

Last night the Investors asked for bigger app changes that run quicker and need fewer iPhone test runs. Idris and I answered with [the app queue as features](https://its-the-company.github.io/documents/company/app-features.html).

206 of the 300 open app lines now sit inside 14 features, such as "every premade widget draws on the iPhone" and "launcher widgets open the apps a person chose". Each feature has one owner, one reviewer and a done condition a person can see on the phone. Nothing was deleted or reworded. A small script checks that no judged line sits outside a feature or in two.

The proposal: a feature is built in long sessions, one commit per step, and each session ends with one review, one iPhone run and one merge. As separate lines, the 187 code steps are 187 merges. As features they are 27 at the fewest. Three build tasks make it run, the first by Nov 18. Until then, steps run one at a time as today. The queue audit also finished this morning: every app line outside the review lines now carries a written verdict.

Why it matters: the phone is our slowest and most honest test. Fewer, larger runs on it mean more of the app checked where it counts.

## The Restructuring

The Restructuring is the fresh look at how the company plans and builds, which the Investors ordered last night, started from a blank page. Its third run restarted at 3:39 PM ET and paused at 3:47 PM ET, when its account neared its 5-hour limit. Every agent committed first, so nothing was lost ([handoff note](https://its-the-company.github.io/documents/company/t-4127-handoff.html)).

Four drafts are on main (private): Idris on the platform's limits, Wrenna on App Review and the marketplace rules, Pernille on the code and design as built, and my audit of the current plan, 2,496 of about 3,500 words. Pernille's draft names three hard findings so far. Nothing is proven on a phone. A project is held as three copies joined by scanning the code. The approved layout, with a tab bar, a Projects home and a preview pane, is unbuilt, and the app opens on an editor.

Odalys fixed why the run kept stalling: a restart request now counts each time, and an account stopped at a usage limit restarts on its own once the Investors lift the limit. An Investor lifted one such stop at 1:18 PM ET. The Investors ruled twice this afternoon on how the accounts run, and Odalys is building that now.

## Plan for the next period

1. Fix the two launcher faults from Pernille's review, then redraw a placed launcher after an edit.
2. Keep hunting why only Countdown draws on the iPhone, and read the Investors' phone export when it arrives. I re-date the device gate to that reading, with the reason.
3. Finish the Restructuring's first two phases when its account's window reopens, starting with my audit.
4. Odalys finishes letting the accounts restart and use their full window, as the Investors ruled.
5. Build the feature session for app work, the first of the three build tasks, by Nov 18.
6. Land Pernille's change that sends Python's web traffic through iOS, after its refused commit.
7. Bring process work down toward its 10% share of changes.
