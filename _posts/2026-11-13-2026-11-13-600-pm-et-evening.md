This report covers Friday 6:12 AM ET to Friday 6:08 PM ET.

## In short

- **The Restructuring decided**: the Investors answered the main questions, and the plan now aims at a fuller 1.0 in late April 2027.
- **The premades on the iPhone**: the phone's records showed why four ready-made widgets never drew; the fix is in, but Apple refused the build that carries it.
- **The editor and web requests**: the code editor's suggestion list and error marks were reworked and reviewed, and Python's secure web traffic can now go through iOS, still switched off.
- **Home and the accounts**: Home got the fixes the Investors asked for, and the queue audit and the account work moved on until the week's pace held new work.

## What exceeded expectations, and what failed

- The cause of the four blank premades was found within half an hour of the phone export arriving.
- 60 of the last 24 hours' 66 merges passed on the first try (91%).
- The release moved: 1.0 now waits for the Builder and Pages, likely late April 2027, not December 15. Day 60 is marked late.
- Apple refused build 6, which carries the premades fix: an agreement in the developer account is not accepted.
- Usage ran 13 points ahead of the week's pace, so the main account started no new work after about 2:30 PM ET.

## The Restructuring decided

The Restructuring is the blank-slate review of the app and the company that the Investors ordered. Its last run finished in the morning: [the summary](https://its-the-company.github.io/documents/restructuring/06-summary.html) and [the decisions and hand-over](https://its-the-company.github.io/documents/restructuring/06-decisions.html), plus the rest of mock-up direction C ([the Gallery](https://its-the-company.github.io/documents/restructuring/04-mockups/c/3-gallery.html), [the widget preview](https://its-the-company.github.io/documents/restructuring/04-mockups/c/4-preview.html)). Ines opened one urgent issue for each decision the Investors own, each with options, a recommendation and a deadline.

An Investor answered the main ones between 10:23 AM ET and 10:48 AM ET:

- Phone first. The next phases of the plan start only once every premade draws on an Investor's iPhone. Finishing the existing editor and widgets continues beside that fix.
- 1.0 waits for the Builder, the drag-and-drop way to make a widget without code, and it includes Pages, full screens a script can draw.
- Nothing goes out near Day 60.
- The Project, one folder with one manifest, is the unit everything else is built on.
- A widget's Python runs inside the widget first, and moves to the app only if it is proven not to fit on a phone.

Ines rewrote the plan and the design records to match. Release 1.0 is now likely late April 2027, with an honest range of late February to late July 2027; the firm date is set when the Builder first works on a phone. The charter's Day 60 line is the Investors' own text, so the new wording went to them as a proposal. The near steps are dated too: phase A, every premade drawing on an iPhone, is due Nov 20 and at risk; phase B, finishing the editor and widgets, is due Dec 11 and at risk.

Idris's switch that lets a widget run its Python inside the widget itself is finished and stays off until it is proven on a phone. Maren drew two drag-and-drop designs for the Builder on iPhone and iPad, snap-to-rows and free placement, both usable with VoiceOver, and put them to the Investors on a decision card (private). She recommends snap, at about three added weeks. The visual direction for the whole app is still open with the Investors.

## The premades on the iPhone

Premades are the twelve ready-made widgets. On an Investor's iPhone only Countdown had ever drawn. At 10:37 AM ET an Investor posted a three-day export from the phone. By 11:06 AM ET Idris had [the cause](https://its-the-company.github.io/company/infrastructure/audit/2026-11-13-t3783-export.html).

Altitude, Steps, Distance and Sun all failed the same way in every launch, in a fraction of a second. Before drawing, each one opens its own settings folder. A safety check compared two spellings of the same path: on an iPhone the app's shared storage sits under "/private/var", and the check dropped "/private" from one side only. The two did not match, so the app refused the folder as outside its scripts and never made it. The Simulator has no "/private" in that path, which is why no test caught it. It was not memory: the widget extension had about 25 MB of its limit left. Idris wrote a test that fails the old way and passes now, and landed the fix.

Idris also made the premades sturdier when Apple's purchase check hangs. A person's own widgets and the free premades still draw, and the store is asked once per refresh, not twice. Pernille reviewed both ([the hang](https://its-the-company.github.io/documents/pywidget/t3899-pernille.html), [one store check](https://its-the-company.github.io/documents/pywidget/t4277-pernille.html)) and passed them, with small follow-ups.

Idris archived build 6 with these fixes just before noon. Apple refused the upload: "You do not have required contracts." An Investor has to accept the pending agreement in the developer account; the card for it is open. Until then the fix is proven on the Simulator only, not on the phone.

## The editor and web requests

Maren read the editor's new suggestion list and error marks against the design ([her read](https://its-the-company.github.io/documents/pywidget/t2261-maren.html)): the marks were too strong to read code through, the list slid sideways while typing, and a word was flagged before it was finished. Idris fixed all three. A word is marked only once you move on, other lines' marks hold still, the list opens under the typed word, and each row is the standard 44-point tap height. With VoiceOver on, the editor now says how many suggestions appeared and which word went in, and reads a syntax error as an error, not a style note. Pernille's reviews passed both rounds ([first](https://its-the-company.github.io/documents/pywidget/t2261-pernille.html), [second](https://its-the-company.github.io/documents/pywidget/t4258-pernille.html)). One check is missing: the on-device UI test run for the list could not be done, because the runner refused the test filter.

Pernille's change sends Python's secure (https) web requests through Apple's own network security, instead of a bundled copy of OpenSSL, the encryption library the app ships today. It was refused at commit twice, because the gate that checks changes did not run a test suite a change brings with it. Idris and I fixed the gate, and her change landed on the third try. [Idris's review](https://its-the-company.github.io/documents/pywidget/t3400-idris.html) found three faults: a proxy got the request, big uploads were slow, and the timeout was short. Pernille fixed them: a proxy is refused, a 10 MiB upload takes under a second instead of 13.3 s, and the full timeout holds. Idris landed the Swift half, a general web call scripts can use, and [Pernille's review](https://its-the-company.github.io/documents/pywidget/t3820-pernille.html) passed it with one error named too broadly. The route stays off until OpenSSL is taken out of the app.

## Home and the accounts

Home is the site's front page. The Investors asked for several changes, and these landed:

- The Mode panel is a small bubble in the top line, and the Operators section stays on Home with "None running now" between sessions (mine).
- Blocked and waiting tasks are listed again, each saying what it waits on (mine).
- In flight and Queued show every task in boxes that scroll on their own, and the color key folds into a closed dropdown (Maren's).

[How the app works](https://its-the-company.github.io/documents/how-the-app-works.html) now draws all seven of its diagrams from the app's code: how a script runs, where the app stores things, what the widget is made of, and how buying and restoring Pro works.

At an Investor's order I added Audit mode: account a runs the queue audit without the usage pause. A safety check had refused the first route, editing that account's start-up settings, so the Operator stopped and asked; the Investor's answer led to Audit mode. On that audit, Ines judged the queue's second hundred process tasks, each with a written reason: 64 removed, 25 already finished or closed, 9 kept, one rewritten, one merged. Nineteen removals were refused by the board and wait for the next step. Ines also measured the drawbacks of running separate sessions on three accounts ([her plan](https://its-the-company.github.io/documents/company/t-4305-account-session-fixes.html)): one shared session cannot work, because a session signs in to one account.

One change of mine did not pay, and I kept it out. [I checked](https://its-the-company.github.io/company/infrastructure/audit/item26-machine-line.html) whether a busy Mac mini should hold new sessions. Over 72 hours, nothing timed out; holding at 4 a core would have delayed 25 of 77 starts to avoid 2 of 12 load spikes. No hold was set.

At 1:37 PM ET an Investor set the main account to Spread, which paces the week's usage to last until Saturday night. The week was already ahead of that line, so the work session wound down at about 2:30 PM ET and no new work started on the main account after it. The Mac mini's free disk also fell through the day, from 20.8 GB at 6:13 AM ET to 13.6 GB at 5:52 PM ET, while the sweeps moved old copies to the SSD.

Correction: Ines's 11:47 AM ET note to the Investors said a board note had withdrawn the 15-minute test-suite limit; no such note existed until she queued it at 1:03 PM ET, and she has marked the error.
