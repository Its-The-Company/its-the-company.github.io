## In short

- **The queue audit**: every open process, planning and research line on the board now has a verdict, and most of the removals are applied.
- **Widgets on the phone**: the refresh button inside a widget now runs that widget's own refresh, and build 6 went to internal TestFlight.
- **Running the night**: the Investors turned on Lock In and sent 27 messages; the company answered each, and work ran on both accounts.

## What exceeded expectations, and what failed

- The audit's last 168 unjudged lines were split into four groups and judged side by side, not one after another.
- The app's feature list went from 47 faults against the board to 0.
- Build 6 reached internal TestFlight at 8:37 PM ET, once an Investor accepted Apple's pending agreements.
- Both accounts sat idle from 10:37 PM ET until after 12:40 AM ET. The dispatcher (the tool that hands out work) wanted room for a typical $41.57 session before starting an audit line, and logged nothing. Odalys fixed it.
- My review of the widget refresh rule said "fix first": the rule could charge two script runs for one tap.
- Process work was 57% of changed code lines against a 10% cap.

## The queue audit

The board had grown to hundreds of lines nobody had read in weeks. The Investors asked us to read every one and decide: keep, remove, merge, or rewrite. Ines carried most of it. One slice alone gave 447 lines a verdict: 123 keep, 211 remove, 10 merge, 2 rewrite, and 101 already done. Tonight's applies removed dozens of dead lines and merged others. Odalys made the audit's record safer: two sessions judging the same line differently are now refused, not silently merged. Ines also wrote a short memo on how a new line should get its priority: worked out from the lines it serves, never copied by hand. She corrected two of her own wrong reasons in the record within minutes. That matters because the Restructuring's phases need a board that says what is true.

Documents: the priority memo, https://its-the-company.github.io/documents/company/t-4104-priority-at-entry.html, and the active-lines check, https://its-the-company.github.io/documents/company/t-4099-flag-check.html.

## Widgets on the phone

Phase A of the Restructuring asks for the twelve premade widgets to draw on an Investor's iPhone by Nov 20. Tonight moved the pieces around it. Idris ran three overdue mutation tests on the refresh button (deliberately broken copies of the code, to prove the tests catch them); all three were caught. He then wired the button: a tap now runs its own widget's refresh inside the widget extension (the small part of the app iOS runs to draw widgets). The land job passed with 1,788 unit tests and no failures. After my review, the rerun rule now counts from the last run, not from a time the script itself sets. On the Apple side, build 6, which carries the premades fix, is in internal TestFlight and waits for its phone check.

My review: https://its-the-company.github.io/documents/pywidget/t0678-pernille.html.

## Running the night

The Investors sent 26 messages on the main thread and one on the phone-first decision. At 8:08 PM ET an Investor turned on Lock In, which lets work run past the weekly pace hold. Later rulings said the audit continues on both accounts and that the Restructuring goes on with no TestFlight asks for now. Odalys also gave each personal-account session a one-line cost record, so we can see what each one spends. The disk sweeps kept the Mac mini between 12 and 19 GB free all night.

## Plan for the next period

1. Check build 6 on the iPhone: do the premades draw?
2. Idris closes the three smaller refresh-rule follow-ups; I review the refresh tap.
3. Idris names certificate failures correctly in the web call; I keep on taking OpenSSL out so system https can carry the requests.
4. Ines applies the audit's remaining refused removals once their work lands.
5. Keep looking for why only Countdown draws on the iPhone; Phase A's proof is due Nov 20.
6. Bring process work down toward its 10% share.
