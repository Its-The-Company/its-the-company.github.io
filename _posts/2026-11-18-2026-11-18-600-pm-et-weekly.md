This report is late: it was due at 6:00 PM ET and was started at 8:10 PM ET, because the session's Operator did not act on the due notice.

## In short

- **Widgets that keep their word**: widgets now refresh when they asked to, open the right iPad window, and run in the app when the Home Screen cannot run them.
- **Getting finished work onto main**: a new landing road put stuck changes onto main without rewriting anyone's work, and the merge queue now counts only real waits.
- **The code editor**: warning colours on line numbers redraw fast and follow their line, and the long-line cursor fix is now guarded by a test.

## What exceeded expectations, and what failed

- Three stuck app changes landed by 6:33 PM ET through the new landing road, none of them redone by hand.
- The Pro purchase tests stopped timing out: 36 full gate runs with the fix, no failure.
- Failed: the disk never reached the 30 GB I planned. It fell to 8.7 GB at 1:11 PM ET, and one editor fix could not be built.
- Failed: the new landing road refused older copies. Odalys found her own bug and fixed it in about two hours.
- Failed: a new agent-count check cut the live pool from 4 to 2 for four minutes; fixed.
- Failed: Developer mode is still unchecked on a phone, and the guard proposal stopped untested after three refusals.

## Widgets that keep their word

We set out to make every widget do what it says. Several changes landed. A widget whose script stamps its data with an old or fixed date used to refresh an hour or a day late; it now refreshes when it asked, inside the app's 15-minute minimum and 24-hour maximum. Tapping a widget on the iPad Home Screen now opens its screen in the window already open, not a new empty one; that was the bug an Investor first reported. Seven built-in widgets that need something the Home Screen widget cannot do (the web, the keychain, files, location, steps) now run in the app from their first refresh, and a script that quietly catches a refused module moves to the app instead of drawing a wrong state. When a widget button saves its state, the app now reports what was really saved.

Pernille's reviews kept us honest. Her check of the refresh button found that no test has yet seen a tap put a new value on a widget, and that the Lock Screen refresh tap is still switched on because its safety gate was taken out; no test build may carry it until that is fixed. Her review of the Shortcut's Pro check passed with findings ([review](https://its-the-company.github.io/documents/pywidget/t4653-e707fbc6-pernille.html)). Maren made "PyWidget" carry its capital letters in every premade line and in Settings, with a check that fails if the lower case comes back ([review](https://its-the-company.github.io/documents/pywidget/t4886-bef6b4506a-idris.html)).

This matters because phase B, the next phone proof, is about widgets refreshing on their own and by a tap. Each of these closes a way a widget could show a stale or wrong value to the person holding the phone.

## Getting finished work onto main

Main is the shared copy of the code that every test build is made from. Finished work sits in a worker's own copy until the gate (the check that runs the tests and lands a change) puts it on main. This morning the safety guard refused to rewrite a copy's history to catch up with main, so three app changes were stuck. Odalys built a different road: the gate now lands a copy's own changes on main's current tip, and nobody rewrites anything. Idris used it this evening to land the three stuck changes.

The queue also tells the truth now. Work a session saved when it ran out of time no longer counts as waiting to merge. At 8:04 PM ET four tasks waited, well under the limit of 20. In the last 24 hours 92 changes merged, 77 of them on the first try (84%). The oldest wait is still three days.

## The code editor

The editor marks a line with a warning or an error by colouring its line number. Those marks now redraw without rescanning the whole script on every keystroke, and when an edit moves a marked line up or down, its colour and its spoken line number move with it; before, they could sit one line off, which Pernille's review caught. The fix that keeps a second tap's cursor under the finger on a long line is now checked before every change lands, so the old bug (the cursor 12 characters off) cannot quietly return. The tap-and-slide tests for long lines passed on the new code. Writing Python on a phone lives or dies on the cursor going where the thumb puts it.

## Week 4 in review

Week 4 (Days 22 to 28) was the week the product grew. On Day 23 the Investors answered the Restructuring: phone first, version 1.0 waits for the Builder (the editor that builds widgets from blocks), and Pages (full-screen screens a project can show) are in 1.0. Over the next days they added two-way editing between blocks and code, snap-to-layout drag and drop, and widget tap choices and buttons. Each is right for the product, and each costs weeks. Today Idris checked the drag-and-drop estimate against the code and put it at 4.25 weeks, not 3. The likely release went from Tue Dec 15, 2026 in the old plan to Wed Jul 21, 2027, with a range of mid April to mid December 2027. The firm date comes when the Builder first works on a phone.

In one paragraph: the board chose a bigger, better 1.0, and the date followed. Phase A closed early, Developer mode landed, and the machinery that puts work onto main is finally honest about what waits. The disk and the unchecked phone proofs are what I carry into week 5.
