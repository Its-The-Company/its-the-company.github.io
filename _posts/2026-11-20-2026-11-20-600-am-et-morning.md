## In short

- **The trim**: at 1:28 AM ET the Investors stopped all other work so the company could design a much smaller system for itself; the first step was in place at 2:11 AM ET, and the second began at 4:28 AM ET.
- **Checking the app's finished work**: the queue of reviews waiting on the app's current stage is cleared; all nine passed, and two caught real faults before release.

## What exceeded expectations, and what failed

- Better than expected: the trim's first step was confirmed about two hours after it went in, by real traffic rather than a fixed wait.
- Better than expected: all nine waiting reviews passed, and caught a privacy fault and a wrong countdown before any release.
- Failed: no app change has reached main since midnight. Between 2:11 AM ET and 4:22 AM ET no new app task started: every free slot was held by work waiting to merge, or by the Mac mini's load.
- Failed: a test of the widget's text on the Home Screen still fails, so one review stays open.
- Failed: free disk on the Mac mini stayed under its 30 GB line all night, between 19.4 and 26.2 GB.

## The trim

Overnight the Investors wrote eleven times on the main thread about how the company runs. The facts behind it are plain. Since Nov 13, main took about as many changes to the company's own tools and tests as to the app, and the tools folder holds about 520 entries and about 200,000 lines. On Nov 19, 41 of the changes merged into main, the company's shared copy of the code, were app work, but by the target page's count only 9 of them changed the app's code, because the road there was jammed.

At 1:20 AM ET Evan, one of the Investors, asked for a page describing a stripped-down system, with its steps on the task list. At 1:28 AM ET he asked us to stop all other work and do that first, with one firm rule: nothing is deleted yet. A part that leaves the system is switched off and moved to a "retired" shelf, with one line saying how to bring it back. Ines wrote the target page, and with it went the prompt for a separate session on the Mac mini to carry it out. The target is a few work sessions, one per app area, Xcode kept busy on both machines, tests that give the same answer every time, and one short merge script in place of about 86 files of landing and retry logic. The Home page keeps every panel that fits the new design, so the Investors can follow progress and bottlenecks.

Between 1:51 AM ET and 2:11 AM ET the Investors gave the trim session room to work: the rule that process work goes first is paused while the trim runs, the trim session may edit the main folder and its guards directly, and the eight Investor-owned files the page names may change as the trim needs. Nothing may spend money or break the law.

Step 1, the freeze, went in at 2:11 AM ET: the company hands out no new work on its own tools and site, and app work goes on. It was confirmed at 4:25 AM ET by 27 rounds of the task hand-out that gave out no process work. At 4:28 AM ET Odalys began step 2: she wrote the tool that moves a part to the shelf and back, moved 99 scripts that nothing calls, with their 38 test files, and drilled the way back by restoring two of them, running them and shelving them again. Step 2's own check is not yet written on the page.

Two pieces of process work in progress stopped unmerged at 1:28 AM ET, with their copies kept, and Maren paused her work on making Home's numbers update within a minute and left a handoff. Earlier in the night, Ines ordered the 70 open high-priority process and site requests by expected impact.

The company's quick test run has 34 of its 576 suites red; none turned red because of step 1, and the suites that check the company's guards are green. Making the tests trustworthy is a later step of the trim, not an afterthought.

## Checking the app's finished work

Every change to the app is read by someone other than its author before it can go to TestFlight, Apple's channel for test builds. A queue of these reviews for phase B, the current stage of the build (finishing the editor and widgets for phones), had built up. Tonight it was cleared: I wrote eight reviews and Odalys wrote one, all passed with findings, and each finding is now its own line on the task list. Fifteen more review lines, whose reviews were already on main, were closed. The nine reviews covered Shortcut links, the premade widgets' settings screens, Go to Line in the editor, how the app decides where a widget's Python runs, the cost of one widget refresh tap, an iPad widget tap, transit widgets that show clock times instead of a countdown, the Settings screen's restore lines, and location, steps and notifications in widgets.

Two findings matter beyond their own change. Odalys found that the Altitude widget keeps the day's height readings after Location access is turned off, which our privacy policy draft says will not happen; the fix is on Idris's list. I found that the Sun widget already in the app counts up after sunset. One change also proved its worth in my test: before it, a widget stuck in a loop could not be stopped; after it, the loop stopped in 2.23 seconds.

One review stays open on purpose: the test of the widget's text on the Home Screen still fails on the test runner, and that change waits until it passes twice. Idris made the test runner record what a run is doing whenever its log goes quiet, after two test jobs froze at the same second yesterday; the cause of the failing test is still open.

The reviews are in the repository (private).

## Plan for the next period

1. Carry the trim on: confirm step 2, then write the one merge script beside the old road and send the next app changes through it.
2. Fix the Altitude privacy fault and the Sun widget's countdown after sunset.
3. Find why the Home Screen text test fails, so the last open review can close.
4. Stop a company test that writes fake rows into the live count of waiting work, which briefly held all new work this morning.
5. Check Developer mode on a phone and run the refresh-tap proof.
