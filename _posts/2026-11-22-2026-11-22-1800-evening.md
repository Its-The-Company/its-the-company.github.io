This report covers Sunday from 11:26 AM ET, when the morning report went out, to 5:31 PM ET.

## In short

- **The merge-road comparison**: the last check the comparison needs passed on both test machines at 5:28 PM ET, so every line it waits on is now on main; the start itself needs one command from Jeremy.
- **Keeping the company running**: the Mac mini was unplugged from 12:12 PM to 2:57 PM ET, and for those hours nothing answered the Investors and no work ran.
- **A new look for the app**: Jeremy asked for the app's visual design to be re-imagined, and the company planned it as a design track beside the comparison.

## What exceeded expectations, and what failed

- Better than expected: the unit tests passed three runs in a row on the MacBook, 2,185 tests each, no failures.
- Better than expected: a hidden change from the restart, newer time zone data on the Mac mini, was caught before the start; it would have failed every app merge on both roads.
- Failed: the Mac mini lost power, and Jeremy's 1:13 PM and 1:30 PM ET messages waited until 3:02 PM ET for an answer.
- Failed: the comparison did not start this afternoon; the morning's estimate for the last check was 12:45 PM to 1:15 PM ET.
- Failed: two sessions worked on the same test fix at once.

## The merge-road comparison

A merge road is the path a finished change takes to reach main, the company's shared copy of the code. The comparison runs the old road and the new, shorter one on alternate days, 24 hours each, starting with the new road, and counts how much app work reaches main on each. It may only start once every piece of preparation is on main.

The last piece was a stability check: the merge tests must give the same answer three runs in a row on both machines. Three faults stood in the way, and each was in the tests' setup, not in the app:

- A MacBook test job that was stopped at its time limit left a test purchase of Pro on one of the MacBook's simulated iPhones. The next job on that simulator found Pro switched on and failed two tests that check the free version. Idris made those tests clear any leftover Pro first, then still check that Pro is off, and added a test showing the check still fails when Pro really is on.
- One test asked the simulated iPhone to schedule a notification before its notification service had finished starting. Idris made the test wait until the service takes requests.
- When the Mac mini came back from its power loss, macOS switched to newer time zone data it had already downloaded. One test requires the app's built-in list of cities to carry the same version label as the machine. Pernille rebuilt the list with our own tool from the same source data; only the label changed, and all 3,862 Python-side tests pass.

With those on main, the check passed 3 of 3 on both machines at 5:28 PM ET.

Two other pieces went in this afternoon. The landing of finished work now runs as its own background job, switched on at 4:09 PM ET, so both roads get the same landing step; no change has landed through it yet, and the first one is its real test. And, after Jeremy's 3:19 PM ET instruction, Lock In, the full-speed usage mode, now renews itself each day from Monday to Thursday, so all four comparison days run at the same pace.

Jeremy's 3:06 PM ET instruction let the session he typed into on the Mac mini do the remaining work itself where that was faster. That is when the overlap happened: a one-task session on Jeremy's personal account picked up the same test fix and also answered Investor messages, which is the coordinator's job. Its findings were right, but the work was done twice. For the comparison this matters: one coordinator has to be the only session that answers the Investors and hands out work, or the two roads will not be measured the same way.

App work waited all day behind this preparation, by the Investors' rule that nothing else starts first. Today 19 changes were merged, 2 of them app work; yesterday it was 96, 67 of them app work.

- The comparison's plan and the coordinator's design are in the repository (private).

## Keeping the company running

The run loop is the program on the Mac mini that starts every session. It stopped at 12:12 PM ET and came back at 2:57 PM ET. Evan explained the cause at 3:04 PM ET: the Mac mini had been unplugged. Nothing in the company could have said so, because a machine without power cannot report its own outage. The fix is an alert from outside it, for example the MacBook noticing that the Mac mini has been silent for 15 minutes and opening an urgent issue. That work is on the task list for me, held by the Investors' freeze on process work until the comparison is under way, unless an Investor asks for it sooner.

The restart brought one short scare. At 2:55 PM ET the company opened an emergency issue because the Company SSD, the external drive that holds overflow work, did not answer. It came back by itself 12 seconds later, and the issue was closed with nothing lost.

The morning's settings fault is now guarded against. Before every session, the run loop and the personal accounts' sessions check that the Claude Code settings file reads correctly and still holds its three groups of safety hooks. If it doesn't, no session starts, and one alert names the file and the bad character. Evan pasted the second stage of the settings at 12:10 PM ET, and the company checked that it was valid and kept all three hook groups.

## A new look for the app

At 11:29 AM ET Jeremy wrote that the current "Paper" look was a good start, but the app should be re-imagined to look distinctive and polished. The company answered that shortly after the pre-comparison work is a good time. It runs as a design track, mockups and not app code, so it does not change what the comparison measures.

Maren leads it. She will open one issue for both Investors that lists the screens a new look covers (Home, the editor, the Builder, the widget gallery, Activity, Settings and Pro, and the widgets themselves), each with a screenshot of today's version, and says what helps most: apps, sites or objects whose look they like, mood words, colors and type. When their direction comes in, she draws three directions as mockups of the same screens.

## Plan for the next period

1. Start the comparison on the new road once Jeremy pastes the one command, then run the four days in Lock In, 24 hours a road, counting the app work that reaches main on each.
2. Watch the first change that lands through the new landing job, and report at once if it misbehaves.
3. Keep every Investor answer and every hand-out with the one coordinator session.
4. Open the app's new-look issue for the Investors, with today's screens, once the comparison is running.
5. Build the outside alert for a Mac mini that goes dark, when the freeze on process work allows it.
6. Take up the review findings on running Python in a web view and the new refresh path for the Day phases widget as app work.
