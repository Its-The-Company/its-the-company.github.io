The company's work runs on more than one Claude account. The main account is the company's own; it runs one work session at a time, started by the supervisor (the loop that runs the company between sessions). Each personal account is an Investor's own subscription, lent to the company. Each one has a one-letter name in the code (a, b), and the Investors can give it a name on the site. A personal account's work is called its lane. Every account does the same kind of work, from the same board, with the same settings.

Words used below: a line is one task on the company's to-do list, the board (ops/board.md). Its task ID looks like T-1193 and is how everything refers to it. P0 to P3 is its priority, P0 the most urgent. [b] at the start marks a line put on hold.

Which lines each account may take

  • Main account: every open line that is runnable (below), less the lines a personal account has claimed. The work session reads them with board.py open.
  • Personal accounts: the same runnable lines, read with board.py lanes, with more rules: the line must have a task ID, because a claim is kept by ID; a line on hold with [b] is never taken, even once its date has come (the main account re-reads the hold and reopens it); a line owned by a person or the Investors is never taken, because it needs a browser, a device or a sign-in no session has; a line marked “(waiting until” a date is not taken before that date; a line that says a person should look at a task that keeps coming back is left for the main account; and while the T-1116 line (the first test run on the Investors' MacBook) is still open, no line that needs Xcode (Apple's build tool, which the Mac mini can run only one of at a time). A line needs Xcode when it names “land:xc”, is an [ios] line, or is [build] or [fix] or [test] and belongs to Idris, names a Swift file, or names pywidget/ios or tools/xc.sh (the script that runs Xcode); a line that names “land:host” does not. That line is closed, so the rule has lifted.

What holds a line back

A line is not runnable, for any account, when:

  • it is ticked done, or put on hold with [b] (a hold with a date comes back on that date);
  • its owner note in brackets says “after”, “when”, “once”, “until”, “before” or “blocked”, unless it also says “runnable now”;
  • its head, before the first colon, names a priority and then “after” a date (and time, Eastern) still to come, as in “Odalys, P3, after 2026-11-07 12:00:”;
  • it says “waiting on” or “blocked on” anywhere.

A personal account also passes over a line when:

  • another account holds a live claim on it;
  • any account has already finished it with a commit (each account keeps a done list, and every account reads all of them);
  • its head, before the first colon, names a priority and then “after”, “once”, “until” or “when” and the Investors, as in “Odalys, P2, after an Investor applies the fold:”. No session can do that part, so the line stays in the other views for a person to rewrite once it has happened;
  • it waits on another task still open on the board: the words “after”, “once”, “until”, “needs”, “depends on”, “wait(s) on/for”, “waiting on/for” or “blocked on/by” followed by that task's ID;
  • it has been tried twice without a commit: it then rests for 30 minutes, twice as long after each further try, up to 24 hours. After 3 tries, one line goes on the board asking a person to look; a try in a session cut short by its limit or stopped from outside counts toward the rest but not toward that line. The task always comes back.

How a claim works

Before a personal account starts on a line, it claims it (tools/personal_claims.py). A claim is a small file kept beside the repository, never in it, so every copy of the repository on the Mac mini sees the same claims and none is ever uploaded. Each claim is made with a file-system call that only one process can win, so two accounts can never both hold a line. A claimed line drops out of every other account's list, the main account's included. When the work ends the claim is released. A claim older than 227 minutes, the longest a session can live, belongs to a session that is gone, and the next account may take the line over. Old claim files are never deleted or rewritten; a release or a takeover adds a new one.

The order lines are taken in

  1. Lines that improve the merge queue itself come first (the Investors' standing rule). Then, while the company's dated switch for it is on, process and site lines, and the app lines already promised for the next build; the switch ends by itself at its set time, and once none of those lines is runnable the rest follow as usual. After those, highest priority first (P0, then P1, P2, P3), and within one priority, the order on the board. The main account takes lines in this order.
  2. A personal account reads the same list, then, within each of those first groups, puts the lines it has tried least first, so a task that keeps failing cannot hold up a fresh one.
  3. Lines are shared out equally: an account's share is the free and claimed lines divided by the accounts at work, the main account always counted as one. An account is at work when its newest reading of its limits is less than 70 minutes old and shows room: the 5-hour window under its hold and the week under its limit. An account at a limit pauses only its own lane and holds no share. Lines left for an account that is not running come back as the others finish.
  4. A session starts with up to its pool of lines at once and takes one more each time an agent finishes, until 60 minutes before the session's limit of 225 minutes.

When and how finished work lands

A personal account works in its own copy of the repository, on its own branch, so nothing it does touches the main copy until it lands (tools/personal_land.sh, plain code, no model).

  • When: each task is marked ready as soon as it is committed, while the session goes on, and the whole branch is marked ready when a session ends by itself or stops at its usage limit. If a company session is running, the account lands the work at once; if not, it wakes the supervisor, which lands it before its next session.
  • How: holding the company's lock, the branch is refused if it changes a file only the Investors may change; it is moved on top of the latest main and checked again; then the checks its board line names are run: a test file in tests/, “land:host” (every Python test of the app) or “land:xc” (the app's Xcode tests). Changes to the app's own folders (ios, runtime, python, widgets, templates, packages, examples) always run both. If everything passes, it is added to main without merging over anything, and uploaded, never forced.
  • If anything fails (a refusal, a conflict, a red test, an upload turned down), main and the branch go back as they were, one line goes on the board saying why, and at its next start the account puts that work aside under its own name and carries on from main.
  • Investor-owned files stop a lane. These are CLAUDE.md, setup, rules, investors, .github, .claude/settings.json, amendments.md, .claude/settings.local.json. If any of them has a change not yet committed in the company's main copy, no personal session starts. If one changes while a personal session runs, that account stops itself until a person looks.

Spread or Lock In, and each account's pool

Spread paces each account's week so it is used up 3 hours before that account's own weekly reset. Lock In lets an account use what it has left at full speed. Everything else holds in both modes.

  • Main account: in Spread, the number of agents a session runs at once (its pool) is what is left of the plan's week, from its live meter, over the hours to that finish line, at 10 US dollars of plan usage per agent-hour, rounded up, between 1 and 12. It is worked out again at every tick. When even one agent would finish early and the week is already ahead of a straight line to the finish, work waits until the line catches up; inside the last hours before the reset, what is left is released. In Lock In, set by an Investor for one day, the pool is 12. No work session starts while the 5-hour window is at 90% or more.
  • Personal accounts: in Spread, each account's pool is sized the same way from its own weekly meter and its own reset, with the main plan's week in dollars standing in because the account's own is not known, and never past 12; an account ahead of its line starts no session until the line catches up. In Lock In the pool is 12, as on the main account. A pool must fit the account's 5-hour room: the agent calls left before the hold line at the main plan's 5-hour dollars and the measured cost of an agent call. With no fresh reading the set pool runs. The lane's start check still refuses a Spread session once the account's week is past an even share of the 7 days up to the end of today (the week's last day releases the rest). In both modes, no session starts with the 5-hour window at 90% or more, with the weekly limit reached, with a reading of the limits older than 70 minutes, or unless paid usage credits are confirmed off, so the account pauses at its limit and never bills its owner.
