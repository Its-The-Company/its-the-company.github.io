## How the replay works (what it keeps, what it asks again)

Each attempt keeps what the record says it was: its outcome, its check seconds, its branch's files, its lane. A policy
changes only these questions, each answered from the record:

- When it starts. One check slot, as observed (no two attempts overlapped, n=226; the lock was busy 76% of the window
  and 80% of all queue wait fell while it was busy). Ready: a first attempt not before its last commit (or ready line)
  and not before the end of the land's last idle gap of 2 min or more before its observed start (a land that idles has
  nothing ready); a retry at the next merge queue run when that is how the record's retry came (27), at once after a
  `waits` (21), else after the observed delay. A lane's next attempt waits for its previous one (one marker per lane).
  Ties go in the record's own order.
- Whether its rebase conflicts. p = 1 - (1 - q)^n, n = the branch's files that main changed since its base (the replay's
  own landings plus every other main commit at its real time); q = 0.195 by maximum likelihood (37 conflicts in 190
  attempts that reached the rebase; p0 = 0: no conflict had n = 0). An observed outcome is kept or flipped by coupling,
  one draw per attempt shared by every policy, so lower exposure can only remove conflicts and higher only add them. An
  observed conflict whose recorded files (the land's log and T-3082's merge-tree replay) are all files a policy takes
  out of conflict goes.
- Whether main is red under it. Each shared red's signature marks a spell, from the last host pass before its first red
  to the first host pass after its last (6 signatures, 9 spells). An attempt inside its spell that runs the failing
  suites is red; outside it, or not running them, it is not.
- How long its checks take: observed seconds, less the host suites a policy does not run, times 1 + slowdown x the
  other checks running.
- A failure a policy removes is skipped: the chain goes on to its next recorded attempt at once. A chain whose last
  recorded attempt is skipped has no recorded next outcome; it gets one tail attempt that lands with the record's pass
  rate (0.446 of checked attempts). The "tails red" column counts every tail red: that is the part of a prediction that
  rests on recorded outcomes only.

Policies: parallel checks (T-3079 / OPT-102: 4 slots; a landing that touches a branch's files breaks its proof and it
checks again; an xc wait holds a slot 120 s at most; slowdown 0.25 per other check, not measured, so the table varies
it); Bors batches of 4 (or 2) with bisection; a cap of 1 (or 2) unfinished tasks per lane (a new task starts only when
the lane's waiting one lands, and its time to merge counts from then); machine records out of git (T-3084, modelled
from its board line: ops/metrics/, ops/runs/, usage, dispatch, inbox state, meters, pool, switchback, lane outbox, the
merge queue's ledger and retry file; not its files); research/inbound/apple/index.tsv merged as union; host suites
chosen from the import graph (every .py under pywidget/ and tools/, plus the path words each suite's code names, since
host suites read Swift, docs and tools/ files by path; any other file in pywidget/ outside ios/ and docs/ runs all
suites); hold while main is red. Already in flight today, so modelled too: OPT-099 first try (host only for diffs that
change pywidget/ beyond docs; a red that main shares does not block, main's run paid once a spell) and OPT-101 spares
(no "worktree missing").

## Findings (n beside each; 40 draws per row)

1. Baseline fit. The replay of the policy as it was lands 60 tasks, exactly the record's 60, when its 4.4 tail attempts
   count red, and 62.1 (44.7 a day against 43.2) when they pass at the record's rate. Checks 151 against 153, slot hours
   24.8 against 25.9, conflicts 39 against 37, shared reds 36 against 41 (5 fall outside their spell when the replay
   moves an attempt). 196 of about 220 attempts start within 30 min of their recorded start (median 1.1 min early).
   Time to merge from ready, median 0.54 h against 0.52 h; p90 3.6 h against 5.3 h (the replay is short in the tail).
2. What is already shipped carries the gain. OPT-099 (first try) alone: 63.3 tasks a day, 55.5 with tails red, against
   44.7 and 43.2 (+42%, and +28% on recorded outcomes only); land rate 0.35 against 0.24. OPT-099 + OPT-101 + OPT-102
   (the land as it stands tonight): 72.5 a day (p10-p90 67.6-77.9), 55.5 tails red, time to merge median 0.10 h, p90
   2.9 h, land rate 0.38. Parallel checks alone: +2.0 a day, all of it tails; their gain is time (median 0.54 -> 0.21 h).
3. Nothing unshipped adds a gain that rests on recorded outcomes. On top of tonight's land, "tails red" stays 55.5 or
   55.4 for every candidate. Means: T-3084 78.0 (+5.5), cap 1 74.0 (+1.5, p10 below tonight's), cap 2 72.5 (+0.0),
   import graph 72.5 (+0.0; slot hours -3%), hold while red 72.5 (+0.0), union with T-3084 78.0 (+0.0 over T-3084),
   batches of 4 instead of parallel 57.9 (-20%).
4. T-3084's +5.5 is one branch. 16 of 40 draws land the 19-task set T-2548+T-2443+... whose only recorded conflict was
   ops/merge_queue_queued.txt (n=1); the next three chains it frees appear in 3, 3 and 2 of 40. If T-3084 leaves that
   ledger in git, its predicted gain is about zero. Its conflicts: 50.9 -> 47.1 a window.
5. The cap does not pay (plan step 5's condition). Alone, cap 1 loses 29% (31.8 against 44.7; 28.8 against 43.2 tails
   red): lanes whose waiting task never lands stop for the rest of the window. Cap 2 is +0.8 alone and +0.0 on tonight's
   land. Conflicts barely move under it (50.9 -> 50.3), because they come from a few shared files (T-3082), not from
   how many branches wait.
6. Batches lose because most checks fail. Of the checked attempts 2 in 3 did not land; a red batch of 4 is bisected into
   up to 5 runs. Batch of 4: 26.0 a day alone (-42%), time to merge median 7.0 h.
7. Union on index.tsv gives nothing. Its 10 conflicts are all one 10-task branch (T-2153+...) whose merge with main also
   conflicts in 21 to 24 code files (docs, Swift; T-3082's merge-tree replay): the land stopped at index.tsv first.
8. The import graph narrows host runs where first try does not (diffs inside pywidget/): 62 of 117 host-running
   attempts reach no host suite, 35 reach all, 20 in between. 3 own reds were in suites their branch's files cannot
   reach (branches that changed only ops/lane-outbox or tools/task_stages.py): not misses, reds the land blamed on the
   wrong branch. Its gain on top of OPT-099 is 0.5 slot hours in 33 h.
9. Cross-check of the predictions in flight (for T-3087): OPT-099 predicts a land rate of at least 50%; the replay gives
   0.35 for it alone and 0.38 with OPT-101 and OPT-102. OPT-101 predicts at least 40%; the replay gives 0.27 for spares
   alone. Both are likely to fall short on land rate while tasks a day rise.

## Recommendation

Ship nothing from this list ahead of reading OPT-099, OPT-101 and OPT-102 (their windows close by 2026-11-09). First
after them: T-3084, already being built and wanted whatever the replay says; it is the only candidate with a predicted
gain on top of tonight's land, and the only one that takes no check away (quality risk none). Its prediction is OPT-105
in log/optimizations.md, on its own metric (conflicts and main commits on machine records), since tasks a day and land
rate are being measured by the three in flight. Do not ship the cap (T-3086), batches, hold while red, union on
index.tsv, or import-graph selection now: the replay predicts no gain for any of them over tonight's land. The import
graph is the one to revisit if host checks become the queue again (it cuts host time where first try cannot).
Nothing was shipped in this task.

## What the replay cannot see

- The window is fixed. A policy cannot make new work arrive, and a chain whose recorded attempts all fail stays
  unlanded unless a skip gives it a tail. Gains past the window's 204 tasks are not counted.
- Readiness is a bound, not a record: 18 attempts have a ready line. With readiness at the last commit instead (the
  sensitivity table below) the baseline fits worse (47.5 a day against 43.2) and every ranking is the same.
- Parallel slowdown on the Mac mini is not measured (3 rows of ops/runs/land_prechecks.jsonl so far); the table varies
  it from 0 to 1.0. At 1.0 (cores give nothing) parallel checks lose 1.1 tasks a day against baseline.
- First try's named-check narrowing for tests/ suites is not modelled (the record keeps no per-suite seconds for them);
  its host narrowing and main's-red rule are.
- A shared red's spell is bounded by host passes, which a branch that runs only named checks does not give.
- The import graph is today's tree, not the window's; a test that reads a file by a name built at run time is missed.
- "Moved during checks" (14) is a rate per check second; a skipped one gives no recorded next outcome either.
