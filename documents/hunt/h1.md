Board line: ops/board.md "Idea hunt", H1. This file replaces my unfinished research/pass14.md and takes in its useful findings (the board names this file paid-unhappy.md; the coordinator asked for H1.md).

Listen to this: "Paid for app, dev now wants monthly sub" (Blink Shell, 1 star, 2024-02-17, https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1594898306?see-all=reviews). Here is the shape of it: in the top-grossing lists, the worst-rated earners mostly fail on **how they charge**, not on **what they do**. Out of ten, only two fail at a job that a better-built app could do, and in both lanes the shelf already holds rivals.

**Result: no lane at B+ this pass. Best: B- (circuit simulation on iPad), B- (a usable Anki-deck client). No hand-off.**

I only read; I posted nothing. Nothing I read gave us instructions. Excluded: kids (C7), medical (EMRA Antibiotic Guide), streaming content (AMC+, HIDIVE), and hardware or service apps (Drivewyze, EseeCloud, Sidus Link).

## Method
- `tools/grossing_pain.py chart` (new, committed with this file): the old iTunes RSS Top Grossing, US, iPhone and iPad, for the eight board genres (6007, 6002, 6017, 6006, 6026, 6008, 6010, 6016) plus four more. Every app looked up and kept if rated 4.35 or below. Pulled 2026-10-23.
- `grossing_pain.py low ID`: the 1-2 star reviews among up to 500 recent reviews (review RSS). I read these **by hand**. The counts below are my hand counts, not regex counts.
- Need counts only **paying users**: an app paid up front (iCircuit, AnkiMobile, FAR/AIM by ASA), or a reviewer who says they paid.
- Shelf check: `tools/appsearch.py` with category phrases. `tools/sizing.py` gives the lane size.
- Also in the tool, untested as a method: `shelf` (the best-rated established app per search phrase), `leaders` (the 1-2 star share for the top apps per phrase), and `wishes` (wish sentences in reviews of any star rating, for lane H3).

## Lane A: circuit simulation on iPad that does not lose your work (B-)
**What it is:** sketch a schematic with the Pencil, simulate it on the device (SPICE: DC, transient, AC; a scope and meters placed on wires), keep the work safe, and export a netlist or an image. For EE students, hobbyists and makers.
- **Need, paid users, hand-read:** iCircuit (paid up front), 44 of 111 low reviews read: crashes, freezes or lost work **22**; components missing or simulated wrongly (relays that conduct when open, an LM317 that fails, no LEDs with their own forward current) **11**; a paid feature moved into a subscription **3**. But **most of the 22 are from 2017-2018**. Since 2024 only about 10 iCircuit reviews say anything. Breadpad adds ~15 crash complaints and ~7 about the interface, but few of those reviewers say they paid. Electronics Stack Exchange: one 2014 answer uses iCircuit; "ltspice ipad" gives 0 questions. HN: Breadpad's own launch post (https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39256602).
- **Shelf:** EveryCircuit has not been updated on iOS since 2023 (3.0). VoltSim (2025-04, 4.7, 91 ratings) is a live rival. There are 10+ tiny 2025-26 entrants (Tersa 2026-09, WireGo, ElectroSim, Circuit Simulator Q). None has volume and a good rating.
- **Size (`tools/sizing.py`, from ratings, a range):** 16,500-49,500 lifetime downloads across five apps, **12-36 a day for the whole lane.**
- **Grades:** need B- (repeated and real, but thin and old), novelty B- (VoltSim and the small entrants), depth A (an on-device SPICE engine; ngspice is BSD-licensed, licence to be confirmed), money Day 30 C, Day 90 C+ (iCircuit has made money at $12.99 for 15 years on a small lane). **Overall B-.**

## Lane B: an Anki-deck client people can actually use (B-)
**What it is:** an iPhone and iPad app that opens .apkg decks straight from Files or a link, schedules them with FSRS, and is simple to learn. It sells once, up front. It does not use AnkiWeb sync (that protocol is not open to third parties; to be confirmed).
- **Need, paid users, hand-read:** AnkiMobile is $24.99 and ranks #46 in Education grossing, so every reviewer paid. Of 79 low reviews: hard to learn or use **32**; can't get decks onto the phone without the desktop app **12**; sync or lost decks and images **9**. This is the strongest paid need count in H1.
- **Why it stops at B-:** novelty C+. Renki's "Anki Flashcards" (2026-07) and nine FSRS apps released in 2026 are on the same idea (0-5 ratings each), as are Koki, Azri and Noji. Users of AnkiMobile are often tied to AnkiWeb sync with their desktop, and we could not offer that. Guideline 4.3 risk sits in a crowded flashcard shelf. Depth B.
- **Grades:** need A-, novelty C+, depth B, money Day 30 B- (paid users exist), Day 90 B. **Overall B-** (novelty holds it down).

## The finding across the ten (not a lane, but useful for any pitch)
Pricing changes drive these reviews more than missing features. Paid-then-subscription anger shows up in Navionics (~25 of 69 read), Blink (~25 of 44), Pyto (~14 of 44), Video Star (~13 of 25), and outside the charts in Notability (subscription and legacy users plus data loss after its own cloud, most of 44 read), Goodnotes 6, and **3D Scanner App** (18 of 24 read are about the 2026 paywall; reviewers say it was "sold to private equity"). For our $200 goal this backs **paid up front, every feature offline, no subscription** as a selling line, and it is free to say. But in every one of these shelves a well-rated rival already takes the switchers (Scaniverse 4.8 is free, Noteful 4.8, Termius 4.7), so the anger does not open a lane by itself.

## From pass14.md (folded in)
- Top Grossing with ratings is a quick signal, but a low rating often means a pricing change or an outage, not an unmet job. Read before grading.
- Letterboxd (37% of 200 recent reviews at 1-2 stars): 88 of 207 low reviews mention ads (regex, not hand-read). TV shows are the repeated ask in its 4-5 star reviews (5 of 22 wish sentences, hand-read). That goes to H3.
- From pass 13, still open: apps rank high in Top Paid on very small volume (Deck-Man #5 on 1 rating). A sizing partner should test this.

## Open
- No B+, so no hand-off line.
- Lane A could rise only with fresh paid demand (r/AskElectronics or EEVblog forum reads; our tools get 403 there). Check the ngspice licence and size before any spike.
- Lane B: check that .apkg import and FSRS are clean to use (Anki is AGPL; we would write our own reader of the documented SQLite format; FSRS is MIT, to be confirmed), and read Renki's reviews when it has any.
