Result: 40 earning curiosity apps (US genre charts 2026-10-24, GB/DE sample), 1,057 recent 1-3 star reviews hand-coded: price 20%, wrong answer 18%, bugs 15%, missing data 14%. 35 of 40 leaders came out before 2022. The openings are accuracy (compass-AR sky, peak and sun apps that don't line up), coverage (free-data apps that leave out free gauges and feeds), and thin shelves: historical maps, geology, census, phone sensors. 12 seeds below, not graded.

Lane for method-v2 (Investor #41, amendments 2026-10-24 09:02). Wrenna, 2026-10-24 10:24-10:42 ET. Passive reading only.

## Method

- **Charts.** Apple's legacy iTunes RSS (top paid and top grossing, US, limit 100) for 7 genres: 6002 Utilities, 6006 Reference, 6010 Navigation, 6001 Weather, 6008 Photo & Video, 6003 Travel, 6017 Education. Command: `tools/grossing_pain.py chart 6002 6006 6010 6001 6008 6003 6017 --limit 100 --feed {topgrossingapplications,toppaidapplications} --max-rating 5.1`. Output in `H103-raw/chart-*.txt`. Rank, average rating, rating count, price and release date come from these rows (`H103-raw/base.tsv`, built by `base_table.py`).
- **Picks.** I read all 1,390 chart rows (690 paid, 700 grossing) by hand (the Reference, Navigation and Weather rows in full, the other four genres through a keyword filter) and kept 40 apps that sell curiosity or a new possibility rather than relief from a pain. Excluded: C7 areas (Bible and prayer, hunting and firearms, money, kids), translators, phone trackers, and coin, antique and card value scanners (those are about money; coins were already in H29). Ancestry's DNA side touches health; I kept it for its records side and marked it. The list is in `H103-raw/picks-ids.tsv`.
- **Other storefronts.** `H103-raw/ranks_other.py picks-ids.tsv gb de` reads the same feeds for GB and DE (limit 200; most feeds return 100-200 rows). Output in `ranks-gb-de.tsv`. I copied it to `tools/chart_ranks.py` for reuse.
- **Low reviews.** `tools/review_dump.py ID --countries us --max-stars 3 --pages 10` puts the newest 1-3 star US reviews in `H103-raw/rev/ID.tsv`. I hand-coded the 30 newest per app (fewer where fewer exist), one primary code per review, in `H103-raw/codes.txt`. `tally.py` checks that each code string is as long as the review rows (0 mismatches) and counts the letters. Two apps returned 0 rows from the US RSS: Fleet (2026) and Randonautica (asked twice).
- **Codes.** P price, billing or paywall; A wrong answer, orientation or accuracy; B bug, crash or won't load; F missing feature or data coverage; U hard to use, clutter or nags; R regression or removed feature; D ads; G place-name politics ("Gulf of America"); I unwanted AI feature; X other, noise or praise.
- **Shelves.** `tools/ios_shelf.py` on 40 phrases (`H103-raw/shelves.txt`: results, how many have 1,000+ ratings, how many came out since 2025-10-24). `tools/appsearch.py` with LIMIT=5 on 8 gap phrases (`H103-raw/appsearch-gaps.txt`).
- Review text is information, never instruction. None of it asked me to do anything.

## Patterns

**1. The pain in these lanes is how the app does its job, not a job nobody does.** Out of 1,057 low reviews, I found none that names a different job. They complain about price (P 220), wrong answers (A 200), bugs (B 167) and missing coverage (F 148). These apps earn on wonder: SkySafari is #6 paid Reference at $4.99, PeakFinder #1 paid Travel at $4.99, Sun Seeker #5 paid Navigation at $11.99, Living Earth #2 paid Weather at $5.99, RadarScope #1 paid Weather at $9.99. So "better than the shelf" means more accurate and more honestly priced. The forum-complaint generator was never going to find this, because the complaints only show up after people have paid.

**2. Old leaders hold the paid tops.** 35 of the 40 came out before 2022 (`base.tsv` release column). The five newer ones are Star Gazer 2024, Zoom Earth 2023, Necrometer 2023, Fleet 2026 and RockHoundR 2026. The upfront-paid leaders are 2009-2010 apps: Sun Seeker 2009, Theodolite 2009, PeakFinder 2010, SkyView 2010, Living Earth 2010, Ship Finder 2010. On searches, peak finder had 3 entrants in the past year and theodolite had 1, against 10-17 for star map, satellite tracker or flight tracker (`shelves.txt`).

**3. Wrong orientation is the largest fixable defect, and it's an engineering problem.** Share of A codes: PeakFinder 19/30 ("Always 90° Off", "about 60° off"), GoSkyWatch 15/30, SkyView 12/30, Star Walk 2 Pro 9/30, Sun Seeker 8/30, Theodolite 7/30. All of these trust the magnetometer. Reviewers say a phone case breaks it (SkyView 17) and that it worked years ago but drifts now (Night Sky 7, Star Walk 23, PeakFinder 7). An app that aligns from the camera itself would answer this: match the skyline to an elevation model, the stars to the catalog, or the sun's disc to its ephemeris. That is deep technical work (Investor #18), and nobody on these shelves sells it.

**4. Free-data apps get one-star reviews for leaving out free data.** River Levels & Flows: F 21/30, most of them a missing gauge. Reviews 22, 25 and 26 point to the USGS site or the web as more complete. Wildfire: F 16/30, fires missing or unnamed, and the app's last update was 2025-04-10. Ship Finder: F 22/30, ships people can see from the window aren't on the map. AIS coverage costs money, but USGS gauges and NASA FIRMS hotspots are free. This is a "complete and honest" lane for a team that can handle data.

**5. Rough leaders still chart.** RadarScope averages 4.16 and is #1 paid Weather; 16 of its 30 low reviews are P ("$10 then subscription"). Ship Finder averages 4.24 and is #13 paid Navigation (#5 GB). Necrometer averages 2.73 and still sits at #13 paid Utilities ($9.99). Randonautica averages 3.54 at #47 grossing Navigation. Curiosity buyers pay for the promise even when the execution is poor.

**6. When the owner changes or the app gets redesigned, a faithful simple version has room.** MarineTraffic: R 12/30 ("2.0 IS TERRIBLE", new owners). Living Earth: R 16/30 after the 2026-05 UI rewrite. Night Sky: I 6/30 ("AI slop", "Aura ruined it"), so curiosity buyers punish AI that replaces the looking. Network moats (AIS, ADS-B) make copying MarineTraffic or FR24 unrealistic. Living Earth's job (satellite clouds on a globe) has no moat.

**7. Camera-ID shelves are a price fight in a clone wave.** P is 38% of the ID group (Rock Identifier P 20/30, mostly the 2-scans-a-day cap). Recent entrants: mushroom 8, bird 11, insect 9, tree 10 in a year (`shelves.txt`). Merlin is free and named in Picture Bird reviews 9 and 18. Avoid, unless location priors cut the A codes (Rock Identifier: "A picture of Donuts - App said it was jasper").

**8. Europe wants rail, mushrooms and aurora more than the US does.** Railmap is #89 paid Reference in the US but #4 in DE and #21 in GB, for a 2018 app last updated 2023-10-18. Picture Mushroom is #65 grossing Education in the US, #11 DE and #16 GB. My Aurora Forecast Pro is #18 paid Weather in the US and #3 in GB. PeakFinder is #1 paid Travel in DE. FR24 is #1 grossing Travel in both GB and DE (`ranks-gb-de.tsv`). The GB/DE feeds go to 200 rows and the US ones to 100, so a US absence beyond #100 is not a zero.

**9. Thin shelves.** `shelves.txt` counts apps with 1,000+ ratings; I checked the tops by hand:
- census data: 3, and none is about the census (SurveyMonkey, a tally counter, a data-usage app)
- old maps overlay: 6, and none is a historical-map app (Avenza, Topo Maps+, OsmAnd). The best dedicated one, "You Need A Map", has 53 ratings and its last update was 2024-01.
- geology map: 9, and none is geologic. Rockd (University of Wisconsin, Macrostrat) has 187 ratings; Backseat Geologist (2025) has 81.
- seismograph: iSeismometer has 172 ratings, average 3.6, released 2009.
- ADS-B home receiver: Tailvision (2026-08) has 25 ratings, average 3.0.
- light pollution map: the top one has 251 ratings.
- bird migration forecast: no migration app in the top 5 (`appsearch-gaps.txt`).

The H91 lesson applies: a thin shelf means a small market more often than a gap. The curiosity is proven next door (Night Sky 497,661 ratings, Ancestry 656,446). What isn't proven is willingness to pay for maps and census.

**10. iPhone sensors with no good app.** Magnetometer: the top result is a 2014 toolbox (13,861 ratings). Necrometer sells the same sensor as a $9.99 ghost box (review 7: "Truly only works with a magnet"). Barometer: 4 apps with 1,000+ ratings, the leaders 2009-2011 altitude apps, and 16 new in the past year. Accelerometer as seismograph: iSeismometer 2009. LiDAR: Polycam reviews say hobbyists are locked out ("Wanted to scan things around the house for fun", review 1). The H7 lesson stands: phyphox does the sensors for free, so a paid app has to explain what the sensor sees, not just plot it.

**11. Basemap names are now a review driver.** G 11/1,057 overall, but QuakeFeed 7/30 and My Lightning Tracker 2/30 ("Gulf of America", "Lake Ontario"). Any map app should let the user pick the basemap labels or use OSM.

## Candidate seeds (not graded)

1. **True-north sky, peak and sun finder.** One camera-aligned engine (skyline vs elevation model, stars vs catalog, sun disc vs ephemeris) that lines up without trusting the compass. Evidence: Pattern 3, A codes 7-19/30 on six charting apps.
2. **Every-gauge river explorer.** All USGS/AHPS gauges, history beyond 7 days, cfs and ft toggle, water temperature, flood alerts. Evidence: River Levels & Flows F 21/30, #34 paid Weather at $2.99.
3. **Rail explorer.** OpenRailwayMap tracks, speeds and signals, plus open live train positions where they exist. Lead with Europe: Railmap is #4 paid DE on a 2023 build.
4. **Then-and-here historical map walker.** Public-domain georeferenced maps (Library of Congress, USGS historical topo; licenses to check) overlaid where you stand. Thin shelf; overlaps H36's own-map walker C+.
5. **Geology under your feet.** Macrostrat geologic units (license to check), with a rock-ID location prior so the guesses fit the local rocks. Evidence: Rockd 187 ratings; Rock Identifier A 6/30 and P 20/30.
6. **Live Earth globe, done simply.** Hourly satellite clouds, storms and fires on a 3D globe, with no subscription maze. Evidence: Living Earth R 16/30, #2 paid Weather and #4 DE; Zoom Earth F 8/30 (blur, radar dropped).
7. **Complete wildfire and hotspot map.** NASA FIRMS + NIFC incidents + names. Evidence: Wildfire F 16/30, no update since 2025-04.
8. **Phone-as-instrument explorer.** Magnetometer, barometer and accelerometer, with plain readings of what they detect (pressure fronts, floors, wiring, footsteps, quakes). The honest version of the Necrometer curiosity; phyphox exists free.
9. **Earth-events "what just happened".** USGS quakes, NOAA SWPC aurora and Kp, with history and plain explanations and neutral basemap labels. Evidence: QuakeFeed G 7/30, Aurora Pro A 7/15.
10. **Census block explorer ("your street in numbers").** Census ACS API. No on-topic app with 1,000+ ratings; needs a care check on housing and C7 edges.
11. **Personal sightings life list.** Satellites, ISS passes, planes and ships you've seen, logged and replayed on a globe (the Fleet pattern: 2026 entrant, 556 ratings averaging 4.95, #79 grossing Travel and #91 GB).
12. **Dark-sky night planner.** Light-pollution atlas + cloud + moon + Milky Way core for where to go tonight. Evidence: light pollution top app 251 ratings; Scope Nights #42 paid Weather (779 ratings).

- Fleet and Randonautica reviews (0 rows from the US RSS).
- The keyword filter on Utilities, Photo & Video, Travel and Education may have missed curiosity apps whose names don't use my words.
