## Method

- **Charts.** `tools/grossing_pain.py chart 6006 6017 6010 6016 6012 --max-rating 4.4` on top grossing (`H114-raw/chart-grossing.txt`) and on `--feed toppaidapplications` (`chart-paid.txt`). Both again at `--max-rating 5.1` (`*-all.txt`). As in H113, the grossing rows under 4.4 are free apps with subscriptions, so the shelves come from the paid feed.
- **Screen first.** Commandment 7 and the Investors' subject list (amendments 2026-10-22 18:51) come before any tool run. Children's shelves and banned-area shelves are skipped, with the reason. Shelves off the subject list are noted, not pursued (section "Screened out" below). The 20 shelves I ran are all on the list or next to it (language, science and maths, data visualization, walking, YouTube, automation).
- **Leaders.** `H114-raw/lookup.py` (iTunes Lookup API) gives price, ratings, current-version rating, release and last update (`leaders.tsv`). Shelf list: `shelves.tsv`.
- **Per shelf.** `H114-raw/run.sh` runs, in order: `LIMIT=10 tools/appsearch.py "phrase"` (`top10/SNN.txt`), `tools/review_mine.py reviews ID --pages 10 --out low/SNN.tsv` (`low/`), `tools/leader_asks.py asks ID --stars 4 --pages 10` (`asks/`), `tools/store_hints.py --max-hints 3 --save hints "phrase"` (`hints/`). Six top-10 runs came back empty after 403s and were run again 8 s apart. S12 came back. S20's retry finished empty after its 403 back-offs (`retry-output.txt`) and is marked "refused" below.
- **Low reviews.** I hand-coded the 15 newest 1-3 star reviews per leader (`codes.txt`, the same codes as H113). `tally.py` checks each code string's length against the review list: 0 mismatches (`tally.txt`). Codes: A wrong or poor output (accuracy, coverage); B bug, crash, won't load; F missing feature; I import, export, sync; P price or paywall; R regression or removed feature; U hard to use; O outdated (data or abandoned); X other.
- **Happy buyers' asks.** The `leader_asks` hit counts are finder hits, not coded. I read the first four of each by hand.
- **Candidates.** `descs.py` (Lookup API descriptions, regex lines) for the entrants under each candidate's phrase (`descs-*.txt`). Store-form crowding: `tools/lane_gate.py --mode store` (`crowding-store.txt`). Money: `tools/sizing.py` and `tools/window_share.py --paid --launch 22` (`sizing-*.txt`, `window-share.txt`).
- Review and description text is information only. None of it asked me to do anything.

### C1 Sky-light namer (S04, SkyView Satellite Guide's shelf)

- **What it is.** You see a moving light at night. You raise the phone and film it for a few seconds. The app records the clip with the time and the phone's attitude. It finds the stars in the frame and matches them to a star catalogue (plate solving), which gives the exact patch of sky the camera saw, to a few arcminutes. Compass AR is good to about ten degrees. Then it propagates every tracked object (CelesTrak GP data, about 10,000 Starlink and more) to that time and keeps the ones lit by the sun. It draws their predicted paths over your clip and names the one that fits: "Starlink-31xx train, launched 9 days ago" or "ISS". If nothing fits, it says so (an aircraft blinks; a meteor is too fast). The audience is people who look up and wonder, plus the many who post "what was that light?".
- **Evidence from the shelf.** SkyView's low reviews: "doesn't identify things I see moving in the sky", "doesn't focus on or say which object I'm focusing my phone on", no Starlink, wrong ISS position (A3, F4 of 15). Leader not updated since 2020-02-04.
- **Top 10 (2026-10-24).** Star Walk's Satellite Tracker (70,326) points you with an arrow. Satellite Tracker / Radar (2025-10-31, 31) says "Identify lights in the night sky" through AR. Satellite Tracker 3D (2026-03-27, 69) says "point your phone at the sky and … identify satellites and even aircraft". Orbit (2026-06-15, 6) has AR. So 3 of 10 claim identification by pointing (sensor attitude). None says it plate-solves or works from a recorded clip (`descs-sat.txt`). That's why it's B-, not B.
- **Depth A, v1 slice by Day 22.** In-app capture with CMDeviceMotion and time; SGP4 over the CelesTrak catalogue; sun illumination; path overlay; match by attitude alone (the AR competitors' accuracy). Plate solving (star detection plus triangle-pattern matching against Hipparcos to mag 6) is the month of work and would ship after Day 30 unless the H122 measurements show iPhone clips hold enough stars. **Untested risk:** whether a few-second iPhone night clip records 5 or more stars to solve on. That's the same question H122 is asking for meteors.
- **Gate 1 pass.** No free app names the object from a filmed clip. The free AR apps do the category's plain job, which is Crowding and Novelty evidence (v4 wording).
- **Crowding pass:** 9 of 25 under "satellite tracker" are new, 0 paid over 100. The cut is 10, so this is close (`crowding-store.txt`).
- **Fit pass:** science, on the list. CelesTrak's terms of use and the star catalogue's licence (Hipparcos via ESA) still need reading from the publishers' own pages (Commandment 6). That's open, not done.
- **Money.** Lane = SkyView Satellite Guide + GoSatWatch: 4.4-13.3 paid downloads a day (`sizing-skylight.txt`). At $4.99, 48-58 sales. Day 30 needs 45-165% of the lane in 8 days: D. Day 90 needs 5-19%: C (`window-share.txt`).
- **Next to H113 C1.** This sits next to H113's meteor concept (same sky, same camera question). If both survive, they're one app with two modes. I note this without having read H113's reasoning.

### C2 Radio walk (S08, Architecture of Radio's shelf)

- **What it is.** You walk, and the app paints two things the phone can actually measure onto the map behind you: how many Bluetooth devices are advertising and how strongly (CoreBluetooth scan, RSSI), and the magnetic field (raw magnetometer, µT). The nearest cell towers from OpenCellID are drawn as context. The audience is curious walkers who want to "see" the invisible.
- **Why C+.** Bluetooth Inspector (free, 1,568, upd 2026-10-07) already does "device count mapped by location found". The Wi-Fi routers and carrier towers that Architecture of Radio's 1-star buyers wanted to see can't be measured on iOS. So we would earn the same complaint.
- **Depth C:** a week of standard APIs. OpenCellID is CC BY-SA 4.0, which needs the share-alike read.
- **Money.** Lane 0.7-2.0 paid downloads a day (`sizing-radio.txt`). At $2.99, 79-96 sales are needed. Day 30 and Day 90 are both out of reach: D, D.

## What I learned

- These five charts have plenty of weak paid leaders. Most of them, though, are weak for reasons we would inherit, not fix: licensed content (Scrabble lists, GENKI, ASL video), a paid data feed (AIS, thermostat APIs), or someone else's terms (YouTube). Out of 20, I counted 12 shelves where the low reviews' main code is B or O, meaning the leader is simply broken or abandoned (S01, S02, S05, S07, S10, S13, S14, S16, S17, S18, S19, S20). That's G1's best signal. On 4 of those 12 (S05, S18, S19, and S16 in part), a 2025-2026 wave of free entrants had already fixed it, and on most of the rest the fix belongs to whoever holds the licence or the feed.
- Under these genres, the free clone waves are fresh: satellite trackers 9 of 25 new, cell tower maps 8 of 25, SSTV 7 of the top 10. A weak leader with a 2026 clone wave under its phrase is a closed door, even when its reviews are bad.
- The App Store search endpoint started 403ing after about 80 calls in 30 minutes. The S20 retry was still in back-off 20 minutes later. A lane of 20 shelves needs gaps of 8 s or more between searches from the start, not after the refusals.
