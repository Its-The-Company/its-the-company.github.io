## 1. Method

- Licence: fetched each publisher's own licence or terms page by curl with the company's User-Agent, saved the HTML, and quoted the sentence. Where an agency's site blocks non-browser clients (FCC, USDA, fcc.gov and usda.gov returned Access Denied; LoC HTML pages and oeis.org and si.edu present a challenge page), I did not bypass the check (Commandment 11); I cite a sibling federal statement of the statute (NPS: "Copyright law does not protect 'any work of the U.S. Government'... 17 U.S.C. §§ 101, 105"; USPTO: "Pursuant to federal law, most government-produced materials... are in the public domain") or a machine endpoint the publisher runs (LoC's ?fo=json, GBIF's API, OEIS's own GitHub mirror). Rows with only the statute citation are marked "statute" in the Licence column and are not counted toward the 25.
- Share-alike read (ODbL, CC BY-SA), written out once here and referenced in the rows: a database we derive from the data (a filtered, re-indexed copy inside the app bundle or on our server) must be offered under the same licence on request; a "produced work" (a rendered map, a screen, a chart) needs attribution to the source and the licence, not release of our code or our other data. For OSM, the ODbL names this split (database vs produced work); for CC BY-SA, adapted material (our edited copy of the text or table) carries the licence, and the app's code and unrelated content do not. Either way the app can be sold. Risk: any proprietary value we add to the derived database itself (our own corrections, our own fields merged in) becomes shareable; so keep our additions in a separate table joined at run time.
- Size and cadence: from the publisher's page or directory listing, dated; "not stated" where the page carries no number.
- Curiosity count: English Wikipedia monthly pageviews (all-access, user agents only), mean of the 12 months 2025-10 to 2026-09, from `https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/metrics/pageviews/per-article/en.wikipedia/all-access/user/<title>/monthly/2025100100/2026093000`. The number measures interest in the subject, not in the dataset; where the two differ I give both.
- Shelf: `tools/ios_shelf.py` on the buyer's phrase (first 50 results; 1,000+ rating count; entrants in the last 12 months, dated). The iTunes search API answered 403 to roughly every second call during this lane; I retried with 25 s waits and 4 s between phrases. Phrases still unread are marked.
- Commandment 7: no medical, financial, political or adult sets. Weather, tides, quakes and storms are hazards, not health advice; the app would show records, never guidance. Commandment 9: every screen is drawn by code from the data; scanned maps, newspaper page images and museum photographs are human-made images and would need Investor approval before any of them appears, so each concept below has a code-drawn v1 that does not depend on them.
