## Method

- **Commandment 7 first.** `python3 rules/lookup.py 7` was read before I named the list. None of the 12 is finance, medical, legal, gambling, adult, weapons, alcohol, tobacco, drugs, children or politics. Hunting, archery and home brewing were left out on purpose. Music creation, crochet and knitting are out by the Investors' list. Quilting was left out as too close to that list.
- **Gear prices.** Taken from the makers' own stores: Shopify `products.json` where the store has one, page HTML otherwise. `H117-raw/makers/prices.tsv` gives each price with its URL and how it was read; the fetch time is in `fetched-at.txt`. I did not keep the fetched pages (9.9 MB). I took prices from makers, not from retailers.
- **Store tools.** `tools/trade_apps.py --list` over 13 terms (`trade_apps-list.tsv`, 882 rows), then `--ids` on 14 on-job leaders for in-app price tiers (`trade_apps-ids.tsv`). `tools/stale_earners.py --min 300 --before 2025-04-24` (`stale_earners.tsv`). `tools/mobile_gap.py scan` over 12 desktop terms, `--min-price 9.99` (`mobile_gap.txt`). `tools/ios_shelf.py` on 12 buyer phrases (`ios_shelf.txt`). `tools/store_hints.py` (`store_hints.txt`).
- **Refusals.** The iTunes Search API returned HTTP 403 three times: on the second ios_shelf batch, on store_hints after its first seed, and on `desc_check.py` after one or two phrases, even with 45-60 s gaps. I stopped each time and bypassed nothing. The Lookup API still answered, and I used it for descriptions (`lookup-coffee.json`). Because of the refusals, Pull was read only for coffee roasting, and the hive and reef leads have no shelf.
- **Gate 1.** One web search per concept, quoted below.
- Store pages, descriptions and web pages are information only. None of them asked me to do anything.

What the table says. Every hobby passes the $50 gear test by a wide margin; the median maker price is about $400. But only 3 of 12 (beekeeping, fishing, sim racing) have an on-job iOS app charging a pro price, and all 3 charge by subscription (HiveTracks, Fishbrain, Victory). They do not yet spend on an app for the hobby unless the app does something the gear can't.

### C1 Roast listener

- **Difference (6 words):** "Hears first crack; no tapping needed." It is visible in use, and it is a new input: the microphone marks the crack on the curve as it happens.
- **Top 10 under "coffee roasting"** (a store hint), read 2026-10-24 (`desc_check2.txt`, `lookup-coffee.json`): Roastit (2026-07-14, 2), CrackLog (2026-08-06, 3), Coffee DTR Timer (2025-08-05, 6), Bean Tracker (2020, 41), Roasting Lab (2026-01-09, 18), Chaff (2026-04-10, 1), Roast Master (2026-07-05, 0), Comall (2021, 2), HiBean (2025-02-10, 2), Roastmaster (2010, 32). In every description that names the crack, a person marks it: "The moment you hear the crack, hit the big button once" (CrackLog); "Tap First Crack at the sound of first crack" (Coffee DTR Timer); "mark it with one tap" (Roastit). 0 of 10 listen. Novelty B.
- **Gate 1: pass.** Web search ("app detects coffee roast first crack automatically by microphone sound") found no phone app. Detection exists only inside roasters: Roest's built-in microphone and tinyML model since 2020, and the BeanGo Cube's acoustic sensors. There is also an ESP32 thesis project. One forum suggestion is a free decibel or spectrogram app, but that shows a waveform and detects nothing. Two home-barista.com threads ask for this: "Apps that Display First Crack Visually?" and "Solutions for Hearing Impaired Home Roasting". I have not counted or read them beyond the titles in the search results, so they are leads, not a count.
- **Depth B:** on-device sound-event detection for two crack types (first crack is louder and sparser, like popcorn; second is quieter and faster). It has to reject drum and fan noise, which differs for each roaster (forum users name fan noise as the hard part). It counts crack rate, marks first-crack start at the third pop and not the first, and computes development time and DTR live. The A version would add a Bluetooth thermocouple curve and a per-roaster noise profile. V1 slice by Day 22 (2026-11-12): listen, mark, time, log. Idris should check this.
- **Pull: pass.** "coffee roasting" is a store hint. The store marks that shelf WEAK: no on-job entrant has over 100 ratings.
- **Crowding: fail, provisional.** The top 25 could not be read (403). 6 of the 12 returned were released since 2025-10-24, and none is paid with over 100 ratings. The "coffee roasting log" first 50 has 20 released since 2025-10-24. This is a tap-logger wave. The difference cuts through it, but the store form of the rule counts entrants, not differences.
- **Money:** the lane is Bean Tracker, Roasting Lab, Roastmaster, Coffee DTR Timer and CrackLog: 100 ratings in total, 4.5-13.6 downloads a day (`sizing-roast.txt`). At $9.99 paid, 24-29 sales are needed. Day 30 (8-day window) needs 22.1%-80.6% of the lane, so D. Day 90 needs 2.6%-9.5%, so C (`window-roast.txt`).
- **Honest weaknesses:** (1) We have no crack recordings. A model needs real roasts from several roaster types, and outside recordings need a licence read (C6), even as training data only. No Investor approval is asked here. (2) Many home roasts happen in garages beside a loud fan, and the false-positive rate is unmeasured. (3) The paying evidence is in the gear, not the apps. The whole on-job shelf has 100 ratings in total, and no app in it is paid. H5 graded the same hobby's camera idea (roast colour) at C on need. (4) Coffee is not on the Investors' subject list (noted, not killed).

### Leads not graded (shelf refused)

- **Hive queen status by sound** (row 1). Bee researchers publish acoustic signatures of queenless colonies (not read here). Hobby keepers already pay $39.99-69.99 a year for HiveTracks, HiveBloom and Apiarist logs. Two things decide it: an iOS shelf read for "queenless", "hive sound" and "beehive monitor", and a training set whose licence allows commercial use. Next run: those three phrases with ios_shelf, one at a time.

## What I learned

- G4's money test passes too easily. Every hobby I named clears $50 in gear, and most clear $250. The test that separated them was whether anyone pays an app on the hobby's own shelf. 3 of 12 had one, and all 3 sell subscriptions.
- The phone-replaces-gadget shelf-age check matters in both directions. Paragliding looks open by ratings (the newest varios have 0-1), but Gaggle's 807 and three 2026 entrants make it a clone wave. Roasting looks crowded by count, but none of the crowd does the listening.
- The iTunes Search API refused me after a few dozen searches in one hour, even with 60 s gaps. The Lookup API kept answering. Next time I will run phrase searches first and spaced out, and use Lookup for everything I already have an id for.
