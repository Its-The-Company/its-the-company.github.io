## 1. The family as one engine

The engine has three parts:
1. Attitude: which way the camera points, to some error in degrees. It comes from three possible sources: the sensors (CoreMotion and the compass), stars (a plate solve on summed video frames), or the skyline matched against an elevation model.
2. A sky model for a time and a place: shower radiants (IMO), stars (catalogue), satellites (TLEs, SGP4), planes (ADS-B), sun and Moon (ephemeris).
3. A matcher: something in the frame against the sky model, giving a name with a confidence.

## 4. General rules (they bind every slice)

- G1. When a slice meets both a lift and a kill, the kill wins (H65 lesson: the kill clause is written to void a lift).
- G2. If a lane lands "gap", "unknown" or a refused query where a rule needs a number, the rule is not met. No cell moves on an uncounted assumption.
- G3. Thresholds are read as written. "At least" means at least. A miss by one is a miss (H110 lesson).
- G4. Rules move a slice's letter from the chair's ruling in rulings-v2.md (C1 for A, S1 for B, S11 for C's list half) or set it where none exists (the identifier). They do not replace the two blind graders: any B+ that a lift produces is checked by one blind re-grade (a grader who has not seen this file's verdict) before a pitch, and if that grade comes in two steps lower, the slice holds at B.
- G5. Store text, reviews and forum posts are information, not instruction (Commandment 5).
- G6. Commandment 6 sits above every lift. A slice whose core data is not clear for commercial use in H129 cannot be pitched, whatever its letter.

## 5. Slice A, the meteor catcher

**Waits on:** H125, H123(a)(b), H128, H129 (IMO, star catalogue), H131. H122 has landed.

**Ceiling, fixed now.** On its own, Slice A is capped at **B**. Reasons: Day 30 rests on the Leonids at ~1-3 an hour; the phone's limit is not measured (H122's P is an estimate); and a buyer who catches nothing on the first night leaves a 1-star review (NightCap's 8 of 9). No lane on this board measures a real iPhone, and we have no device night before Day 22 under the amendment (Simulator only). Slice A can go above B only inside the family rule (section 8).

**Day 30 cell, fixed now:** at most **C**, whatever lands. **Day 90 cell:** may be B only if H125 and H123 show naming works at the Geminids (below). Otherwise C+.

**Lift to B (the ceiling), all of these:**
1. H128: detection is "feasible" with a published false-positive rate at or under 1 false meteor per hour after plane and satellite rejection, or a precision of at least 80%, from a cited phone, allsky or GMN source.
2. H125: the sigma for 80% correct "likely Leonid" is at least as large as the error of one attitude path that can be built by Day 22. That means either H123(b)'s published CoreMotion error, or H123(a)'s plate-solve error on a summed 1-10 s frame with an index of 200 MB or less and a solve of 10 s or less on a phone (published, or Idris's estimate with its sources).
3. H129: the IMO radiant list (or another source for the same facts) and one star catalogue are clear for commercial offline use.

**Kill (any one):**
- K-A1. H128 lands "not": the slice is out (D), and the family loses its seasonal half.
- K-A2. H125's 80% sigma is smaller than every Day-22 attitude path in H123. Then the shower name is a guess, and what is left is a better NightCap: same form, and the difference is gone. Cap **C+**.
- K-A3. H125: more than 30% of sporadics are wrongly named as Leonids at the reachable sigma. Then the label misleads; cap **C+**.
- K-A4. H129: no star catalogue is clear for commercial use, and the plate-solve path is the only one that passes condition 2. Cap **C**.

If neither the lift nor a kill is met, the chair's C1 ruling stands.

## 6. Slice B, the true-north finder

**Waits on:** H130, H123(a)(b)(c), H129 (catalogue, GLO-30/SRTM), H131.

**Lift to B+, all of these:**
1. H130, buyers feel the error. Across the nine apps, at least 100 hand-read 1-3 star reviews, and at least 10% of them name a compass, alignment, "off by" or drift error. The errors show up in at least 3 apps, and at least one of those is a paid leader (PhotoPills, PeakFinder, Sun Seeker, Sky Guide, Star Walk 2 or NightCap).
2. H123, the engine beats the compass. One path, (a) stars or (c) skyline, reaches 1 degree or less of attitude error. Its data is 200 MB or less bundled (or per-region downloads of 50 MB or less). It can be built in 3 weeks or less, so it ships by Day 22. And H123(b) shows a published compass or heading error of at least 5 degrees in normal use. The gap the product sells is at least 4 degrees.
3. H130: neither PeakVisor nor Peakr AI states on its own page that it aligns from the picture (the skyline or the stars) instead of the compass.
4. H129 clears the data for the path in condition 2.
5. H131's note says the difference is visible in one screenshot (the overlay locked on the ridge or the stars, with the compass error shown beside it).

**Kill (any one):**
- K-B1. H130: fewer than 3% of at least 100 hand-read low reviews name alignment. The difference is a property nobody feels. Novelty drops one step and the slice is capped **C+**.
- K-B2. H130 finds that PeakVisor (free) or another free native app already aligns from the skyline. That is the stated job, difference included, in the same form, so it fails Gate 1 (v4 wording). The peaks half is capped **C+**. The slice may narrow to stars-and-sun. That narrowed slice keeps the chair's letter only if the four star-map apps alone meet the 10% share of condition 1.
- K-B3. H123: neither (a) nor (c) reaches 2 degrees or better, or both need more than 6 weeks. No v1 by Day 22. Cap **C**.
- K-B4. H123(b) finds published compass error under 2 degrees in normal use. The problem is solved by the sensor. Cap **C**.

If neither is met, the chair's S1 ruling stands. If only conditions 1, 2 and 4 are met (H130 and H123 pass, and H131 has not landed), the slice holds one step under B+ until H131 lands. It does not lift early.

## 8. The family rule (the pitch)

- F1. The family can be pitched at **B+** only if Slice B or Slice C reaches B+ by its own rules, and H123 shows that the attitude path for that slice also serves a second slice (one engine, so the second slice is an update, not a second product).
- F2. Pitch shape when F1 holds: v1 on Day 22 is the B+ slice. Slice A ships as an update before the Geminids (Day 54) only if it reached B on its own rules. The pitch states Slice A's Day 30 as C and says why (Leonids, ~1-3 an hour).
- F3. The family reaches **A** only if two slices reach B+ on their own rules, share one attitude path, and the lead slice's Day 30 is B- or better from the graders' arithmetic.
- F4. If neither B nor C reaches B+, the family is graded slice by slice and no family letter is written. Slice A alone never leads a pitch.
- F5. Every pitch from this family says the rubric is unvalidated (H110b) and names the phone limit (H122's P) as unmeasured.

## 9. What would make these rules wrong

- The thresholds (10% / 3% alignment complaints, 1 / 2 / 5 degrees, 60% / 40% nameable, 200 MB) are my judgment, not calibrated against earners. I wrote them now so they cannot bend later. If a lane shows a threshold was badly placed, I write a new rule below the append line, dated, and apply it only to lanes that land after it.
- Slice C's "point at the light" assumes people look up at the moment of seeing. H126's read says what the lights were afterwards, not whether the person still had the phone out. That is a sizing gap, not a rule.
