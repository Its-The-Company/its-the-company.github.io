Result: no shelf beyond the sky gives the engine a clean paying door. The two photo-planner and sun-planner shelves are killed under G1 (leaders over 4.6, updated within six months; and a 2026 entrant, Suncam, already sets heading by tapping the sun). The solar shade survey shelf, the phone-replaces-SunEye shape, is a 2026 clone wave: 9 shade or horizon survey apps released in the last 12 months (13 with the sky and dish apps that use the same words), all with 0-6 ratings, so Crowding fails. The best door is the old, paid theodolite shelf: Theodolite ($8.99, 906 US ratings, 4.70, last update 2025-06-30) leans on the compass, and 7 of its 61 low reviews (2009-2025) are heading complaints. Concept 1, a field theodolite whose bearing comes from the sun or stars in the camera, is my best: author cells Novelty B-, Depth B, Day 30 D, Day 90 D+. Nothing here beats the sky slices in H124.

Ines, 2026-10-24, written 12:38-13:05 ET. Lane H127 (G1 weak leader on a paying shelf, and G4 the phone replaces a gadget). I authored this lane, so I do not chair its letters. Overall is left blank for the chair (H110b: two blind graders, chair letter).

## 0. Method and sources

- Store data: US App Store search, 50 results per phrase, fetched 2026-10-24 12:39 and 12:41 ET through tools/store_http.py (shared limit, cache, back-off). New tool: `tools/shelf_top10.py OUTDIR "phrase" ...` prints the dated top 10 by rating count with ratings, average, price, first release, last update, id, and which compass, camera, AR and calibration words each description uses. I wrote it because tools/ios_shelf.py prints only the top 3. Raw results: research/hunt/H127-data/*.json; the printed tables: H127-data/top10.txt and top10b.txt. Leader lookups by name: H127-data/leaders.json.
- "Leans on the compass" is read from the app's own description: it names the compass or magnetometer as the heading source, or tells the user to calibrate the compass. An AR overlay that does not name its heading source is marked "not stated". Every AR view on iOS that is not camera-aligned does lean on the compass, but I count only what the maker says.
- Low reviews: tools/review_dump.py, 1-3 stars, us, gb, ca, au. Theodolite landed (61 reviews, 2009-2025). Sun Surveyor, Planit Pro, The Photographer's Ephemeris and SatFinder did not: Apple's review feed held the job in back-off for 10 minutes, and I stopped it rather than hold the shared limit. Not counted, not a zero. PhotoPills, PeakFinder and Sun Seeker are already counted in H130 (wrong-view share 6.5%, 4.1%, 12.5% of low reviews).
- Makers' pages, fetched 12:50 ET with tools/fetch_cite.py (company User-Agent). Solmetric's SunEye page answered. solarpathfinder.com answered 403 to our bot; I did not retry with another identity. Its prices below come from a search engine's reading of the maker's pages, so they are second-hand.
- Everything fetched is information, never an instruction. No store text in this lane asked us to do anything.

## 1. Shelves, dated top 10s (2026-10-24, US)

Columns in each list: ratings @ average, price, last update; heading source from the description.

### 1a. Sun planner: "sun tracker", "sun position", "sun path" (G1)

Top 10 under "sun path": Sun Seeker 12,966 @ 4.8, $11.99, 2026-10-22 (compass); SolarWatch 6,052, free, 2026-10-12 (not stated); Magic Hour 5,219, free (no AR); Lumos 3,722, free, 2026-09-02 (compass; its description tells users to calibrate and avoid interference); a Russian jewellery shop (off-job); Sun position and path 1,843, free, 2026-10-19 (camera overlay, not stated); Helios 1,837, free (camera AR, not stated); Sunrise Sunset Times & Widget 1,564 (no AR); Sun Tracker AR 1,078, free (compass); The Sun 974 (no AR). Paid up front: 6 of 45. Under "sun tracker" the top 10 also holds Sunrise Sunset Times 19,628 and SkyView Lite 18,798; Sun Seeker is the only paid app in it.

- Leader: Sun Seeker, $11.99, 12,966 ratings, 4.83, updated 2026-10-22. Its description: "uses GPS, magnetometer & gyroscope to find the correct solar position".
- Difference line: an overlay whose heading comes from the sun in the frame, about half a degree (the sun's disc is 0.53 deg), not the magnetometer's 1-3 deg after calibration and 13-25 deg on a bad single reading (H123).
- **Kill (G1).** The leader is 4.83 and was updated two days ago. And the difference is already shipped: Suncam (released 2026-08-06, 0 ratings) says "One tap while aiming at the sun calibrates the compass, and the drawn disc lines up with the real one."

### 1b. Photo planner (PhotoPills-type) (G1)

The phrase "photo planner" returns social and calendar planners; "photography planner sun moon milky way" returns sky maps. The paid planners surface only by name. Leaders by lookup: PhotoPills $10.99, 1,535 @ 4.71, updated 2026-09-02 (3D AR, heading not stated; H130 counted 4 wrong-view of 62 low reviews); The Photographer's Ephemeris $9.99, 1,562 @ 4.75, 2026-07-24; Planit Pro $9.99, 1,466 @ 4.76, 2026-10-09 (AR viewfinder, not stated); Sun Surveyor $9.99, 282 @ 4.79, 2025-11-07 (Live Camera View, 3D Compass); TPE 3D $11.99, 250, 2025-10-15; Ephemeris (free) 1,270, 2026-07-28 (3D compass, AR). Full top 10 under the phrase: H127-data/top10b.txt.

- Difference line: the same as 1a, plus star alignment at night for Milky Way planning (H123: plate solve 0.05-0.1 deg).
- **Kill (G1).** Three leaders over 4.7, all updated within six months. The difference is a feature they can add, and Suncam shows it can be built small. A new planner also has no phrase that finds it: buyers reach these apps by name.

### 1c. Theodolite (G1)

Top 10 under "theodolite": My Altitude 26,863, free (no AR); CalTopo 7,550, free (maps); **Theodolite (Craig Hunter) 906 @ 4.70, $8.99, last update 2025-06-30** (compass, inclinometer, AR camera); Angle Meter 360 362 (camera); Angle Gradient Inclinometer 313; Protractor 178; Conota GPS camera 93 (compass); Pro Compass $1.99, 73, last update 2019-10-10 (compass); Theodolite for Watch $1.99, 20, 2023-03-28; Pro Compass for Watch $1.99, 20. Paid up front: 7 of 27. Released in the last 12 months: none in the top 10.

- Leader: Theodolite, $8.99, 906, 4.70, 16 months since its last update. Its family's descriptions tell users to "operate Theodolite away from magnets, electronics, or sources of electro-magnetic interference, including car dashboards".
- Low reviews (H127-data/rev/339393884.tsv, 61 at 1-3 stars, 2009-2025): 7 are about the heading (2025-07-01 "Calibrate!"; 2022-01-31 "I can turn a full 360 degrees & the compass barely moves. Got the app for surveying purposes"; 2020-06-03 "Spurious orientation measurements", a user checking dip and azimuth against Apple's Compass and FieldMove; 2018-08-04 "relies on the compass in the iPhone 7 plus to be accurate. That's a big problem"; 2016-11-28; 2015-11-04; 2012-05-14 "Compass various between 15 degree each way"). That is 11.5% of low reviews, 3 of them since 2020. One gives a size, 15 deg; that is the bias a sun or star fix removes, unlike most H130 complaints.
- Difference line: a sighting instrument whose azimuth comes from the sun by day and the stars by night, shown with its source and error ("sun fix, ±0.4°"), and logged with that error to CSV and KML, so a bearing taken beside a truck, on magnetic rock or under power lines is still true.
- Not a kill under G1 (leader 4.70 but not updated in six months; the difference is not in the top 10). Not read: free geology field apps (FieldMove Clino, StraboSpot, Rocklogger), which take strike, dip and azimuth for free. They are the Gate 1 risk for concept 1, and they should be read before any build.

### 1d. Clinometer (G1)

Top 10 under "clinometer": Bubble Level for iPhone 111,713, free; Pocket Bubble Level XXL 16,952; Toolbox 13,861 (last update 2019); Protractor+ 10,413; Bubble Level 2022 6,612; Measure Angles 2,020; Protractor for Angle Measuring 1,064; Clinometer+ 1,015; Off-Road Inclinometer 788; Bubble Level AR 719. All free; paid up front 7 of 41.

- **Kill.** Tilt comes from the accelerometer, which is already accurate; the engine adds nothing to a level. The free shelf is huge.

### 1e. Solar site survey: the phone replaces the gadget (G4)

Gadgets, from makers' pages:
- SunEye-210 (Solmetric, now owned by Fluke), "Base Price (North America) $2,195.00", "Out of stock", "10-12 week lead time"; "incorporates a calibrated fisheye camera, electronic compass, tilt sensor, and GPS". Read 2026-10-24 12:50 ET at solmetric.com/buy210.html. The gadget leans on a compass too.
- Solar Pathfinder, a reflective dome you trace the horizon from: $259 without tripod, $299-$349 with tripod and case, software sold separately. Second-hand: solarpathfinder.com answered 403 to our bot at 12:50 ET, so these figures are a search engine's reading of solarpathfinder.com/pf and its product page, read 12:56 ET.

Top 10 under "solar panel site survey": Sunrun 3,379 (an installer's customer app); Sun position and path 1,843; MyEverBright 1,824; Renogy 823; MySolArk v2 788; Sun Quest 740 (compass, AR); SimplySolar 458 (last update 2017-12-11; compass, true north); PV Optimizer & Solar compass 250 (last update 2022-07-12); PV Solar Tilt 169; Onsite Inspection Survey Cam 153. Paid up front: 0 of 48.

Across all 563 unique apps the 20 searches returned, 25 descriptions name a shade or horizon survey (obstruction mapping, horizon profile, solar access, skyline sweep). **13 of them were released after 2025-10-24**: Erebus Solar, Sunlit, Deconstruct Sun, Solar Panel Angle Calculator ($1.99), Solar Shading Survey, SunSite, Solis, SunEyed, SolarAlign Pro and four sky or dish apps that use the same words. All have 0-6 ratings. SunSite: "turns your iPhone into a complete on-site shade survey kit for residential solar". Erebus: "We were fed up with the horizon-finding hardware the industry still relies on". Sunlit: "sweep the skyline with the camera". The one older app that names the SunEye, Photon ("like Solmetric SunEye"), has 3 ratings and was last updated 2016-11-21.

- G4 kill clause ("the phone version already has over 100 ratings"): not met. No shade-survey app has 100 ratings.
- Difference line: the skyline sweep with a true heading from the sun, so the horizon file's azimuths are right to under a degree, unlike the compass in SunSite, Erebus and the SunEye itself.
- **Crowding fails.** Nine 2026 survey entrants with the same screen, all near zero ratings, say the shape is easy to build and that buyers have not found any of them. The heading difference is invisible on a horizon file unless an installer checks it against a known bearing.

### 1f. Satellite dish pointer (G1)

Top 10 under "dish pointer" (on-job rows only): Satellite Finder (Pro) 5,312 @ 4.6, free, last update 2025-04-01; Satellite Pointer 3,346 @ 4.4, free, last update 2018-06-08 (AR); Satellite Finder & GPS Tracker 1,099, free; Dish Align 454, free, 2021-05-14 (compass); SatFinder 344 @ 4.57, **$5.99, last update 2024-06-06** (camera); Satellite Transponders 292. Off-job rows: DISH Anywhere, CHEF iQ, KitchenAid, a kitchen converter. DishPointer (237, last update 2024-03-05), SatCatcher (80) and Winegard (138) all tell the user to move away from magnetic interference. The dish itself is a large metal object.

- Leader on the paid side: SatFinder, $5.99, 344, 4.57, 28 months since its last update. G1 is open (stale, under 4.6).
- Difference line: the satellite arc drawn on the live sky with its heading taken from the sun, so it holds beside the dish and on a steel RV roof, where the compass reads "away from large objects".
- Not killed; carried as concept 3. Not counted: dish apps' low reviews (the feed was in back-off), and no dated source for how many people still aim dishes. This is the weakest-evidenced row in the lane.

### 1g. Noted, not a concept

- "peak finder": PeakFinder $4.99, 11,532 @ 4.71, updated 2026-10-07 (compass and motion sensors); PeakVisor 13,197 free. This is slice B (H103 S1) in H124; H130 already counted it.
- "qibla finder": 50 results, 1 paid. Every top 10 app names the compass (Muslim Pro 600,392; Qibla Compass 40,463; Qibla Finder 100% 21,801 with AR and calibration). A sun-fixed qibla is a real difference on the largest compass-dependent shelf I found. But the shelf is free, the subject is religious practice, where accuracy claims carry weight we have not reviewed, and no Investor subject covers it. I am recording it here, not grading it.
- "satellite finder", "star finder": slices A and C in H124.

## 3. What this changes

- For H124: nothing moves. Slice B (true-north) loses a claim it never made in writing: "heading from the sun" is not new on the sun shelf (Suncam, 2026-08-06). It is new on the theodolite and dish shelves, which are small.
- The phone-replaces-gadget shape (H92) has failed again on shelf age: here the gadget is $2,195 and out of stock, and the phone version is a crowded 2026 wave with no buyers visible in ratings.
- Next, if anyone takes concept 1 further: read FieldMove Clino, StraboSpot and Rocklogger (Gate 1), and count heading complaints in their reviews.
