Result: **Slice A (meteor catcher) is clear to ship** if its shower and radiant data come from the Global Meteor Network (CC BY 4.0, commercial use allowed with credit) and not from IMO's calendar, which states no licence (© IMO, all rights reserved by default). **Slice B (true-north finder) is clear on its skyline path only** (Copernicus GLO-30: free, worldwide, redistribution and modification granted, fixed notice text). Its star path is **not clear as built**: tetra3 and cedar-solve code are Apache-2.0, but the star databases they ship are built from Hipparcos, and ESA licenses Hipparcos, Tycho and Gaia DR3 under **CC BY-NC 3.0 IGO**, with any use that "directly or indirectly generates a financial gain" needing a written request to ESA first. A database rebuilt from the Yale Bright Star Catalogue (complete to about V=7, enough for H123's +4 to +5 frame limit) would avoid ESA, but no publisher page I could reach states a BSC5 licence: human check. **Slice C (sky identifier plus sightings list) is not clear as pitched**: satellites are fine (USSPACECOM gives blanket approval to redistribute TLEs/OMMs with citation), but planes are not, because OpenSky's API is for "research and non-commercial purposes" and commercial use means contacting them, which is outreach and belongs to the Investors. Stars in C have the same problem as B.

Wrenna, 2026-10-24, pages read 11:38-11:42 ET (`date` run at 11:37:27 and 11:41:58). Every row below cites the publisher's own page, read on 2026-10-24. Raw pages are in `research/hunt/H129-raw/` (fetched with `tools/fetch_cite.py` and curl, company User-Agent, no sign-in). This is a licence reading by a researcher, not legal advice: every "yes" still goes to the human check in Commandment 6 before ship.

## What this means per slice (H124's three slices)

- **A, meteor catcher: clear.** Needs radiants and shower dates (row 11, CC BY 4.0, bundled offline, credit line in Settings), the phone's own sensors for the ~10 degree attitude H124 asks, and a Sun/Moon ephemeris from published formulas (no data licence). Do not copy IMO's calendar table (row 1). If a star overlay is wanted later, it carries B's star problem.
- **B, true-north finder: clear on the skyline path, not clear on the star path.** GLO-30 (row 8) gives a written, free, worldwide grant with fixed notice texts; SRTM (row 9) is CC0 by NASA's default. The star path has Apache-2.0 code (rows 12, 14) but every database the solvers ship is Hipparcos-derived and non-commercial (rows 13, 15). Two ways out: rebuild the database from BSC5 (row 4; H123's count gives 36-46 stars per frame at its stellar limit, all inside BSC5's V=7), after a human check finds BSC5's terms; or an ESA authorisation request by email to ESA's data-licences address, which is outreach and the Investors' call (Commandment 50).
- **C, sky identifier plus sightings list: not clear as pitched.** Satellites are clear with citation (rows 5, 6), but Space-Track's API needs an account (a sign-up, Investors) and CelesTrak allows one fetch per 2 hours, so TLEs go phone-ward from our own refresh, not from each phone. Planes are not clear (row 7). Stars share B's problem. A v1 that names satellites and planets only would be clear on data; the life list itself <span title="What keeps an idea below the top: the cap that applies to it now.">holds</span> the user's own records and needs no data licence.

## Not done, and named gaps

- BSC5's terms: no publisher page reached states them (CDS ReadMe 403 on three URL forms; VizieR's licence field loads by script). This is the one check that would clear B's and C's star path without ESA.
- OpenSky's full terms-of-use page refused us (403 twice); the commercial line comes from their API documentation.
- CelesTrak states no licence of its own; the redistribution rule I rely on is Space-Track's.
- No fee is published for ESA commercial authorisation or OpenSky commercial access.
- IAU Meteor Data Center's shower list (another radiant source) not read: out of the packet's list.
