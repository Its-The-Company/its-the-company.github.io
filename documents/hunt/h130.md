Result: buyers feel the alignment difference. 62 of 1,094 low-star reviews (5.7%) on eight compass-overlay apps say the overlay points the wrong way, and 26 more (2.4%) complain about calibrating. The camera-only control, NightCap, has 0 of 161. But the errors buyers describe are mostly gross: 14 of the 17 that give a size are 90° or more. Those come from a broken heading, not the 5-15° magnetometer bias that camera alignment fixes. Only 3 of the 17 are 30° or less. The pain is real and named. The slice S1 would fix is the smaller part of it.

Maren, 2026-10-24. Board line 85 (G8 on S1, the open cell in research/hunt/H113-grades-maren.md). Written before opening research/hunt/H124.md.

## Method

- IDs: PhotoPills 596026805, Sun Seeker 330247123, SkyView 404990064 (paid), Night Sky 475772902 and Star Walk 2 Pro 892279069 came from `tools/appsearch.py`. That tool then returned HTTP 403 (Apple rate limit), so PeakFinder 357421934, PeakVisor 1187259191, Sky Guide 576588894 and NightCap Camera 754105884 came from App Store URLs already in research/. Peakr: 6752787881, found by web search ("Mountain Identifier: Peakr", Ideas All Day Ltd).
- `python3 tools/review_mine.py reviews ID --pages 10 --out research/hunt/H130-raw/<app>.md`: US most-recent feed, up to 500 reviews, keeps the 1-3 star ones.
- `tools/theme_count.py` on each file with `compass`, `off by`, `wrong direction`, `calibrat`, `misalign`, `drift`. Output is in H130-raw/theme_counts.txt, and every hit in full is in H130-raw/hits_full.txt.
- I read all 95 hits by hand and gave each one code (H130-raw/hand_codes.tsv):
  - **H**: the overlay or view points wrong. Sky, peak or sun is in the wrong place, stuck on north, or drifting.
  - **C**: calibration or compass friction with no wrong view stated: can't calibrate, must recalibrate every time, or a permission gate.
  - **X**: matched a word but is not about alignment ("cut off by the speaker", Milky Way layer missing, no tutorial).
- Limits:
  - The saved review text stops at about 300 characters, so a few reviews are cut mid-sentence.
  - The feed carries no dates, so I cannot date the complaints. Reviews mention 2021, 2023 and 2025, which suggests the 500 reviews span years.
  - SkyView is the paid app. SkyView Lite (18,798 ratings) was not pulled.

Counted: 3 at 30° or less, 14 at 90° or more. Errors this large are not magnetometer bias. They come from a heading reference flipped after an update, a sensor stuck on north, or a view that drifts. Six H reviews tie the fault to an app update or a device change: peakfinder-04, skyguide-01, skyguide-05, starwalk2-10, starwalk2-16, skyview-25. That is an app bug the buyer blames on "the compass". The small-error complaints (a few degrees to a telescope, the next peak over at 20°, 30° of azimuth) are the ones camera alignment would fix outright.

## How PeakVisor and Peakr say they align

- **PeakVisor** (App Store page, read 2026-10-24: free, 13K ratings, 4.6; IAP $9.99 a month, $39.99 a year, $159 for "3D Maps & AR Peak Finder"):
  - The description says only "uses your iPhone's camera and augmented reality (AR) technology". It does not mention a compass, calibration or accuracy.
  - The version notes do:
    - 5.101: "peak labels and silhouettes stay glued to the mountains more tightly as you move", and "You can turn Visual Stabilization on or off in Settings".
    - 5.105: "Photos taken outside PeakVisor now line up with the terrain properly too".
    - 5.114-5.116: "Mountain names sit more accurately on the landscape in the camera view".
  - Its site (peakvisor.com) says "a high-tech 3D compass" and that the Sun Trail is "a super handy tool to position the compass". Its photo page (/en/news/identify_mountains_in_photos.html) aligns by hand: "adjust the rendered 3D terrain panorama to perfectly match horizon in the photo", dragging the cross and the side rotators.
  - Reading: PeakVisor aligns the live view with the compass plus a visual stabilizer, and photos by hand-matching the horizon. Its own pages never claim automatic camera alignment to true north. Its 1-3 star reviews still carry 3 H and 3 C, including the reproduced 134° error.
- **Peakr** ("Mountain Identifier: Peakr", 6752787881, Ideas All Day Ltd; v1.0 2025-09-25; 7 ratings at 3.1; IAP $4.99 / $19.99 / $34.99):
  - Its main mode is AI identification of a photo, and the page warns: "a likely visual match, not a verified fact".
  - Its live mode, Look Around (v1.2.0), works from "location, compass, and OpenStreetMap data". In the page's words it is "an intecative view where you can literally point your phone around to find peaks, no photo recognition needed".
  - So Peakr's live view is compass-aligned, and its vision is used to name a peak in a photo, not to align the view. One of its seven reviews: "does recognize the range but doesn't identify the specific peak".

Neither page states camera-derived true-north alignment. My H113 cell said "neither states the alignment", and that holds for the live view.

## What this means for S1 (data for the chair, not a grade change)

- **Felt?** Yes. About 1 in 15 low-star reviews on a compass-overlay app says the overlay is wrong, against 0 on the camera-only control. 39 of the 62 H reviews name calibration (a script count over hand_codes.tsv; most say they tried it and it failed, a few only ask how). starwalk2-07 asks outright for a heading not "permanently linked to Apple Compass". That is the job S1 claims.
- **Is it S1's slice?** Partly. Most stated errors are 90-180° bugs, which a competent compass app would also avoid. A camera-locked heading fixes both kinds, but the selling line has to show a 20-30° miss being fixed, not a 180° one. Otherwise it reads as "we don't have their bug".
- **Size:** 2.0% of all fetched reviews (H, overlay apps), and 5.2% on SkyView. This is a top-five complaint for SkyView only. Elsewhere paywall and crashes are larger.
- Not done here: SkyView Lite, Stellarium, Sky Tonight and the sky apps' own pages for how they align. A blind second reader on hand_codes.tsv, as the X/C/H line is a judgment call on 9 reviews (photopills-02, -05; peakvisor-05, -07; skyview-10, -29, -30, -31, -43).
