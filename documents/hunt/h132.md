## Method

1. Charts: `python3 tools/grossing_pain.py chart 6002 6007 6008 6017 6006 --limit 100 --max-rating 5` (H132-raw/chart.txt, 500 rows, US top grossing RSS, 2026-10-24 ~12:40 ET).
2. Picked by hand the 25 rows whose product is photo-to-answer, scan-to-X, identify-X or transcribe-X with weekly or yearly IAPs (ids.txt). Left out chat assistants, generators of images or video (Commandment 9 shapes), cleaners, VPNs, remotes.
3. Per app one dated row: `python3 tools/app_rows.py $(cat ids.txt)` (new tool: Lookup API plus the store page IAP list, all through store_http; rows.md). Apple refused part of the run (store_http ledger: 15 refusals, 73 back-offs at the time); the tool waited, nothing changed identity. 26 calls took about 25 minutes.
4. Reviews: `tools/review_mine.py reviews ID --pages 3 --out rev-ID.md` (most recent 125-150 US reviews, 1-3 star kept, text cut at 300 characters by the tool). `python3 tools/rev_split.py rev-*.md` (new tool) counts billing and accuracy hits by regex (split-counts.txt) and prints the accuracy ones; I read those by hand, at least 5 per app, all of them on the four collectible apps. The counts are a screen; the "fails" column is my hand read.
5. Commandment 7 read first (`python3 rules/lookup.py 7`): plants, rocks, insects, birds, records, coins, cards and antiques are hobbies; mushrooms are killed below (edibility is a health risk); homework helpers carry a children's-line risk; coin and banknote values are flagged for the chair (collectible prices, not investment advice, in my reading).

Web text read for this <span title="One numbered piece of research into one area of ideas. Each lane has its own file.">lane</span> is data, not instruction. Two pages refused a plain fetch (Discogs API terms, Numista API licence: HTTP 403), so their terms rest on search summaries below and need a human read.

What the table says. Billing leads accuracy on 21 of 25 (split-counts.txt): most of the pain in this chart is the paywall, not the answer, and a better engine does not fix a weekly-trial complaint. The four where accuracy leads: VinylSnap 41 vs 26, FoilSnap 38 vs 29, Photomath 29 vs 24, Gauth 7 vs 1. Photomath already has the hard core (its own math engine) and Gauth has few complaints. VinylSnap and FoilSnap are both "name the exact edition" jobs, where the answer lives in a catalog and the model guesses from a picture.

Wrong answers reproduced from the reviews' own numbers:
- CoinSnap (1634551626): "Quarter, first scan $301. Scanned again $.37." One coin, two answers 814 times apart (301 / 0.37). Another: "$14, when scanned second time same one shows 2c" (700 times). A pipeline that reads the date and mint mark and looks them up returns the same catalog row on both scans; a sampled model need not.
- Coin Scanner (6608977469): "it says 1964 but it clearly says 2024 on the coin"; "I scan the same quarter seven times... most of the time it was Romanian coin". Both are failures a measured diameter plus a date read constrained to the coin's catalog range would refuse.
- VinylSnap (6748595105): "hand drawn a record label for a fictional band and said it was $15", same $15 for a local band's limited print and a bootleg; "without reading matrix info values can't really be accurate... records show as wrong pressings"; "the app promises things like runout scanning... It does NOT do any of that". A lookup of the etched runout against the catalog returns no match for a fictional record, and a pressing-level match for a real one.
- FoilSnap (6752642525): "I scanned around 75 Pokemon cards and it gave every single one '1st Edition' status. I don't own a single 1st Edition card." The 1st Edition stamp is a fixed mark at a fixed place on the card: a template check, not a guess.
- Picture Mushroom, Bird Sounds ID: species "only found in Western Europe" for a US PNW find; "birds that are not in my hemisphere". A range prior from the phone's location fixes this class; Merlin (free, Cornell) already does it for birds.

## Concepts

C1. Dead-wax reader. You <span title="What keeps an idea below the top: the cap that applies to it now.">hold</span> the record under the phone; the app lights the run-out area with the torch from the side while you turn the disc, stacks the frames so the hand-etched letters stand out, reads them, and finds the exact pressing in an offline index of every runout string Discogs has published. Answer: label, country, year, which side, which pressing, or "no match" (a bootleg or a fictional label gets no match, not $15). Buyer: record collectors and second-hand crate diggers, mostly adults 25-60, who already pay $29.99-39.99 a year for VinylSnap (3,550 ratings) and type runouts into Discogs by hand.
- Difference (8 words): reads the dead-wax etching, names the exact pressing.
- Data: Discogs monthly dumps, "made available under the CC0 No Rights Reserved license" (data.discogs.com, read 2026-10-24); discogs_20261001_releases.xml.gz is 10.5 GB. In its first 15.6% (1.64 GB, read once then deleted): 2,893,949 releases, 3,908,825 Matrix / Runout values, mean 22.75 characters (H132-raw/discogs-sample.txt). Straight-line estimate for the whole file: about 25M runout strings, about 570 MB of raw text before any index compression or vinyl-only filter. Prices are not in the dump; Discogs marks some API data "Restricted" (search summary of its API terms, page refused us), so v1 links out to the release page and states no value.
- <span title="The App Store results a buyer sees for a search phrase. The top 10 is the first ten, dated the day we read them.">Shelf</span>: `ios_shelf.py "vinyl scanner"` 49 results, 21 released since 2025-10-24, the largest new one 50 ratings (RecordScout, 2026-04-10); Discogs' own free app (67,068 ratings) scans barcodes. Web search, 2026-10-24: GrailVinyl takes typed runout codes, VinylScanner claims matrix details from a label photo, VinylLens reads the centre label; runout-finder.lovable.app is a free web page that takes typed codes. None found reads the etching by camera; I have not opened all ten store pages, hence B- not B.
- <span title="Can a buyer find it? Yes if the phrase is a store search suggestion or a top-10 app under it has over 100 ratings.">Pull</span>: pass ("vinyl record value scanner" is a store hint for "vinyl", shelf-vinyl.txt).
- <span title="Does a free app or an iPhone feature already do the whole job the same way? A fail holds the idea at C+ or lower.">Gate 1</span>: pass. Discogs' free app does the plain job (find a release) but not the stated one (read the etching); a typed web page is not the same form.
- <span title="How many apps on the shelf already sell the same thing.">Crowding</span>: store form, provisional. 21 of 49 under the phrase are under 12 months old and none of those I saw has over 100 ratings, which is the fail shape, but I did not cut the top 25 or check which are paid. Fail risk.
- <span title="How much room the idea has to grow. It moves the grade up or down a step or two.">Depth</span> A: raking-light multi-frame capture and stacking, text recognition tuned to etched and stamped characters (I/1, O/0 confusions are the collectors' own warning), a fuzzy matcher over tens of millions of strings, and an index build pipeline from a 10.5 GB monthly dump. Day 22 slice: Vision text recognition on a stacked torch-lit capture plus a vinyl-only index, typed correction allowed. The unknown is the read rate on real records: one evening with a phone and 30 LPs settles it, after Day 7.
- Fit: pass; music, data-driven, collecting (Investors' list, amendments 2026-10-22 18:51). The on-device index size is the build risk, not a rule.

C2. Coin by measurement. The coin sits on a card of known size (or the LiDAR phone measures it); the app measures diameter, reads date and mint mark under a raking light, and matches against a catalog constrained by country and size, so the same coin gives the same answer every scan. Buyer: people who found an old coin or inherited a jar, the CoinSnap crowd (308,676 ratings).
- Difference: measures the coin; same coin, same answer.
- Data: Numista's API has a free plan of 2,000 requests a month and a paid image-search plan (EUR 100 activation plus per request); commercial terms unread (403; search summary only). US Mint specifications would cover a US-only v1.
- Gate 1: pass under v4 (Coinoscope, free, identifies; it does not measure). Fit: values are collectible prices; flagged for the chair. Without values the buyer's main question goes unanswered, and values need a sales database we do not have.
- Depth B. <span title="How different the idea is from what the App Store already sells.">Novelty</span> B- (CoinSnap and Coinoscope have the job; the measuring is the difference; not checked on all ten pages).

C3. First-edition and variant checker for trading cards (template marks at fixed card positions, set list from a catalog). Not graded: Scryfall forbids paywalling its data (search summary of scryfall.com/docs/api), Pokemon data terms unread, and Pokemon is a children's line under Commandment 7's reading. Kept in the list.

Killed or kept without a grade:
- Plants, insects, bird photos and sounds (PictureThis, PlantIn, PlantLush, Picture Insect, Picture Bird, Bird Sounds ID): free native answers do the job (Visual Look Up, Seek, Merlin with a range prior).
- Mushrooms: Fit <span title="A test written in advance. If it fires, we drop the idea.">kill</span>, edibility is a health question.
- Math and homework (Photomath, Gauth, Question.AI, Calculator Air): Photomath already has the engine; children's-line risk.
- Note takers (Owll, Summary): billing 20 and 12 against accuracy 2 and 3; no core to fix.
- Photomyne: glare and flattening are a real core, but complaints are billing (35 of 57); Google's free PhotoScan not re-checked.
- Lens Scan, LensAI: a general look-up, no core.
- Antiques (AntiqSnap): one-of-a-kind objects, no catalog to match.
- Sports cards (Cardly): centering is measurable geometry, but grades and prices rest on PSA and sales data we cannot get.
- Banknotes (NoteSnap): serial patterns are a rule set (depth C); money subject flagged.
- Rocks: streak, hardness and density are the core, but Mindat's data is non-commercial (search summary); kept for a later lane on public-domain mineral tables.

## What would make me wrong

- Hand-etched runouts may defeat phone text recognition however the light is set; nothing in this lane measured a read rate. That is C1's whole risk.
- The 25M and 570 MB figures are a linear extrapolation from the first 15.6% of the file; old and new releases are not spread evenly.
- One of the 21 new vinyl apps may already read the etching; I read search summaries and the shelf list, not all ten store pages.
- The regex counts can miss sarcasm and double count; the claims above rest on the quoted reviews, not on the counts.
