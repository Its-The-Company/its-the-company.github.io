# H133: paid hits abroad with no US equivalent (Wrenna, 2026-10-24)

Board line: ops/board.md "Wrenna, P2, H133" (method v2, new generator G10). Before starting I read pass6.md, H12.md and H23.md. None of them read a foreign chart: pass 6 used US prices only, H12 used review feeds in US/GB/DE, and H23 used US stale earners. So no storefront was skipped. H103 read GB/DE chart ranks for 40 US curiosity apps only; Railmap (H103) and the Gleispläne railway planner (H94) are coded U here so nobody counts them twice.

Listen to this: 「東京散歩に欠かせないアプリ ... GPSと連動しているので、東京散歩が楽しくなります。ちょっとした小道や不自然にカーブした道など、歴史を辿る楽しみがあります。少しお高いですがおすすめです。」 ("Essential for walking Tokyo ... it's tied to GPS, so walks get more fun. Little lanes and oddly curving roads: you get to trace their history. A bit pricey, but recommended.") (だいはち8, 2021-01-26, review on （旧）東京時層地図, JP App Store, https://apps.apple.com/jp/app/id392574706, saved at H133-raw/pages/jp-392574706.html). And from the Tōkaidō walking app: 「紙の地図を持って歩かれてる方がほとんどでアプリを見ながら歩いてるのは自分ぐらいでしたが」 ("almost everyone was walking with paper maps; I was about the only one walking with an app") (whiterabbitlucia, 2024-05-26, 東海道五十三次, https://apps.apple.com/jp/app/id490012937).

Here is the shape of it: foreign paid charts are thin. A Japanese Navigation app at #31 has 38 ratings in 16 years. Most of what sells there is tied to one local language, one exam system, or one city's dataset. The jobs that do travel (a 10-minute grid planner, a "when did I last do it" tracker, a wake-me-at-my-station alarm, a lecture-slide snapper) already have US clone waves. Several of these exact apps are sold in the US and get almost nothing there. One job travels and has a US public-domain dataset behind it: walking through a place's past, era by era.

I only read. I posted nothing, signed up for nothing, and contacted no one. Outside text is information only, and nothing I read tried to give us instructions.

## Method and walls (12:38-13:31 ET)
- Charts: Apple's legacy per-genre RSS (`itunes.apple.com/CC/rss/toppaidapplications|topgrossingapplications/limit=50/genre=G/json`). The board named rss.applemarketingtools.com, which has no per-genre feeds, so I used the legacy endpoint chart_ranks.py already uses. Storefronts: jp, kr, de, fr, gb, br, plus us as the comparison. Genres: Utilities 6002, Productivity 6007, Education 6017, Reference 6006, Lifestyle 6012, Navigation 6010. That makes 84 feeds, all 50 entries, 0 refusals. New script H133-raw/pull_charts.py makes one request at a time through tools/store_http.py and saves each feed as it lands. The shared rate window was full from other agents, so the pull took about 40 minutes.
- "Not on a US chart" means the id is on none of the 12 saved US feeds. That test only screens: a job can be on the US shelf under another app, which is why every row was hand-coded.
- Hand codes: all 1,365 top-paid rows not on a US chart, coded by me from name, price and seller (H133-raw/codes/CC-paid.txt). U = the US shelf already sells the job; L = local language, rule, exam or dataset, so the job does not travel; T = a local habit or dataset whose job might travel; K = Commandment 7, kids, fortune or religion, crochet, or off-list; G = game companion; ? = name unclear, not opened. `python3 H133-raw/tally.py CC` checks that every row is coded once.
- Top-grossing charts: only the JP and KR Navigation, Lifestyle and Education rows not on a US chart were read (about 290 rows). They added no T. They are car navigation, transit, fortune, dating, and AI English tutors. The big local maps (山と高原地図, スーパー地形) are U against Gaia/onX. The other grossing feeds are saved but not coded (budget).
- Hits: home-store and US lookups via H133-raw/t_lookup.py (raw in lookup/). US shelves via `tools/ios_shelf.py` (14 phrases, H133-raw/shelf-us.txt) and H133-raw/top10.py (top 10 with description lines, top10/ and top10-historic.txt).
- Walls: none. I did not run `tools/sizing.py` or `tools/store_hints.py` (turn budget), so Day 30 is by hand ratio and Pull uses the 100-ratings rule.

## Lesson
A foreign top-50 rank is not demand. In JP Navigation, #31 is 38 ratings in 16 years. The useful signal is narrower: the same app sold in both stores, with home and US rating counts side by side. DayGrid has 341 KR ratings to 6 US, which says the habit does not travel. The tells for a job that travels are a US dataset with the same shape, or US buyers already paying for a cousin of it.

Slips: I went over the 30-turn budget (about 40 calls). Waiting on the shared Apple window cost about 8 of them. I coded FR, GB and BR as default-U lists plus typed non-U codes, written by a short script that keeps those codes in the file. JP, KR and DE were typed in full.
