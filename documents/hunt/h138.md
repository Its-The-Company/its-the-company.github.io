# H138: paid replacements for Apple's own free apps (G14, method v2)

Wrenna, 2026-10-24, 12:45-13:37 ET. Board line Refill 16. Raw in research/hunt/H138-raw/.

Listen to this, from a Things 3 buyer, five days old: "Apple Reminders has caught up to it in many ways, so I'm not sure it's worth $10." (Things 3 review, rev-904237743.json, 4 stars.) And from a Sun Seeker buyer: "If you want an accurate way to plan PV layout; this is worth every penny." (rev-330247123.json.) Here is the shape of it: people pay to leave a free Apple app when the paid one does the job more exactly, or reaches a place Apple's does not (the wrist, the car), or takes something of theirs Apple's will not (their own audio files, a paper map). A prettier skin alone is the weakest reason, and the one Apple catches up on.

## Method

1. Charts: US top paid and top grossing, 200 each, in Weather, Photo & Video, Productivity, Navigation, Utilities, Reference, Books, Health & Fitness, Lifestyle (18 feeds, `H138-raw/collect.py`, names in `charts.tsv` via `chartnames.py`). Searches: 2-3 buyer phrases per Apple app, 25 results each (37 phrases, `search.json`, `shelf.tsv`).
2. Per Apple app, the replacements that chart (paid or grossing), picked by hand; 26 apps. Store page IAP list and the 50 most recent reviews, 4-5 stars kept (`reviews.py`; `iap.tsv`, `rev-ID.json`, `rows.tsv` from one lookup call). All Apple calls through tools/store_http.py; no refusals, but the shared limit made 18 chart feeds take about 10 minutes.
3. Reviews holding pay or switch words (pay, worth, subscription, lifetime, stock, default, built-in, instead of, better than...) read by hand and coded one line per review in `codes.tsv`; counts below summed from that file. The finder phrase list is mine; a review that says why it pays without those words is missed, so the counts are a floor for each reason.
4. Kill check: Apple's iOS 26 press release, saved as `H138-raw/apple-ios26-newsroom.html` (https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/06/apple-elevates-the-iphone-experience-with-ios-26/). Only Translate and Maps are named there among the 13; the rest have no iOS 26 change in that release (I did not read each app's support pages: budget).

Not done: tools/ios_shelf.py and price_table.py were not run as such; their two calls (search and store page) are done by collect.py and reviews.py for all 26 apps at once. leader_asks.py's ask-phrase filter was replaced by a pay/switch filter, since the job here is what buyers pay for, not what they ask for. DeepL and Pedometer++ not pulled (ids not in my pulls). sizing.py not run: Day 30 and Day 90 are n/c.

Totals from codes.tsv: 26 apps, 838 kept reviews, 169 read; D 32, X 22, O 15, C 14, F 14, P 7, N 67 (a review can carry two codes). Gaia GPS and Tape Measure gave 0 pay/switch reviews in their newest 50.

What the rows say:
- The paid chart, not the grossing chart, is where Apple's apps get replaced by depth: RadarScope, Theodolite, Sun Seeker, Things, Due, Bound, Just Press Record are one-price apps from 2009-2019 still in the top 30 of their genre. The grossing replacements (Carrot, Day One, Gaia, iTranslate) win on breadth and reach.
- "Pays once" is a sales line on its own (15), concentrated in calendars and to-do lists (11 of 15), where depth is thin.
- Where buyers name accuracy (D), the subject is narrow and physical: radar, the sun's path, a peak's bearing, a translation. That is where an algorithm can be the difference.
- What are they doing instead? On Maps, a 2023-last-updated $19.99 app with a buyer saying it broke on iOS 16 still sits at paid Navigation #67, because it holds the paper quads.

Killed: a translation-accuracy concept (iTranslate buyers' D 5 of 5) by iOS 26 Live Translation on-device in Messages, FaceTime and Phone (release above). Not raised: calendar, to-do, notes, journal and step concepts, where buyers pay for a single price, reach or a skin, and the depth line is empty.

## Lesson

A free-app replacement sells depth where the subject is physical and checkable (radar, sun, bearing); where it is not, it sells a single price, and Apple catches up on that.
