## Method

- Tool: `tools/wikidata_cover.py` (sample, cover, table); test `tests/wikidata_cover_test.py`
  52 pass, 0 fail, fixture responses only.
- Per title: one `wbsearchentities` call (English label and alias search, 10 hits), then one
  `wbgetentities` call (claims) on up to 5 hits whose English description fits the kind (or the
  top 3 if none does). Match = first hit, in search order, whose P31 or description fits film or
  series and whose year (P577; P580 or P577 for series; earliest) is within 1 of the row's year.
  No SPARQL. One request at a time, 0.5 s apart, `maxlag=5` on every Wikidata call (API
  etiquette for a task no user waits on), User-Agent naming the project and the repository.
- Truth: when the list row links an English Wikipedia article, the item that article belongs
  to (one call per 50 articles) is the right answer. "Right" counts unmatched rows as not right.
- Fields read on the match: runtime P2047, director P57, genre P136, episode count P1113
  (series only), and two join keys, Letterboxd film ID P6127 and IMDb ID P345.
- Wikidata was lagged above 5 s from 14:13 to 14:32 ET (wdqs1012 at about 10 s); the tool waited
  as asked and stopped twice with exit 3, then ran 14:33-14:42 (436 calls, 46 lag waits) and
  14:42-14:57 for the stand-in (resumed once after a second lag stop at row 89). No etiquette
  was lowered.

Wrong items seen (9): same-name films ("Beats", "Dear David"), a documentary vs its subject
("Eat the Rich: The GameStop Saga"), a franchise vs one edition ("Too Hot to Handle: Germany"),
and "Arrested Development" (the 2013 Netflix season matched, not the 2003 series).
Unmatched "no year" films are mostly items with no P577 yet ("Barbie: Epic Road Trip",
"Being Eddie", "Aap Jaisa Koi").

## Findings

1. Films that Wikipedia lists are well labelled: 78-95% matched by title and year, and director
   and genre on 92-100% of matches (n=100 and n=100).
2. Series are the weak half: 69% matched, runtime on 22% and director on 39% of matches, episode
   count on 75% (n=100). Watch-time questions over TV cannot lean on Wikidata runtimes.
3. Title search is not identity: 6% of truth-known rows matched the wrong item (n=160). The app
   would need a join key, not a title search, wherever the export offers one.
4. Every matched film carries a Letterboxd ID and an IMDb ID (173 of 173). Whether a Letterboxd
   export row can be joined on P6127 depends on what the export's URI column <span title="What keeps an idea below the top: the cap that applies to it now.">holds</span> (H21 says the
   ZIP has diary, ratings, watched and reviews CSVs; the URI format is not checked here).
5. The recent, non-English and documentary tail is where matches fail: no publication date yet,
   same-name items, or no English label.

## Not measured

- A real Letterboxd export. The stand-in is an upper bound, not a library.
- Non-US and pre-1950 films, and titles with no Wikipedia list entry.
- The licence itself: Wikidata is CC0; the runtime and genre values were not checked for
  accuracy against any other source.
