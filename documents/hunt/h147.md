Result: 320 of 508 chart apps read (14,705 most-recent US reviews, page 1 of the review RSS, fetched 2026-10-24 14:05-15:05 ET); 13 phrase hits, 0.88 per 1,000 reviews; 2 are false positives, 1 is praise; 9 of the 11 real ones ask for a different price form (one-time instead of subscription), not a missing job; 2 name a job: one that iOS gives no API for, one already sold behind a subscription on its own shelf. Three concepts below with author cells; the generator as specified does not find a new job. The strongest signal is for our own pricing: buyers on these charts say "one-time" in their own words.

## Method

- Charts: Apple's top free RSS, limit 100, US, genres Productivity 6007 (99 listed), Utilities 6002, Education 6017, Reference 6006, Health and Fitness 6013, Photo and Video 6008; `research/hunt/H147-raw/chart.json`. Health and Fitness kept by name only (walk, step, pedometer, hike, trail): 9 of 100 (Commandment 7). A summary filter kept 36 including diet and heart-rate apps, so I dropped it.
- Reviews: `python3 tools/pay_phrases.py fetch` (new; test `tests/pay_phrases_test.py`, 21 checks green), one call at a time through tools/store_http.py; apps interleaved by rank across genres, so the cut falls evenly. Page 1 only (up to 50 most recent); the median app's oldest review read is 2026-09-27, so this is about a month of voice per app.
- Phrases (regex, case-insensitive, straight or curly apostrophe): would pay, I'd pay, take my money, pay for a version, one-time purchase/payment/fee/price. "wouldn't pay" is not a hit.
- Every hit hand-read in full (`H147-raw/hits.tsv`, text cut at 1,200 characters; the longest hit is 1,041). Codes: P price form, J job asked, X praise, N not a pay statement.
- Paid check: `tools/shelf_top10.py` on the phrase a buyer would type (`H147-raw/shelves/`).
- Raw reviews (13 MB) stay in /tmp/H147-reviews; the repo keeps chart.json, per_app.tsv (n reviews and hits per app), hits.tsv and the shelf JSON (1.4 MB).

## What it means

The phrase "would pay" in top-free reviews is mostly about how to pay, not what for. Nine of eleven genuine statements reject a subscription and name a one-time price ($3, $5, $10, $20, $200). That is a price-form signal, and v2 grades a price form as a property the buyer cannot see in a screenshot. The two jobs: Reels-only blocking is closed by the platform, not by competitors (an engineer's ruling: I know of no public API that reaches inside another app's UI; the nearest buildable thing is a whole-app schedule shield, which Opal and others sell); plain-language sky search exists today behind Night Sky's subscription.

For our own product this is useful evidence: buyers on these six charts ask in their own words for a one-time unlock, at $3 to $20. That supports the StoreKit 2 one-time unlock already planned (non-consumable plus restore), whatever the product is.

Difference lines (at most eight words):
- C1: ask the sky in words, offline, once.
- C2: removes Reels, keeps Instagram.
- C3: Quizlet's ease at a single price.

Depth, as an engineer weighs it:
- C1: two to four weeks. A sky engine (planet positions from Schlyter/VSOP-class series, as in H121-raw/moonrise.py; satellites from CelesTrak elements through SGP4; the 570 KB BSC5 catalogue from H123), then a question parser on Apple's on-device model (iOS 26 Foundation Models) mapping "bright, low, west, now" onto alt/az and magnitude filters. The parser is the new part and the testable part: fixtures of a question, a place and a time, with a known answer from the ephemeris. Room to grow (events, "will I see it tomorrow"). It is B because the engine is real work; it is not A because the four leaders already have the engine.
- C2: what can be built is a ManagedSettings shield of whole apps on a schedule plus a Safari content blocker on instagram.com/reels; that is a weekend, and the shelf has it. The asked-for part has no API.
- C3: an FSRS scheduler and an .apkg reader (zip plus SQLite) and a Quizlet text import: a parser across formats, a week or two. Nothing a buyer sees in a screenshot.

## What would make me wrong

- The 188 unread apps (ranks 63-100) are smaller apps where a missing job is likelier to be voiced; a second pass there could find one. Same command, cache kept 6 h.
- Page 1 is one month. Pages 2-10 reach back further; a 1-3 star read of fewer apps may carry more job asks than a 50-recent read of many.
- The phrase list is the board's five; "wish it could", "would buy" or "worth paying" are not in it and may carry the jobs.
