The premise of G18 does not hold on these shelves: reviews that say the writer would rather pay once number about 5 of 2,746 (0.2%) after a hand-read, and the leaders on two of the five top-share jobs already sell a lifetime or one-time price. The resentment is about trial-to-charge traps, weekly billing and refused refunds, not about the shape of the price. Three concepts carry the v2 author row; two of the five top-share jobs are killed on rules (Commandment 9, Fit). Best as author: Keeper, a one-price photo cleanup (Novelty C+, Depth B-). ## 1. Method and tools

- `python3 tools/sub_resent.py chart research/hunt/H149-raw 6007 6002 6017 6006 6008 --per 8` (new tool): US Top Grossing RSS per genre, limit 100, each app looked up; an app counts as a subscription app when its description names a subscription or auto-renewal (Apple's 3.1.2 disclosure). First eight such apps per genre in chart order, no app twice: H149-raw/apps.tsv (fetched 14:12 ET). The board line named tools/chart_ranks.py; that tool ranks given ids in other storefronts, so the grossing pull went through the same feed grossing_pain.py uses.
- `sub_resent.py reviews ... --pages 4`: up to 200 most-recent US reviews per app (customer-review RSS); 1-2 star reviews kept in H149-raw/low-<id>.tsv (text cut at 220 characters) with two regex flags, P (price, subscription, charge, trial, refund, cancel) and O (one-time, lifetime, pay once). `sub_resent.py shares` writes H149-raw/shares.tsv.
- `sub_resent.py store` and `tools/shelf_top10.py` for the five jobs, store order, plus a description scan for one-time or lifetime wording (H149-raw/onetime-scan.txt, H149-raw/store-*.tsv, H149-raw/shelf_top10.txt).

**Hand-read check of the flags (seeded, random.seed(149)).** P: 20 flagged reviews drawn from the first 1,558 low reviews: 17 of 20 are a price or subscription complaint (misses: "wasted my money" on a blocker that does not block, a "$200 piano", one with no price word in view). 20 unflagged: 1 of 20 is a paywall complaint the regex missed ("you need to unlock them"). So P over-counts by about 15% of flagged and under-counts by about 5% of unflagged; the totals roughly cancel (about 86 false against about 49 missed on that sample's sizes). O: all 26 flagged read by hand; 21 are "one time" meaning "once" ("I used it one time"), "lifetime" meaning a life, or a wish for a subscription. True "I would pay once" or "not a one-time payment" reviews: Quizlet 2 ("not just a one time purchase like ... anki"; "if it was a one time payment I'd consider it"), CamScanner 2 ("not a one time payment it is a monthly subscription"; "a lifetime subscription option"), Cleaner Guru 1 (the weekly cost compared with paying once; truncated, counted as half). About 5 of 2,746.

## 5. What would make this wrong

- The P flag is a regex; the hand-read check is 40 of 2,746 reviews (precision 17/20, misses 1/20). The ranking among the top five could move with a full hand code; the 43% total is a rough figure.
- 200 most-recent reviews per app is one month or less for the large apps; a bad week (Gmail's redesign at 170 of 200 low) shapes the share. The P share divides by low reviews, so it is less sensitive to that than the low share itself.
- "Would pay once" may live in 3-star reviews or outside the store (Reddit). I read only 1-2 stars; a G18 rerun on 3-star reviews could find more.
- The one-time and Lifetime readings come from descriptions; In-App Purchase lists were not read (App Store pages), so "names Lifetime" is the maker's own word, not a price seen.
