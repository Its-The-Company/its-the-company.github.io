## 4. Weakness 3, the compatibility share is unmeasured (H153, on H152's corpus)

**Waits on:** H153's static share with n and the script list (G4); H152's corpus if committed, else Idris's own 30-script list.

**Lift (answers the first countable reason, "the difference is visible on Day 22"), all of these:**
1. n is at least 30 scripts from at least 15 repositories (so no one author's style sets the share).
2. The v1 slice (console, dialogs, clipboard, the `ui` subset in PITCH-12 section 7 Day 8-10) covers at least 50% of n by static import and call analysis: at least 15 of 30, or the same share of a larger n. Half the real scripts an owner has can run on the version that sells in the Day 30 window.
3. v1 and 1.1 together cover at least 75% of n. The product as pitched, at full depth, serves three in four old scripts; the "out" class (modules the pitch never names) is under a quarter.

**Kill (any one):**
- K3-1. The v1 slice covers under 25% of n (7 or fewer of 30). The Day 22 product serves fewer than a quarter of real scripts, and the store page may claim no more than that number (PITCH-12 weakness 3). The difference the Novelty cell rests on is not visible in use to most owners on launch day; Novelty drops one step to C+, the ladder gives B (C+ up two for Depth A), and the chair's step holds: **cap B-**.
- K3-2. v1 and 1.1 together cover under 50% of n. The pitched module list, at full depth, misses half of what old scripts use; Depth A was graded on a reimplementation that reaches the scripts, and the lane shows it does not. Depth drops one step to B; the ladder gives B, the chair's step holds: **cap B-**.
- K3-3. H153 lands with a share but without the per-script list, or with n under 20, or with the test failing: G2, the share is unmeasured, nothing moves, and PITCH-12 keeps its "unmeasured" line.

Static analysis counts imports and calls, not behaviour; a script that imports only v1 modules can still fail on an unshimmed argument. So the lift here answers "visible on Day 22" and no more; the Simulator harness after a choice sets the number the store page may claim.

## 10. What would make these rules wrong

- The thresholds (50% / 25% v1 share, 75% / 50% v1+1.1, 30 scripts from 15 repos, 20 / 4 M posts, 15 authors, half of A, 30 / 10 usable scripts, 10 store-naming posts) are my judgment, not calibrated against earners. I wrote them now so they cannot bend later. If a lane shows one was badly placed, I write a new rule below the append line, dated, and apply it only to lanes that land after it.
- The M count reads the forum and the subreddit in 2026, and a forum for a dead app empties as its owners leave; a low M can mean they left rather than that they are content. That is why the kill sits at 4 and the lift at 20, with a wide gap that holds B.
- Static import analysis over-counts compatibility (a script can import only v1 modules and still need an unshimmed call) and public GitHub scripts over-represent the owners who publish; the harness after a choice corrects the first, nothing in this round corrects the second.
- Section 6 writes "permits or is silent" as the clear for the documentation licence because a licence that is silent on API description is the common case, and the question it leaves is the Investors' (G6), not a reason to hold a letter. If the Investors want counsel's read before any shim, that is their call under C50 and it changes the build plan, not this grade.
- Nothing here tests whether a stranded owner pays $9.99 again rather than rewriting. That is the price test after a choice, not a hunt rule.
