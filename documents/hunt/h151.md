What the sources say, read plainly, with no opinion on what follows from it:
- The incumbent's pages carry only the PSF notice (rows 1-4), including the pages Ole Zorn wrote. Its licence page is an old 3.6.1 copy that lacks the 0BSD sentence for code examples.
- Google v. Oracle decided fair use on its facts, assumed copyrightability, and said it was not answering more than it had to (rows 9-10).
- The PSF policy lists "Python", the logos, "PyCon" and "PyLadies" as its marks. The only time the pages I read discuss a bare "Py" prefix is for event names (row 11). The pages I read do not say whether "Runpy" needs permission. It is not "Python", so the "Python in product names" rule does not plainly reach it, and the policy is silent on "Py" in product names.

## Questions only counsel can answer (for the report's Investor questions, Commandment 50)

1. Which licence, if any, covers the Pythonista-only module pages (ui, scene, canvas, console and the rest)? Ole Zorn wrote them (sectionauthor), but the pages carry a PSF copyright footer and nothing else (rows 1, 2, 4). May a paid app copy their prose, or must we write our own reference text from scratch?
2. If we reimplement the same module names, function signatures and behaviour (the documented API) in a paid iOS app, is that covered in the US by the Google v. Oracle fair-use reasoning, given that the Court assumed copyrightability and limited itself to its facts (rows 9-10)? Does it matter that we would sell worldwide?
3. In the EU and UK, does the SAS v WPL judgment let a paid app reproduce another program's functionality and interfaces, and how far may our documentation follow the incumbent's manual? Row 8 has no quote yet: counsel should read the judgment itself.
4. Does shipping CPython and its docs inside the app, or reusing PSF doc text, trigger the PSF Licence v2 clause 2 and 3 duties (keep the notice, include a summary of changes)? Are the doc code examples under 0BSD (row 7), or are older copies under PSF-2 only (row 3)?
5. Does a commercial product named "Runpy" need PSF permission? The policy covers "the PSF trademarks", and "Py" is not named as one (row 11). Does the collision with the stdlib `runpy` module name (row 12) raise confusion, App Store review, or trademark-clearance concerns that a search of the trademark registers would answer?
6. May the store listing say "runs Python" or "Python 3 on iPhone" under the nominative "always allowed" clause (row 11)? May it name the incumbent ("Pythonista-compatible scripts") in the description or keywords?

Not done: the SAS text (row 8); a trademark register search for "RUNPY" (USPTO/EUIPO: not tried, and the decision belongs to counsel); the omz site's terms page, if one exists (none is linked from the docs index).
