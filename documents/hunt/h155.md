Owner: Idris. Board line: "H155 forecast scorer lift read". <span title="The worker who reads both graders and decides. The decision, a ruling, is one row in the rulings file.">Chair</span> ruling: research/hunt/rulings-v2.md, H138 C2 (Ines, 14:07).

The lift, as the ruling writes it: "one month of NBM station text for three US stations scored against their observations, models differ enough to be worth a screen, fetch size per phone measured, archive terms read." It decides; I apply it as written.

## Archive terms (read before any data fetch, 2026-10-24 ~15:50 ET)

Source: Iowa Environmental Mesonet, Iowa State University. Disclaimer page: https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/disclaimer.php

> "The materials found on this website are in the public domain and may be used freely by anyone for any lawful purpose. Attributing the Iowa Environmental Mesonet of Iowa State University would be appreciated."

> "While we use care to provide accurate weather/climatic information, errors may occur because of equipment or other failure. We therefore provide this information without any warranty of accuracy."

Rate guidance, https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/cgi-bin/request/daily.py?help :

> "This service has a per-IP throttle requiring sequential requests with a two second sleep between requests."

The MOS service page (https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/cgi-bin/request/mos.py?help) states no throttle. I apply the stricter one to every call: sequential requests, 3 s sleep between them, one fetch per station and model, and nothing re-fetched (raw kept in /tmp, summaries in H155-raw/).

## Pre-registered (committed before any error is computed)

Stations, three US climates: KDSM Des Moines (continental, IA_ASOS), KPHX Phoenix (desert, AZ_ASOS), KSEA Seattle (marine, WA_ASOS).

Models: NBS (NBM short-range station text, the "NBS" bulletin) and GFS MOS (MAV, IEM model code GFS). NBH (hourly, to ~25 h) carries no max/min, so it is not scored for high/low; if it has a precipitation probability for the day it is not used either, to keep the comparison on one footing. NAM MOS is added only if the same call pattern returns it; it does not count toward the lift.

Window: 12Z runs from 2026-09-22 through 2026-10-21 (30 runs per model and station). Lead day 1 = the local day after the run date, lead day 2 = the one after that.

Forecast fields: max at the forecast hour 00Z-06Z ending the local day, min at 12Z-18Z of the local morning (local day = valid time minus 12 h, as a date). Precipitation day forecast "yes" when the larger of the two 12 h PoPs ending 00Z and 12Z that fall on that local day is 50% or more.

Observations: IEM daily.py max_temp_f, min_temp_f, precip_in for the same station. Rain day "yes" when precip_in >= 0.01 (trace is no). Known mismatch: the ASOS day is midnight to midnight local standard time, while MOS max is 7am-7pm and min 7pm-8am. It biases both models against the same observation, so it does not favour one model; absolute errors read a little high.

Error: mean absolute error in whole-degree F for high and low; fraction of days wrong for rain yes/no. A day counts only when both models and the observation are present (paired), and n is that paired count.

Display threshold, fixed now: the models "differ enough to show on a screen" at a station and lead when, with paired n >= 20,
- the MAE gap in high or low is >= 1.0 F (a screen shows whole degrees; under 1 F the scorecard would print the same number for both), or
- the rain wrong-rate gap is >= 10 percentage points (3 days in 30),
and the gap has the same sign in the first and second half of the window (a stability check, not a significance test).

Lift fires on the "differ" leg when at least 2 of the 3 stations show at least one such gap. The other legs: fetch size per phone measured (bytes for 30 days of one station, both models plus observations), and whether that fetch needs a server we run.

Errors are MAE in F for hi/lo, fraction of days wrong for rain; n = paired local days (both models and the observation present); 12Z runs 2026-09-22 to 2026-10-21. Command: `python3 tools/forecast_score.py fetch DIR` then `score DIR`; summaries in H155-raw/ (table.md, score.json, fetch.json), raw CSVs left in /tmp.

**Differ leg: does not fire.** 0 of 3 stations show a gap over the pre-registered threshold (needs 2). Every n is 30, so the misses are not for want of days. The nearest: KSEA lead-1 high, NBS 1.40 vs GFS 2.37 F (gap 0.97, both halves the same sign), and KSEA lead-2 high (0.90); KPHX lead-1 low, NBS 1.83 vs GFS 2.77 (0.93, both halves). These are under 1.0 F by a few hundredths to a tenth; I <span title="What keeps an idea below the top: the cap that applies to it now.">hold</span> the line as written and do not round. Rain gaps are 0-7 points, under 10, and Phoenix had almost no rain days, so its rain row says little. On this month the two NOAA models agree to within a degree everywhere; a scorecard would mostly print the same whole number twice.

**Fetch size, one station, 30 days (measured):** NBS 401,663-408,720 bytes, GFS MOS 256,939-258,442, ASOS daily 1,248-1,254; total about 667 KB as served. IEM sent it uncompressed (no Content-Encoding on a gzip request); gzipped the same bytes are about 100 KB (NBS ~71-73 KB, GFS ~28-31 KB). The month request carries all four daily runs; only 12Z is scored, about a quarter of the rows (KDSM NBS 690 of 2,783), but mos.py has no run-hour filter, so a phone gets the quarter only by one request per run (30 per model, sequential).

**Server we run:** not needed to make the read work. mos.py and daily.py answer plain HTTPS GETs with no key and CSV out, so a phone can call them directly. The open part is permission at scale, not mechanics: IEM is a university service with a per-IP throttle on daily.py and no stated service level, and asking IEM whether an app may point its users at it is outreach, which is the Investors' call. NOAA's operational servers keep only recent runs of these bulletins (my knowledge, not read today; NCEI's archive terms unread), so without IEM a backfill would likely need our own archive, which is a server we would run.

**Terms:** quoted above with the link; public domain, attribution appreciated, no warranty; 2 s throttle respected (3 s between all 9 calls, one call each).

## Verdict for the chair

Three of the four legs are met: per-model errors with n, fetch size measured, terms read. The leg the ruling calls "models differ enough to be worth a screen" fails on the threshold fixed in commit 35c4e458 before any error was computed. As written, the lift does not fire, so H138 C2 stays at B-. Not tested here: other months (one month in early autumn), other stations, NBM's own PoP against GFS in a wet season, and Apple's or CARROT's forecasts, which the ruling already notes the app could not score for free.
