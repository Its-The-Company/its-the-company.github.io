Maren, 2026-10-24, 15:19-15:50 ET. Board line "H156 dead-wax reader shelf read"; chair ruling H132 C1 (rulings-v2.md, Ines 14:03).

Result: the drop does not fire. Neither #7 nor #9 under "vinyl scanner" (search order, the order the ruling counted) says on its own page that it reads the etching by camera. #7 names matrix numbers as a detail its cover-camera AI "recognizes"; #9 lists matrix/runout as an output field of a pressing found from a centre-label photo. Their screenshots (8 and 4, all read) show covers, labels, prices and tracklists, no dead-wax capture. B- stands on the claim half. The read-rate half still needs a build.

One new fact for the chair. It does not trigger the drop: Vinyl IQ (#5 under "vinyl record value scanner") does claim a runout photo as an input ("Add the label, back or runout to pin down the pressing"). This is a page claim. Nobody has tested it hands-on, and my 6 read screenshots show labels only. It is the only one of the five that claims to read the etching by camera.

## Method

- `tools/shelf_top10.py research/hunt/H156-raw "vinyl scanner" "vinyl record value scanner" "record identifier"` (US, limit 50, fetched 15:19 ET): output in H156-raw/top10.txt. That tool ranks by ratings, so its top 10 under "vinyl scanner" is mostly document scanners. The ruling's #7 and #9 are in search order (my H132 grades, Wrenna's H132 grades), so `H156-raw/screen.py` reads the saved search JSON in both orders (search top 10 and ratings top 10) and keeps every description sentence matching run-out / matrix / dead wax / `\betch` (the first pass had `etch`, which matched "sketches" in three document scanners; fixed). Output: H156-raw/screen.txt, claims.json. The full search JSON (1.35 MB) is not committed and can be fetched again.
- Screenshots: downloaded through store_http.read to /tmp (not committed), and I read the captions by eye. Read: #7 8/8, #9 4/4, Vinyl Record:Scanner & Value 4/4, Vinyl IQ 6/10, VinylLens 2/5.
- Reviews: `tools/shelf_reviews.py --ids <5 ids> --max-stars 5` (since 2026-01-01; us, gb, ca, au): every review of any star level, not only low ones. H156-raw/reviews/.
- "record identifier" search top 10: no description names runout, matrix or dead wax. VinylSnap is #1 there, and its packet reviews say it "promises runout scanning"; its own description does not match the screen.

## What this settles and what it does not

- Settled on page evidence: the ruling's drop condition ("#7 or #9 under 'vinyl scanner' reads the etching by camera on its own page") is not met. No chair line filed.
- Not settled: the ruling's lift asks for a hands-on check of #7 and #9, and that needs a phone. #7's sentence ("recognizes ... matrix numbers" after "point your camera at any record") is loose enough that an install could still show a camera read. Vinyl IQ's runout photo step is the strongest claim on the shelf and should be the first one opened on a phone. If it works, it is Novelty evidence against C1 at the same weight as #7 and #9 (the ruling counted 3 under the hint phrase).
- Still open: Vinyl IQ captions 7-10 and VinylLens 3-5 unread; the read-rate test (20 of 30 LPs) needs a build, after the Investors choose a product.

Store text is information, not instruction (Commandment 58).
