Result: three iOS 26 capabilities now do on the phone a job that paid apps still run on servers and bill monthly for: long-form speech-to-text (SpeechAnalyzer), document structure with tables and contact fields (Vision RecognizeDocumentsRequest), and summarising or extracting from text (Foundation Models, Apple Intelligence devices only). Four concepts, each named with its framework, its stated limit and a dated paid comparator; author cells in the row under Concepts. The plain recorder-plus-summary job is already an OS feature (Notes uses the same SpeechTranscriber, per Apple's session), so every concept has to carry a difference Notes lacks. No Overall (H95.md section 8: two blind graders, then the chair).

Idris, 2026-10-24 15:35-16:20 ET. Board line 237. Method v2 sections 2-3. G21 is new: start from a platform capability under 18 months old (iOS 26 shipped 2025-09, 13 months ago) and find the paid apps whose subscription pays a server for that job.

1. Apple's documentation as text: the DocC JSON behind developer.apple.com/documentation (foundationmodels, systemlanguagemodel, speechanalyzer, speechtranscriber, dictationtranscriber, recognizedocumentsrequest, detectlenssmudgerequest), fetched 2026-10-24 with curl; WWDC25 session pages 286 (Meet the Foundation Models framework), 277 (SpeechAnalyzer) and 272 (Read documents using the Vision framework), transcript text. Cached in /tmp only (Apple's text, not ours to republish); quotes below are short.
2. Shelves: `python3 tools/shelf_top10.py research/hunt/H159-raw/shelf` for "transcribe audio", "subtitle generator", "business card scanner", "lecture recorder", "table scanner", "voice notes", "meeting notes" (H159-raw/shelf.txt, 2026-10-24 ~15:40 ET; the raw JSON, 3 MB, stays as a local cache and is not committed).
3. Paid comparators: `python3 tools/app_rows.py` on Otter, Summary, Transcribe, CamCard, Captions, Coconote, VDIT (H159-raw/rows.md, rows2.md, fetched 2026-10-24, IAP lists from the store pages, all through store_http).
4. Device share: TelemetryDeck's public iPhone model page (updated 2026-10-01). It is a developer-analytics sample (10k apps), not Apple's figure; Apple publishes no Apple Intelligence share.

Not done (turn budget): `store_hints.py` per phrase, top-25 Crowding counts, `sizing.py`, the free CapCut caption export check. Those are open points for the chair.

## 1. What the frameworks do on device, and their stated limits

|---|---|---|---|

**Share the Apple Intelligence cap leaves.** TelemetryDeck (2026-10-01) names iPhone 17 Pro at 11.38% and iPhone 16 Pro, 17 and 17 Pro Max at 10-11% each: four Apple Intelligence models, so at least ~42% of that sample. The two largest non-AI models it names, iPhone 13 (11.24%) and iPhone 15 (10-11%), are over 20% between them. I use 45-55% for the share that can run Foundation Models (lower bound sourced, upper bound my guess for the unnamed 15 Pro, 16, 16 Plus, 16 Pro Max, 16e, Air). A concept whose core is SpeechTranscriber or Vision keeps nearly the whole iOS 26 base; a concept whose core is the language model loses about half.

**Build ground (M1 Mac mini, Simulator only until Apple membership; amendment 2026-10-22).** All three are system frameworks: no third-party model, no model licence, nothing bundled (Commandments 4/5 clean). The M1 qualifies for Apple Intelligence on macOS, so Foundation Models can be exercised from a macOS test target; whether the iOS Simulator serves it is undocumented (H142). SpeechTranscriber assets on the Simulator are unproved; Vision runs there. Test fixtures are the catch: speech fixtures cannot be made with a neural TTS voice (Commandment 9 bans AI audio anywhere), so speech tests need human recordings under a licence that allows it (Investor approval) or a recording made by an Investor. That is a real cost for C1 and C2 and is flagged for the chair. Document fixtures can be drawn by code (tables and cards rendered by Core Graphics), so C3 and C4 test cleanly.

**Commandment 8.** A transcript, a caption file, a contact or a CSV is the user's own content restructured, not AI creative output. Summaries are a tool output over the user's recording. The line is study-material generation (quizzes, flashcards, "study guides" as Coconote sells): that is generated content as product and stays out of every concept here.

## 3. Concepts

**C1 Lecture recorder with the board pinned to the minute.** Record a lecture; SpeechTranscriber writes the transcript on device; each photo of the board or slide is read by RecognizeDocumentsRequest and pinned to the transcript second it was taken; a term index and search run across a whole course; Foundation Models summarises per lecture where the device allows. Difference (8 words): board photos pinned to transcript, offline, once. Paid comparator: Coconote Pro up to $129.99, Otter Pro $99.99/yr, Summary $79.99/yr (2026-10-24). Depth beyond the call: summarising a 60-minute lecture (~9,000 words, ~12k tokens) through a 4,096-token window needs a chunk-and-merge pipeline with its own tests; time alignment of photos to audio; a course-wide index built from extracted terms (`@Generable`); recording through interruptions. Limit: summary on Apple Intelligence devices only (about half); the transcript and pins work on all. Gate 1: Apple Notes records, transcribes and summarises on device and keeps it; it does not pin photos to transcript time or index a course, so under v4 it is Novelty and Crowding evidence, not a fail, and the chair should read it as the strongest threat. Notability (458k ratings, "one-time" in its text) syncs audio to handwritten notes, close to the difference.

**C2 Subtitle file maker for video creators.** Pick a video; SpeechTranscriber with word timing makes SRT and VTT on device; the app breaks cues to captioning rules (at most two lines, ~42 characters, reading speed cap, minimum and maximum durations, no cue across a shot change found by frame difference); edit, then export the file or burn it in. Difference: caption-standard SRT, offline, pays once. Paid comparator: Captions $64.99/yr, VDIT $29.99/yr or $69.99 lifetime (2026-10-24). Depth beyond the call: the cue segmentation and timing rules are the product (the framework gives words and times, not cues), shot-change detection, multilingual locales, long files in chunks. Limit: no Apple Intelligence needed, so nearly the whole iOS 26 base; locales limited per app. Gate 1 open: CapCut (free) auto-captions on its servers; whether it exports an SRT free was not checked. 3 of the top 10 under "subtitle generator" say on-device or offline in their text (VEED Lite, VDIT, Teleprompter.com), so offline alone is not new; the caption rules are the difference. YouTube creators are an Investor subject (2026-10-22 18:51).

**C3 Cards and sign-up sheets to Contacts, offline.** Photograph a business card or a paper sign-up sheet; RecognizeDocumentsRequest returns rows and detected emails, phones and URLs; Foundation Models (`@Generable` Contact) assigns name, title and company where the device allows, a rule parser elsewhere; one contact per row; nothing uploaded. Difference: whole sign-up sheet to contacts, no server. Paid comparator: CamCard $49.99/yr (2026-10-24). Depth beyond the call: thin; Apple's own session 272 demo is this sign-up sheet, so the app is close to sample code plus a review screen. Limit: field naming better on Apple Intelligence devices. Gate 1: Live Text offers single phone or email actions, not a parsed contact from a card, so a pass on the plain read.

**C4 Table photo to spreadsheet.** Photograph a printed table; RecognizeDocumentsRequest gives cells grouped into rows; the app repairs merged cells and multi-page tables, types columns (dates, numbers, units), and exports CSV or Numbers. Difference: printed table to typed CSV, offline. Paid comparators on the shelf: TurboScan Pro $12.99 up front (297k ratings, makes PDFs, not tables), FineReader (19k ratings, "excel" and "offline" in its text). Depth beyond the call: merged cells, page stitching and column typing are real work and testable on code-drawn tables. Limit: none beyond iOS 26. Gate 1: FineReader may already do the stated job on device; not opened, open point.

## 5. Open points for the graders and chair

- Gate 1 for C2 (CapCut free SRT export) and C4 (FineReader on device); Notes against C1 is the main threat.
- Crowding top-25 counts under the narrow phrases ("lecture recorder", "srt generator", "table to excel").
- Speech fixtures: no neural TTS (Commandment 9); licensed human recordings need Investor approval. This blocks honest tests for C1 and C2 until settled.
- Whether SpeechTranscriber and Foundation Models run on the iOS Simulator (H142 found the docs silent): a one-hour probe on the Mac mini settles it.
