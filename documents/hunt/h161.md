## What was run

1. GitHub issue search (REST `search/issues`, read-only, one call at a time with 3 s gaps; the search limit showed 30 of 30 left before the first call), titles only, `is:issue`, open and closed, sorted by reactions, top 100 per phrase: "iOS app" (51,918 hits), "iPhone app" (5,540), "mobile app" (40,069), then "iOS client", "iOS version", "native app". 582 unique issues; 368 sit on repos with 5,000 or more stars. Raw: `H161-raw/search_*.json`; repo facts (stars, licence SPDX id, homepage) from `gh api repos/{owner}/{repo}` in `H161-raw/repos.json`; ranked list `H161-raw/ranked.json`, `ranked_all.txt`. Tool: `tools/issue_asks.py` (new, reusable for the next G23 pass).
2. Hand-sort of the ranked list: most high-reaction issues are framework bugs (Flutter, React Native) or requests to a company's own app (Claude Code, Codex, Telegram, Signal). Those are not asks for a client someone else could build, and are set aside. The asks that are "please make an iOS/mobile app for this open tool" are in the table below.
3. Maintainer answers: every comment by an OWNER, MEMBER or COLLABORATOR on the 14 candidate issues (`H161-raw/maintainers.txt`).
4. Documented API: the repo README checked for an API reference (`H161-raw/readme_*.md`).
5. iOS <span title="The App Store results a buyer sees for a search phrase. The top 10 is the first ten, dated the day we read them.">top 10</span> on the tool's name and on its job, US store, 2026-10-24 16:51-16:55 ET, via `tools/appsearch.py` (store_http.py) with LIMIT=10: `H161-raw/top10_a.txt`, `top10_b.txt`. Every query in both batches returned rows, so no empty result is unchecked.

## What the lane shows (generator level)

- Counts are small. The largest open-tool ask is 131 reactions (Notable, seven years open). Of the 15 asks above, 11 are under 80. A reaction is a counted user, but 50-130 counted users over years is a few hundred buyers at most, and many are on Android.
- The H41 lesson holds strongly. Where a tool has an API and a technical crowd, builders ship clients within weeks: 7 OpenCode-named apps (first 26 days after #6536), HermesPilot 17 days after #11911 among up to 6 Hermes-named apps, 3 Open WebUI clients, 2 Linkwarden clients plus the official one. Builders were counted, as the spec asks; they fill these shelves.
- Paid clients do sell, at a ceiling. Conduit ($3.99, 90 ratings in 14 months) is the clearest paid client for an open tool here. Most others are free.
- Licence matters less than it seemed. A client talks to the tool's API and ships none of its code, so AGPL or "NOASSERTION" on the server does not bind the client. The tool's name is a mark in every row: a client says "for NocoDB", never "NocoDB".
- The empty shelves are the slow tools: NocoDB (65k stars, documented REST API, no iOS client in the top 10) and Focalboard (no client, maintainer silent, slowing project).

## Open

- NocoDB's licence text (NOASSERTION in the API) and its trademark terms were not read; read the terms before any fetch of nocodb.com pages (G2a).
- The search reads only the top 100 per phrase over all of GitHub; asks with few reactions on mid-sized repos are not reached. A second pass with `repo:` qualifiers on a 5,000-star repo list would widen it; I judge it unlikely to change the shape (counts small, builders fast).
- Graders: two <span title="A second worker who grades the idea without seeing the first worker&#x27;s grades, and records them first.">blind</span> lines and a <span title="The worker who reads both graders and decides.
