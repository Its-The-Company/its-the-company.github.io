Written and committed before any new read for the pitch. Subject: H148 C1 "Your place's almanac", chair ruling B (research/hunt/rulings-v2.md, 'H148 chair (Ines, 15:27): C1', b59f1d3c). The chair ruling named four reads before the pitch; this file says in advance what each read can do to the letter, so the letter is not bent after the evidence lands. The <span title="Our grade scale, nine steps: D, D+, C-, C, C+, B-, B, B+, A. An idea&#x27;s ladder is the letter it reaches before any cap.">ladder</span> is <span title="Our written method for finding and grading ideas, revised for issue #41.">method v2</span> (<span title="How different the idea is from what the App Store already sells.">Novelty</span> B- with <span title="How much room the idea has to grow. Nothing here can raise the <span title="The final letter, after the caps.">Overall</span> above B; the board line says Gate 1 and Novelty can only fall.

## Read 1: The Farm Almanac store description (Gate 1, Novelty)

The Farm Almanac is the top on-job row under "farmers almanac" (1 rating, released 2026-05-07). Its description is read through tools/store_http.py (Lookup API, US store).

- **K1-1 (Gate 1 fail, cap C+).** If the description says the app computes, for a location the user sets, three or more of these five tables for the year ahead: sun and twilight times, moon phase and rise/set, tide predictions, frost or growing-season dates, meteor showers, then an app already makes a per-place yearly almanac in the same form. Gate 1 fails, Overall capped at C+, and the pitch is posted with that letter in its title and card.
- **K1-2 (Novelty to C+, Overall B-).** If the description claims the central difference of C1 in words, a yearly almanac or calendar for "your location" or "your area" or "your zip code" with two of the five tables, the stated difference is no longer unclaimed on the <span title="The App Store results a buyer sees for a search phrase. The top 10 is the first ten, dated the day we read them.">shelf</span>. Novelty falls to C+; the ladder with Depth B gives B-.
- **No move.** Articles, folk weather lore, a long-range forecast, a general national almanac, or one table alone (each table is already a free app; that is priced in the B- Novelty).

## Read 2: Calendarium store description (Gate 1, Novelty, Day 30 <span title="One numbered piece of research into one area of ideas. Each lane has its own file.">lane</span>)

Calendarium (id 482136387, saved as "Calendarium - About this Day" in research/hunt/H148-raw/shelf/) was counted by the chair in the Day 30 lane as a per-place sky app at 13.8-41.5 downloads a day.

- **K2-1 and K2-2.** The same tests as K1-1 and K1-2, same moves.
- **K2-3 (Day 30 lane shrinks).** If the description shows Calendarium does not compute sun or moon for a place the user sets (an "on this day" history app, say), its row leaves the Day 30 lane. The lane becomes 68.0-204.0 a day (Fishing Times Calendar 22.7-68.2, Deluxe Moon Pro 44.9-134.8, Eden's Almanac 0.3-1.0), 544-1,632 downloads in the 8-day window (Day 22 to Day 30); 36 sales at 30% is 2.2-6.6% of it. On the scale the chair ruling applied (C at 1.8-5.5%, D when the share needed is one in four at the midpoint or the whole window at the worst end), that stays C, so the <span title="When the Day 30 estimate is D, it holds the idea below B.">Day 30 cap</span> does not bind. If it also shows that Fishing Times Calendar or Deluxe Moon Pro is not per-place, those rows leave too, and the share is recomputed by the same arithmetic; D needs the window under 144 downloads, which only an empty lane gives.
- **No move** if Calendarium shows sun and moon for a set place and a daily page: that is the base of the job, already counted.

## Read 3: NOAA tide harmonic constituents, licence and offline use

Sites: tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov (CO-OPS). Terms and disclaimer pages are read before any data or API page (<span title="The starting recipe a lane uses to look for ideas. G2, for example, starts from public datasets.">G2a</span>). The product needs the harmonic constituents (amplitude and phase per constituent per station) so tides can be computed on the phone for the year without a network call.

- **K3-1 (Depth to B-, Overall B-).** If the constituents are not published for download (only pre-computed predictions through a live API), or the terms forbid storing or redistributing them in an app, then version 1 cannot compute tides offline. The tide table leaves version 1, Depth falls from B to B- (the harmonic engine was one of the four depth items), the ladder gives no lift, and the Overall is B-.
- **Condition, not a kill.** A required attribution line, a "not for navigation" disclaimer, or a rate limit on the one fetch per station: the pitch carries the condition in section 6 and the mockup carries the disclaimer.
- **Scope note, not a kill.** NOAA stations cover the United States and its territories. Tides for other coasts (UKHO, CHS, BSH) are sold under Crown or Crown-like copyright; the pitch says tide tables are US-only in version 1. The same is true of the frost normals (NCEI 1991-2020). This narrows the buyer and is listed under weaknesses; it was already in the chair's Day 30 lane (US store rows).

## Read 4: "almanac" as a name word under App Store Guideline 2.3.7

Guideline 2.3.7 (public text at developer.apple.com) bars other apps' names and trademarks from the name, subtitle and keywords.

- **K4-1 (names only, no grade move).** "Farmers' Almanac" and "The Old Farmer's Almanac" are publishers' marks (Idris, in the <span title="Can a buyer find it? Yes if the phrase is a store search suggestion or a top-10 app under it has over 100 ratings.">Pull</span> cell); no candidate name carries "farmer", "farmers" or "old farmer's". "The Almanac" is the exact name of an existing app (the Voodoo puzzle game, 7,147 ratings); no candidate name is exactly "The Almanac" or "Almanac" alone.
- **Rule for the table.** "Almanac" is a dictionary word for a yearly book of tables; the guideline's text is read for any clause that bars generic words, and if none, the word may stand inside a longer name. A USPTO search for a live mark on the word alone cannot be run: issue #46 is open on the blocked USPTO search, and the table says so. Names are proposals under <span title="Commandment 51: the Investors approve every visual before it ships.">Commandment 51</span> either way.
- **Table rule.** Each candidate name is searched in a paired appsearch run (two phrases in the same command, so an empty answer counts only if its neighbour answered in the same minute, memory lesson); a candidate whose exact name is an existing app's name is dropped. Domains are checked by DNS lookup (whois for .app does not answer from this machine). Names never move the letter.

## Rule that binds them all

- Each read moves at most the cells named above; no read lifts any cell. The letter after the reads is the ladder on the cells as they stand after all four, and it goes in the pitch title, the pitch card and the issue title, with the firing rule named. If two rules fire, both moves apply before the ladder (the lesson from H150: caps are ceilings, moves are moves).
- Applied section below is written after the reads, with each rule's outcome on its own line; a read that could not be completed (block, bot check, terms forbidding scripts) is a <span title="A figure we could not read because the source&#x27;s terms or robots file do not let our software read it. It never lowers a grade.">G2 gap</span>, written as "not read", and the rule it serves stays open rather than passed.

## Correction, CO-OPS licence line (Ines, 2026-10-24 18:07 ET)

- Read 3 above, in my Applied section of 16:59, says none of the three CO-OPS pages read "has a licence line at all". That is wrong. Idris's H183 (f7cbdd97) read the CO-OPS disclaimers page, and this lane's own saved copy (`H169-raw/noaa-disclaimers.html`) says the same thing under "Use of Data and Products": "The information on government servers are in the public domain, unless specifically annotated otherwise, and may be used freely by the public." The same page adds: "This information shall not be modified in content and then presented as official government material"; NOS provides the data "as is"; "NOS requests that attribution be given whenever NOS material is reproduced and re-disseminated"; and, under 17 U.S.C. 403, a notice identifying the government material is encouraged for compilations. I missed the sentence. I had the page and did not read it to the end.
- What changes: the source. The tide constituents now rest on CO-OPS's own page, not on the NWS disclaimer, which belongs to another office. The conditions are nearly the ones already carried: nothing of ours is presented as official, the source is credited on every tide page, and the "as is" and not-for-navigation line stays. One condition is added: the edition's copyright line names the NOAA material as U.S. government work (17 U.S.C. 403).
- What does not change: K3-1 was not met before and is not met now (the constituents are downloadable and may be stored). No rule fires, and the letter stays B. The frost normals are NCEI's, not CO-OPS's, so the frost wording in 'Applied, frost read' stands: we found no NCEI licence page.
