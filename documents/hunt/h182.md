## Table 1: Vision text recognition on scanned Irish print (pre-1948 spelling, all pages)

**These are library scans, not phone photographs.** They have flat pages, even light and 2,300 to 3,100 px widths. A phone photo of a book adds curl, skew, blur and shadow. **Treat these numbers as an upper bound for the camera case.** All pages are from 1899 to 1923, so all are pre-1948 (pre-Caighdeán) spelling. No modern-spelling Roman-type page is in the sample, because none is public domain in the US.

Setup: `VNRecognizeTextRequest`, `.accurate`, revision 3, macOS 27. Vision lists no Irish among its 33 recognition languages. I ran three configs. In the first two, the language hint `es-ES` (which has á é í ó ú) or `en-US`, with language correction off, gave **identical text on all 12 pages**: the hint changes nothing. The third config was automatic language with correction on. The truth is my transcription by eye of each zoomed crop (Idris, an AI worker, not a human), written before scoring. Gaelic type is written in Roman letters with the lenition dot dropped, and the scorer also drops any dot above in the OCR. Comparison is case-insensitive.

What the counts show, without a verdict:
- On Roman type, Vision reads Irish nearly character-perfect. Both missed fadas were dropped to the bare vowel (`fíor` read as `fior`), with no false fadas. The fada denominator is small (26), because two of the three Roman books are classical texts printed with few accents.
- On Gaelic type, the errors are systematic, not noise. Gaelic r is read as `p`, s as `r`, g as `5`, d as `o`, and the Tironian ⁊ as `7` (counted correct). A Gaelic-type reader would need its own recognizer or a post-correction map; Vision alone does not give usable text.
- **Rows are short of the board's 10 pages.** One page I first filed as Roman (GnomharthaNaNAspol) turned out to be Gaelic uncial when I looked, and one (postsheanchas) is mixed. I added two more Roman pages from books already used (12 scored, 5 Roman from 3 books). Page type was first guessed from the OCR output, then confirmed by eye on every crop. The mixed page has 174 characters, under 200.
- Transcriber risk: on Gaelic type, r/s glyph reads are mine. One fada is uncertain (`Ní`, scaluie n31, noted in `truth.json`).

## Table 2: coverage of de-mutated tokens by BuNaMo headwords

Input: 2,000 tokens from 31 Irish Wikipedia articles. The articles were drawn from 64,484 (namespace 0, no redirects), shuffled with seed 182, taking up to 100 prose tokens per article. BuNaMo has 42,843 files: 41,395 distinct lowercased headwords and 171,307 distinct listed forms. They cover nouns 30,708, adjectives 8,737, verbs 3,359, prepositions 16, possessives 7 and noun phrases 5. Rule (fixed, no lexicon lookup): lowercase prefix before a capital, then `t-`/`n-`/`h-` before a vowel, then eclipsis (bhf mb gc nd ng bp dt), `ts`+vowel/l/n/r, `h`+vowel, and lenition (consonant + h). An elided `d'`/`b'`/`m'` is split off at tokenization.

The most frequent unmatched tokens (headword measure): `an` 94, `agus` 64, `na` 44, `is` 36, `sa` 26, `é` 26, `go` 26, `uí` 14, `ba` 13, `nó` 12, `ina` 11, `mar` 10, `tá` 9, `ann` 9, `atá` 9, `in` 8, `eile` 8, `ceallaigh` 7. Most are articles, conjunctions, the copula, pronouns and particles, which BuNaMo does not set out to cover. Next come inflected forms (`tá`, `daoine`, `rugadh`, `ceoil`) and names (`ceallaigh`, `kelly`, `niall`). The full list is in `coverage_summary.json`. Known rule faults in the sample: the English word `the` became `te` (counted as a match), and `ú` appears three times, left over from ordinals like `13ú` after digits were dropped. The per-token log is `H182-raw/coverage_tokens.tsv`.

## Reproduce

```
cd research/hunt/H182
python3 -I fetch_pages.py ../H182-raw/pages <id[@k][:n]> ...        # serial, IA
swiftc -O ocr.swift -o /tmp/h182ocr && /tmp/h182ocr OUT.json es-ES 0 <images>   # also en-US 0, auto 1
python3 -I crop.py ../H182-raw/pages ../H182-raw/ocr <crop_dir>      # crops + region OCR lines
python3 -I score_ocr.py ../H182-raw                                  # Table 1
python3 -I coverage.py <bunamo.zip> <gawiki dump .bz2> ../H182-raw   # Table 2
```
Downloads (page images, the BuNaMo zip, the dump) stay local and uncommitted, in `H182-raw/pages`, `dl_bunamo` and `dl_wiki`.
