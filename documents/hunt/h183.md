## 1. Terms read first (<span title="The starting recipe a lane uses to look for ideas. G2, for example, starts from public datasets.">G2a</span>, Commandment 6)

Saved in `H183-raw/terms/` (fetched with `tools/fetch_cite.py`, list `H183-raw/terms.txt`).

- **CO-OPS disclaimers page** (`tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov/disclaimers.html`), section "Use of Data and Products": "The information on government servers are in the public domain, unless specifically annotated otherwise, and may be used freely by the public." Also: "This information shall not be modified in content and then presented as official government material." "NOS is providing these data 'as is'." "NOS requests that attribution be given whenever NOS material is reproduced and re-disseminated." 17 U.S.C. 403 notice is "encouraged" for compilations.
- **Correction to an earlier read.** H169 (Read 3) and <span title="A memo that proposes one idea to the Investors as the product. Only a B or better becomes one.">PITCH</span>-13 section 6 say the CO-OPS pages read "carry no licence line" and lean on the NWS disclaimer instead. - **Rate guidance** (data API page, "API Throttling"): CO-OPS throttles heavy single users and recommends "sleep statements" between calls and downloading only what is needed. It also asks callers to send an `application=` identifier. Size limits (web services page): 6-minute data one month per request, high/low one year.
- **What I did:** 26 API calls in total (2 MDAPI station lists, 6 harcon, 6 high/low, 12 six-minute), one at a time, 3 s apart, `application=ItsTheCompany_research`, a company User-Agent, passive GETs only (`H183-raw/bin/get.py`). No sign-in, no contact.

## 2. Method

- Constituents: MDAPI `stations/<id>/harcon.json?units=english` (amplitude in feet about MSL, `phase_GMT`). 31-34 non-zero constituents per station out of 37 listed.
- Engine: `H183-raw/bin/tide.py`, about 250 lines of Python with numpy only, no tide library (nothing installed, nothing for log/software.md). Mean lunar and solar elements from Meeus; equilibrium arguments, node factors f and corrections u from Schureman (SP 98), 37 NOAA constituents with shallow-water compounds built from M2 and K1. h(t) = sum f H cos(V + u - G).
- Self-test (`tide.py selftest`): the speed implied by every constituent's V matches NOAA's published speed for all 37 constituents at all 6 stations (tolerance 0.0072 deg/h); f(M2), f(K1), f(O1) at node 0 and 180 match Schureman's extremes (0.963/1.038, 1.113/0.882, 1.183/0.806). The first version of the test failed: a one-day step hides the 15 deg/h of Schureman's T. Fixed to a one-hour step mod 360.
- Truth: CO-OPS's own predictions from the data API, datum MSL, GMT, feet: high/low (`interval=hilo`, 2026-11-12..2026-12-31) and the 6-minute curve over the same days (12,000 points per station).
- Matching: our extrema from a 1-minute curve, refined by a parabola; each CO-OPS high or low is paired with our nearest extremum of the same type within 3 hours. CO-OPS publishes times to the minute and heights to 0.001 ft, so about 0.5 min is the floor.
- Nodal factors: frozen at mid-year, 2026-07-02 12:00 UT (the configuration reported below). Computing them per instant instead is shown in section 4.
- **Disclosure: one convention was chosen after seeing the comparison.** My first M1 term (V constant -90, u = xi - nu + Q) left a 0.06 ft RMS residual at Seattle, and fitting M1 alone to that residual left 0.0016 ft (`H183-raw/seattle-residual.txt`). I tried 20 M1 conventions (4 constants x 5 u forms) against the same five stations (`H183-raw/m1-convention.txt`) and kept the one that was best at every station (constant 0, u = xi - nu - Q). Because that is a choice fitted to the targets, I added **San Francisco (9414290) as a <span title="What keeps an idea below the top: the cap that applies to it now.">hold</span>-out** after the choice and before running it. It is marked below.

The two Galveston events over 10 minutes have height errors of 0.001 and 0.003 ft: at a slack, near-flat stand a 0.001 ft difference moves the turning point by many minutes.

**Galveston's 16 extra extrema.** In the window our curve turns 160 times, and CO-OPS lists 144. The 16 extra are small wiggles on flat stands that CO-OPS does not publish. I did not find CO-OPS's rule for dropping them (not on the pages read). An engine that prints every turning point would print 16 tides at Galveston that NOAA's tables do not have. This is a count, not a fix.

**Seattle's residual.** 0.014 ft RMS remains, and it still projects onto M1 (the largest M1 of the six, 0.079 ft). The M1 convention is close but not exact.

## 5. Subordinate stations (`H183-raw/subord-counts.txt`, `bin/subord.py`)

From MDAPI `stations.json?type=tidepredictions` (one call) and the same list with `expand=tidepredoffsets` (one call), read 17:56 ET, against H169's 1,373-station harcon list (read 16:54 ET):

- CO-OPS publishes tide predictions for **3,502 stations: 1,260 reference (R), 2,242 subordinate (S)**.
- Of the **1,373 harcon stations: 1,256 are R, 42 are S** (they have constituents but CO-OPS predicts them from offsets), and **75 are not in the tide-prediction list at all**. About 30 of the 1,373 are outside the US and its territories (H169's state counts).
- 4 R stations are not in H169's harcon list.
- **Offsets are served, in bulk, for all 2,242 S stations**: all four values (height and time offsets for high and for low) are non-null for every one. Height adjustment types: R (ratio) 1,974, F (fixed) 268. Every S station names a reference; 2,241 reference an R station that is in the harcon list. The one exception is Malakal Harbor (1841275), whose reference is itself. 109 distinct reference stations carry all 2,242.
- So an engine covering the full CO-OPS prediction list needs the 1,256 + 4 harmonic computations plus an offset step for 2,242 points. The 75 harcon stations that CO-OPS does not predict have no CO-OPS prediction to check against.
- Not done here: computing a subordinate station's highs and lows from its reference and comparing them with CO-OPS's subordinate predictions. Offsets act on high and low events only, so the app would show no smooth curve at an S station.

## 6. Facts the stress test can use (no rule applied)

- Engine size: one file of about 250 lines plus a 37-row table. It matched CO-OPS to within 0.6 minutes and 0.01 ft at Boston only after two conventions were found (frozen node factors, M1). Both are traps a careful engineer hits in a day, not a week. The Swift port is untested.
- PITCH-13's v1 slice test (10 stations, 30 days, within 10 minutes and 0.3 ft) is stated in counts here: 6 stations, 50 days. Events over 10 minutes: 0 at 5 stations, 2 of 144 at Galveston. Events over 0.3 ft: 0 at all 6. Plus 16 unpublished extra turning points at Galveston.
- Coverage: harmonics alone reach 1,260 of 3,502 CO-OPS prediction stations (36 %). Offsets reach the other 2,242, and the offset data is served in the same bulk call.
