## What was read, and how

- **Store reads.** iTunes Search API, `entity=software&limit=50`, with `&country=gb` or `&country=ie` only, through `tools/store_http.py` (shared rate limit, cache, back-off). No apps.apple.com or support.apple.com page was read (G2a ruling, `method-v2.md` section 'G2a: Apple's sites'; `store_http.py` refuses those hosts before any call). Command: `cd research/hunt/H189-raw/bin && python3 pull.py`. Raw results: `H189-raw/store/<cc>_<phrase>.json` (each <span title="What keeps an idea below the top: the cap that applies to it now.">holds</span> its URL and read time). All 8 reads answered; none refused.
- **Ratings, release date, price** are the storefront's own: Search and Lookup with a country return that store's `userRatingCount`, `releaseDate` and local price (HeyRua: 756 US in `H163-raw/sizing.md`, 466 IE, 242 GB).
- **Lane rule A** is H179's (`H179-raw/sib_lane.py`), applied word for word by `H189-raw/bin/lane.py`: the union of the <span title="The App Store results a buyer sees for a search phrase. The top 10 is the first ten, dated the day we read them.">top 10</span> under the language's two phrases, kept if the app name carries the language's name; blas. (6759219241) out of every per-language list; 'baby' names out; 'Irish' names out outside Irish. Output with every id: `H189-raw/lane.txt`.
- **Lane rule B** is rule A minus apps whose `primaryGenreName` is News. I wrote it after reading the IE table, where rule A admits three Irish newspaper apps (Irish Independent News 335158729, Irish Independent ePapers 1101239262, Irish Newspapers 1447919420) because their names carry 'Irish'. It is post hoc, so both are shown and I pick neither. My first draft of B (keep only Education and Reference) also dropped on-language course apps filed under Travel or Games; I replaced it before any sizing ran (`lane.py` docstring). B differs from A only in the IE Irish lane.
- **Phrases differ from H179's in one place:** H179's US Gaelic lane used 'gaelic grammar' + 'learn scottish gaelic'; the board line asks for 'scottish gaelic' + 'gaelic grammar'. So the US+GB Gaelic sum below adds two lanes drawn from different phrase pairs.
- **Lanes:** `tools/sizing.py --country <cc> --asof 2026-10-24 <ids from lane.txt>` at its defaults (1%-3% of downloaders rate, G9), Odalys's `--country` (7f647378). **<span title="Our arithmetic estimate of what the idea would earn by day 30 (the $200 deadline) or day 90. It never raises a grade.">Day 30</span>:** `tools/window_share.py --price P --lane-low L --lane-high H --launch 22 --days 30 90 --paid` (G6), Day 22 launch, so an 8-day window. Both run by `bash H189-raw/bin/size.sh` into `H189-raw/sizing/*.md` and `H189-raw/window_share.txt`; ids are read from `lane.txt`, never typed.
- **Prices run:** $2.99 for every lane (the board line). Also the row's own price where it differs: $7.99 for Gaelic (H179 C2's G12 price) and $4.99 for Irish (<span title="A memo that proposes one idea to the Investors as the product. Only a B or better becomes one.">PITCH</span>-14's price).
- **Storefront sums:** one app sells in every storefront and ratings are counted per storefront, so lanes add without double-counting a rating. `python3 H189-raw/bin/combined.py` reads each total from its file (US: `H179-raw/sizing/welsh.md`, `scottish_gaelic.md`, `irish__parent_reference_.md`; `H163-raw/sizing.md` HeyRua row) and writes `H189-raw/combined.txt`. Midpoints (G7 reads Day 30 at the midpoint): `python3 H189-raw/bin/mid.py > H189-raw/midpoints.txt`.

## Limits and open points

- **Proceeds abroad are not $2.99.** GB and IE prices are £2.99 and €2.99 tiers that include VAT, and Apple pays proceeds on the price net of VAT. `window_share.py` has no currency or VAT option, and I read no exchange rate or VAT table, so the shares above use $2.99 (or $4.99, $7.99) gross as if it were a US sale. The true GB and IE shares are higher by the VAT and the exchange rate; not measured.
- **The ratings share (1%-3%) is US-calibrated** (G9) and applied unchanged to GB and IE.
- **Rule B is post hoc** (above). It changes only the IE Irish lane.
- **H179 C2's second lift**, a commercial-use Gaelic lexicon, is not read here; this lane counts the store only.
- **The board line says six tables.** It names six phrases; Irish is read in two storefronts, so there are eight phrase tables below, one per language, storefront and phrase.
- **Not read:** IE for Welsh and Gaelic; store hints (`store_hints` refused or slow under H171 ruling b); any app's description.
