**Search.** One Search call per phrase: `entity=macSoftware`, `country=us`, `limit=25`, through store_http.py. Top 25 = the API's order. An empty or refused answer is recorded as unread and retried once at the end; no phrase is replaced.

**Keep rule K (all must <span title="What keeps an idea below the top: the cap that applies to it now.">hold</span>).**
- K1 demand: `userRatingCount` (US, lifetime) of 200 or more on the Mac record.
- K2 money: `price` above 0 (paid up front), or a free download whose own store description (Search/Lookup `description` field, a permitted read) names a price, a subscription, an in-app purchase, or a paid Pro/Premium unlock. A free app whose description names none of these is "free, money unread", counted, not kept (the IAP list lives on apps.apple.com, which G2a forbids).
- K3 subject and law: not in a Commandment 7 area (finance, medical, legal, gambling, adult, weapons and substances, children under 13, politics); not a game; not music creation, crochet or knitting.
- An app found under two phrases is one row, under the first phrase that found it.

**iPhone or iPad form check (every K row).** One Lookup of the seller (`id=<artistId>&entity=software,iPadSoftware`), then the `mobile_gap.py` sibling order: same store id (universal purchase); same seller and a shared name word; same bundle-id family and a shared name word; same brand under another seller. If the seller lookup finds nothing, one iOS Search for the app's name before its tagline (limit 25) and the same test. The result is one of: "universal", "same seller", "bundle family", "name, other seller", or "none". A "name, other seller" match is read by hand before it <span title="A test written in advance. If it fires, we drop the idea.">kills</span>. A "same seller" match whose shared word is generic (a hand read shows a different product) is read by hand and may be overturned, with the reason written.

**Kill rules, in order (first one that fires is the row's reason).**
1. An iPhone or iPad form exists (any match above that survives the hand read).
2. The job needs macOS itself: it acts on the Mac's own file system at large, other Mac apps, the menu bar, system processes, the network interface, or a terminal; iOS gives a third-party app no such access (the app sandbox), so no iPhone form of the same job can be built.
3. No computation on the buyer's own input: the product is the seller's content (a reference, a course, a word list), a viewer with no computation, or a game.
4. Out of <span title="Whether we may and can build it: our rules and banned areas, and a first version Idris can finish by Day 22. A subject off the Investors&#x27; list is noted, not dropped.">Fit</span> by Day 22 on its face (a whole IDE, a full office suite, a CAD kernel): written as "scope", with the one-line reason.

**Survivors.** For each row no rule kills: the computation on the buyer's own input (one line, from its description), the price as the store states it, ratings, release and current-version dates, and the iPhone <span title="The App Store results a buyer sees for a search phrase. The top 10 is the first ten, dated the day we read them.">top 10</span> for the same phrase (`entity=software`, limit 10, one call, dated), with how many of those 10 do the survivor's computation by name or description (a hand read, quotes kept in raw).

**<span title="One product idea inside a lane. C1 is the lane&#x27;s first idea.">Concepts</span>.** From survivors only: at most three concepts, each the survivor's computation on an iPhone with a stated difference. Each gets the method-v2 row (`| Concept | Route | Pull | Novelty | Depth | Gate 1 | Crowding | Fit | Day 30 | Day 90 | Overall | Why |`), author letters, <span title="The final letter, after the caps.">Overall</span> left to the chair. No survivor, no concept: the lane reports that G32 found none on these 30 phrases, which counts toward refill 22's falsifier.

**What would make this lane wrong.** The Mac count is US-only and lifetime, so an old app can pass K1 on ratings from 2012; release and update dates go in every row so a reader can discount it. The Search API's top 25 under a phrase is not the paid chart; a paid Mac earner that the phrase does not surface is missed, and that is the phrase list's limit, not a finding.

## 0b. Instrument failure and a proxy rule, written before any proxy count (19:27 ET)

What happened, in order. The 30 searches ran 19:18-19:20 ET (all 30 answered; 18-25 results each, 660 rows; raw `H192-raw/search/`). 98 rows passed K1 (83 distinct apps), 53 of those passed K2, and the form check (19:20-19:23 ET, `H192-raw/forms.tsv`) found every one of the 53 is itself an iPhone/iPad app: `kind` is `software` and its store id is its own iOS id ("universal"). Kill rule 1 takes all 53.

The reason is the instrument, not the market. Of the 660 rows, 385 are iOS apps offered on Apple silicon Macs and 275 are true Mac apps (`kind` `mac-software`). All 275 Mac records carry `userRatingCount` 0, including PCalc (2010) and Microsoft Excel. Lookup by id says the same (`H192-raw/probe/lookup-403504866.json`, `lookup-462058435.json`: 0 and 0). Yet PCalc's US review RSS page 1 holds 50 written reviews, 2020-02-27 to 2026-08-23 (`probe/rss-403504866-p1.json`). So the Search and Lookup APIs do not expose Mac ratings, K1 as written cannot pass any Mac-only app, and the strict result of section 0 is: **0 kept, by a measure that cannot see the thing it measures.** That result stands as written. Show me the bytes: a zero from a <span title="A second worker who grades the idea without seeing the first worker&#x27;s grades, and records them first.">blind</span> gauge is not a zero.

Proxy, added now, after seeing the zeros and before reading any proxy count (so it is pre-registered against the counts, not against the instrument failure; say so wherever it is cited):

- **K1' (demand proxy).** Page 1 of the app's US customer-review RSS (`itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=<id>/sortby=mostrecent/json`, through store_http.py, 50 entries at most) holds at least T written reviews, and its newest is dated 2025-10-24 or later (alive).
- **T, calibrated first.** Read page 1 for the iOS-kind K rows with 200-450 lifetime ratings (the band just over K1's line), and set T to the median entry count among them, <span title="A measured fact that holds a grade below a letter, however good the rest is.">capped</span> at 50. Their ratings are iOS ratings and Mac reviewers may write at another rate; the calibration rows and T are written here before any Mac row is read.
- **Who is read.** Every distinct `mac-software` row that passes K2 (paid up front, or its own description names a price, subscription, in-app purchase or paid unlock), one RSS call each. Then the section 0 form check, kill rules and survivor steps as written.

Two faults found in my own run, both fixed before any result here was written. (a) `tools/mobile_gap.py` `sibling()` treats any record with `wrapperType` `software` as iOS; a seller Lookup with `entity=software,iPadSoftware` also returns the seller's `mac-software` records, so every Mac app matched itself as "universal" (first run 19:33-19:36, all 52 "universal"; `forms_mac.log` is the rerun). My script now passes iOS `kind` only. Whether `mobile_gap.py scan` (Lookup with `entity=software` alone) can hit the same trap is unread; a lead for its owner, not a claim. (b) Three form matches rested on a generic word (PDF Reader, Notepad, "photos") and were overturned by hand, as section 0 required; five generic-word matches were confirmed from the seller's own list (OneNote, Excel, Duplicate Photos Fixer, JSON Editor Mobile, Focus Matrix).
