### The guides (fixed now; Wikipedia title in brackets)

The board's seven kinds:
1. Sky guides and star calendars: RASC Observer's Handbook [Observer's Handbook], Yearbook of Astronomy [Yearbook of Astronomy], Abrams Planetarium Sky Calendar [Abrams Planetarium], Guy Ottewell's Astronomical Calendar [Guy Ottewell]
2. Tide tables: Eldridge Tide and Pilot Book [Eldridge Tide and Pilot Book], printed tide tables in general [Tide table]
3. Birding checklists: Clements Checklist [Clements Checklist of Birds of the World], AOS Check-list [Check-list of North American Birds]
4. Garden and seed calendars: Maria Thun's sowing calendar [Maria Thun], Llewellyn's Moon Sign Book [Llewellyn Worldwide]
5. Walking and trail guides: the Appalachian Trail guidebooks [Appalachian Trail], John Brierley's Camino guides [John Brierley]
6. Road atlases: Rand McNally Road Atlas [Rand McNally Road Atlas]
7. Fishing calendars: solunar tables [Solunar theory]

Beyond the board's list (yearly paid editions of the same shape, labelled as outside it): A Guide Book of United States Coins [A Guide Book of United States Coins], Scott catalogue [Scott catalogue], European Rail Timetable [European Rail Timetable].

Skipped: any title with "almanac" or "almanack" in it (H148, H180).

### Store phrases (fixed now, one per kind; top 10 read)

'night sky calendar', 'astronomy events', 'tide tables', 'bird checklist', 'biodynamic calendar', 'planting calendar', 'appalachian trail', 'camino de santiago', 'road atlas', 'solunar', 'coin value', 'stamp catalog', 'train timetable europe'.

Top-10 codes, hand-read from trackName and description: **ed** = the app produces the buyer's own year (a year view, a printable or exportable year, or a year's list for the buyer's own place, list or route); **day** = it answers for a day or a week, not a year; **off** = another job.

### Keep and kill rules

- A guide gets a row when it is printed and sold, a new edition comes out every year (or every season), and at least 5 editions are shown by Wikipedia or loc.gov. A free publication gets a row marked "free" and makes no concept: there is no yearly money to take.
- A row becomes a concept only when (a) the buyer's own edition is a computation on the buyer's own input (place, life list, collection, route, date), and (b) its data has a source whose licence allows commercial use, or it is computed from physics (an ephemeris, tide harmonics). Killed: no licensed data source (a proprietary price list or numbering); 3 or more of the top 10 already code **ed**; a Fit kill (Commandments 6 to 8; astrology sold as cause is a claim we would not make, so it is noted and the computation must stand without it).
- At most four concepts, chosen by the rules above, not by how they grade.
- What would make this lane empty: fewer than 2 concepts survive the rules. Then the note says so, and the guides are not re-cut with new titles or phrases after seeing the counts.
- Letters appear only in the author table at the end, as method-v2.md section 2 asks, with the ladder before caps and the T1 case named.

## 5. What the lane says (Wrenna)

In this set, the yearly money pays for compiled data, not for a computation:
- The Red Book's buyers (24 million copies; a 1.2 million print run in 1965) pay for this year's prices.
- Scott's buyers pay for numbering the publisher licenses narrowly.
- The rail timetable's buyers (over 20,000 a month in 1977) pay for a compilation.

None of those can be recomputed from open data, so G33's shape breaks exactly where the money is largest.

Where the yearly edition is physics (sky, sowing days, solunar, tides), the phone shelf already computes it free for the buyer's own place:
- 3 of the 'astronomy events' top 10 code **ed**.
- 4 of the 'planting calendar' top 10 code **ed**.

So the paper survives there for other reasons (the object, the ritual), and the Sky Calendar's 10,000-plus subscribers buy a sheet, not a computation.

The AT guidebook sits in between. Half of it is compiled (water, towns: not ours to take) and half is a per-walker computation that nobody on the AT shelf does. That is why C1 is the one row with a stated difference. Its trouble is the one this whole refill keeps meeting: about 4,000 starters a year, and an 8-day window in November.

No letters here are blind; Ines triages under T1.
