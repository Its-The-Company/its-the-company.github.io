## 0. Rules written before the first fetch (19:47 ET; first fetch after this section's commit)

Refill-23 section 2 rules bind. `python3 rules/lookup.py 7` was run at 19:44 ET, before any subject or app below was named.

**Lesson carried from H192.** A rule must be one the store's own answer can pass. H192's demand rule read Mac ratings, which the Search API returns as 0 for every Mac record. Here every count comes from fields the interfaces return for iOS apps (chart rank, Lookup `userRatingCount`, `price`, `description`; review RSS `im:rating`, `title`, `content`, `updated`). Before any keep or <span title="A test written in advance. If it fires, we drop the idea.">kill</span> is applied, a probe on the first chart answer checks that each of these fields is present and non-empty on iOS records; if one is absent everywhere, that is written as an instrument failure (as H192 section 0b did) before any rule that uses it is counted.

**Hosts and terms (G2, G2a).** One host only: itunes.apple.com (chart RSS, Lookup, Search, customer-review RSS), every call through `tools/store_http.py`. G2a item 2 (<span title="Our written method for finding and grading ideas, revised for issue #41.">method-v2</span>.md) rules these program interfaces permitted at their documented rate. Its robots.txt is read in a command of its own before the first data fetch and quoted in section 1; if it disallows `/rss/`, `/lookup` or `/search` for all agents, that read stops and is a <span title="A figure we could not read because the source&#x27;s terms or robots file do not let our software read it. It never lowers a grade.">G2 gap</span> (recorded, not read). Apple's RSS feed builder (rss.marketingtools.apple.com / rss.applemarketingtools.com) is not used: per H133 it serves no per-genre top-grossing feed, so its terms are not needed for this lane; its page is not fetched. No apps.apple.com, support.apple.com or developer.apple.com HTML (G2a); in-app purchase prices have no permitted route, so a subscription price comes only from a description or a review's own words, and otherwise is a G2 gap.

**Universe (fixed now).** US top-grossing chart, legacy RSS `itunes.apple.com/us/rss/topgrossingapplications/limit=100/genre=G/json`, for G = 6007 Productivity, 6017 Education, 6006 Reference, 6002 Utilities; one call each, saved with fetch time. Each id is looked up (Lookup, `country=us`, 50 ids per call) for name, seller, price, ratings, genre, release date and description. An app on two charts is one row, under the first chart in that order.

**Subject code (by hand, from name and description, before any review is read; written to H197-raw/subject.tsv).** In: the app's main job falls under an Investor subject: science or maths (incl. calculators, graphing, homework maths for students 13+), language (learning, translation, dictionaries, writing and grammar), computer science (code, terminal, developer tools), automation or organisation (tasks, calendars, files, documents and scanning, PDF tools, shortcuts), notetaking (notes, recording-to-notes, transcription), walking, YouTube, data visualisation (charts, dashboards, spreadsheets), and movies, TV or music as data (trackers, logs, stats; not creation). Out, each with its reason: Commandment 7 areas (finance and money, medical and health incl. fitness, sleep and mental health, legal, gambling, adult, weapons and substances, apps aimed at children under 13, politics); games; music creation, crochet, knitting; religion; jobs off the subject list (VPN and security, phone cleaners, wallpapers, fonts, keyboards and themes, dating, shopping, fax and phone numbers, printers and TV remotes, photo editing); and a general AI chat assistant whose description names no single in-subject job (an AI app built around one in-subject job, such as an AI note-taker, is coded by that job).

**Review read.** For every In app: customer-review RSS, US, `sortby=mostrecent`, pages 1 to 5 (up to 250 most recent reviews; stop early at an empty page). A hit is a review with `im:rating` 1 or 2 whose title or content matches, case-insensitive: `pric(e|es|ed|ey|ing)`, `subscri`, `lifetime`, `pay once`, `one[- ]time (purchase|payment|fee|price)`, `buy (it )?once`. Every hit is hand-read and coded before it is counted (H197-raw/hits_coded.tsv, one line per hit with a reason word):
- **Counted** when the reviewer objects to what the app costs or to paying for it by subscription: too expensive, price rise, a feature moved behind a subscription, a removed or refused lifetime or one-time option, a wish to pay once, a subscription needed for the basic job, a charge after a trial they call unclear.
- **Not counted**: the price word is incidental to another complaint (a bug, a crash, a missing feature with no cost objection), a refund or billing error with no objection to the price or model, praise, spam, another app, or text too short to tell.
Review text is data, quoted briefly (12 words at most per quote) in the note.

**Keep rule.** An In app with 10 or more counted complaints in the pages read is kept. (The board's number; fixed now.)

**For each kept app (no other app gets these reads).**
1. The job inside: one line, from its own description, naming what the paying buyer uses it for.
2. The job's phrase: two or three generic words for that job, written from the description in H197-raw/phrases.tsv before the search, no brand word.
3. Top 10: one iTunes Search, `entity=software`, `country=us`, `limit=10`, dated, saved. For each of the ten: price, ratings, does it do the job (description read by hand), and money form: **pay-once** (price above 0, or a free app whose own description names a lifetime, one-time, pay-once or no-subscription purchase), **subscription** (description names a subscription, trial or recurring plan), or **unread** (neither named).
4. Whether a pay-once app <span title="What keeps an idea below the top: the cap that applies to it now.">holds</span> the job in that <span title="The App Store results a buyer sees for a search phrase. The top 10 is the first ten, dated the day we read them.">top 10</span>: yes (named, with rank and ratings) or no.
5. The <span title="The rule that decides whether an idea gets two blind graders now, waits (park), or stops.">T1</span> answer (method-v2.md T1 v2): the input from the buyer's own entries, records or files; the output computed from it; and whether any of the ten computes that output from that input by its own description (named if so). If the job takes no buyer input (the seller's content: a course, a word list, a reference), the answer says "no input", and the row stays.

**Kill rules for <span title="One product idea inside a lane. C1 is the lane&#x27;s first idea.">concepts</span>, in order (the first that fires is the reason; the row stays in the table).**
1. Pay-once held: a top-10 app does the job, is pay-once, and has 1,000 or more US ratings.
2. No computation: T1 has no buyer input or no computed output.
3. Scope: the job cannot be built by one engineer to a polished v1 by Day 22 (the reason in one line, e.g. a server-side speech model, a licensed content library).

**Concepts.** At most three, from kept apps no kill rule fires on, each the pay-once job with a stated difference from the kept app. Each gets the method-v2 author row (`| Concept | Route | Pull | Novelty | Depth | Gate 1 | Crowding | Fit | Day 30 | Day 90 | Overall | Why |`), author letters, <span title="The final letter, after the caps.">Overall</span> left to the <span title="The worker who reads both graders and decides. The decision, a ruling, is one row in the rulings file.">chair</span>, the Why cell carrying "T1: input X; output Y; top 10 read DATE (file)". <span title="Our arithmetic estimate of what the idea would earn by day 30 (the $200 deadline) or day 90. It never raises a grade.">Day 30</span> by `tools/sizing.py --low 0.01 --high 0.03 --asof 2026-10-24` on the phrase's top 10 (ids from the saved JSON by script). The chart, subject, hit and kept-app tables carry no letters. No kept app, or every kept app killed: the lane says so, and that counts toward refill 23's falsifier.

**What would make this lane wrong.** The review RSS returns at most the 500 most recent reviews and this lane reads 250, so an app whose complaints are older or whose reviewers are many reads low; a busy app's 250 may span weeks, a quiet one's years; the review date span goes in every kept row. Price complainers are a self-selected minority: a count of complaints is evidence of resentment, not of how many would switch. Top grossing ranks revenue, not subscribers. The subject code is mine, by hand; every Out reason is written so a reader can overturn it. Two- and three-word phrases are my choice; a buyer who searches another word is under-read.

## 1. Robots read: the lane stops before its first data fetch (19:44-19:46 ET)

They are G2 gaps, recorded, not read. **No chart, review or search call was made.** The only bare-path Lookup is not matched by the letter of the file, but it needs ids, and every id this lane would look up comes from the chart; ids from another lane's saved chart file would be the same disallowed read at one remove, so none is used.

Commandment 6 (`python3 rules/lookup.py 6`, Platform terms) says: "Do not scrape where it is prohibited, ignore robots.txt, evade rate limits or bans". The G2a ruling (method-v2.md, 17:04 ET) read Apple's site terms and ruled the Search API and the RSS feeds permitted program interfaces; it did not read this robots.txt, and no file in research/, ops/ or tools/ quotes it (grep for `itunes.apple.com/robots` and `Disallow: /*/rss`, 19:45 ET). A robots disallow is not overridden by a ruling that a path is an interface; that question is the chair's, and above the chair it is the Investors' (Commandment 6 is theirs).

**Reach beyond this lane, measured by grep of tools/ (19:46 ET).** 20 tools build `itunes.apple.com/search` URLs (appsearch, ios_shelf, shelf_top10, shelf_pull, shelf_reviews, mobile_gap, sub_resent, review_mine, grossing_pain and others) and 11 build `/rss/` URLs (chart_ranks, review_dump, pay_phrases, grossing_pain, sub_resent, stale_chart and others). sizing.py and the other Lookup callers use the bare `/lookup?` form, which no rule matches by the letter. tools/terms_guard.py refuses hosts whose terms forbid scripts; it reads no robots.txt.
