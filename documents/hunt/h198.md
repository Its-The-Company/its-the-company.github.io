"Others the search finds" is read narrowly: an exam named in two or more top-10 rows of the phrases below that is not in this list is reported in section 5, not given a row.

**Phrases (22, fixed now).** For each exam word e in {jlpt, dele, tcf, delf, tef, goethe, hsk, topik, cils, ielts, toefl}: 'e practice' and 'e prep'.

**Sources, in this order; each host's terms and robots.txt read in a command of its own before its first page fetch (G2, G2a).**
1. Store: one iTunes Search per phrase through `tools/store_http.py` (`entity=software`, `country=us`, `limit=50`, cut to the first 25), saved per phrase with fetch time, fields trimmed (id, name, price, ratings, release, current version date, seller, genre, first 1,500 characters of description). Descriptions from the Search answer only (G2a item 2). No apps.apple.com, support.apple.com or developer.apple.com HTML. No Amazon, Etsy or Goodreads.
2. Exam bodies' own pages, for three facts only: the fee (one country's fee, the US where the body publishes it, else the body's home country, currency as printed), the candidate count (the body's own statistics page or annual report), and test dates per year. At most 3 pages per host. If a host's terms forbid automated access or reproduction-for-reading, that host is a <span title="A figure we could not read because the source&#x27;s terms or robots file do not let our software read it. It never lowers a grade.">G2 gap</span>: recorded, not read; no secondary page replaces a body's number in a table, though a news report may be named in prose as "secondary, not used".
3. Exam bodies' pages are copyrighted. The note records facts (a number, a date, a format) with the link, never a quoted passage beyond the few words a fact needs, never sample items. Raw saves of body pages are trimmed to the lines carrying the fact (H198-raw/bodies/), not whole pages.
4. <span title="Our arithmetic estimate of what the idea would earn by day 30 (the $200 deadline) or day 90. It never raises a grade.">Day 30</span>: `tools/sizing.py --low 0.01 --high 0.03 --asof 2026-10-24` on each <span title="One product idea inside a lane. C1 is the lane&#x27;s first idea.">concept</span>'s phrase <span title="The App Store results a buyer sees for a search phrase. The top 10 is the first ten, dated the day we read them.">top 10</span> (ids from the saved JSON by script).

**Codes per top-25 row (word lists by script, hand-checked on every row a count rests on).**
- On-job: the app prepares a candidate for this exam (named in name or description), or for the exam's language at certificate levels. Off-job otherwise (a general dictionary, another exam, a game).
- Money (description only): Paid (price above 0); Sub/IAP (names a subscription, trial, Premium, Pro, unlock or in-app purchase); Free-unread (none named; the IAP list is a G2a gap).
- Compute: the description names a computation on the candidate's own answers: a predicted score or pass/fail, a level estimate, per-section or per-skill weakness from their answers, a review schedule from their misses (spaced repetition), a plan to the exam date from their results, a timed mock with a scored result. A question bank, flashcards with no schedule, a word list, audio or a video course is not a computation. The code records which kind.
- New: released 2025-10-24 or later.

**Keep rule (a candidate exam needs all three).**
- K1 money: the fee read from a permitted body page, or a G2 gap recorded (a gap never <span title="A test written in advance. If it fires, we drop the idea.">kills</span>); and at least one paid <span title="A paid app on the shelf that sells nearly the same thing. Its price and ratings are our yardstick.">comparator</span> (Paid, or Sub/IAP with over 100 ratings) in either phrase's top 25.
- K2 computation: a <span title="The rule that decides whether an idea gets two blind graders now, waits (park), or stops.">T1</span> answer: input from the candidate's own answers (mock sections, timed answers, their misses, their exam date), an output computed from it, and the top 10 under the concept's phrase read with none of the ten computing that output from that input by its own description.
- K3 fit: no Commandment 7 area; adult and teen candidates only (13+); no exam-cheating aid (no live-exam help, no leaked or reproduced official items; every practice item would be original); not crochet, knitting or music creation.

**Kill rules, in order (the first that fires is the reason).**
1. Shelf has it: 3 or more of the concept phrase's top 10 name the same computation in their descriptions.
2. No paid comparator: neither phrase's top 25 <span title="What keeps an idea below the top: the cap that applies to it now.">holds</span> a Paid row or a Sub/IAP row with over 100 ratings (no proof a candidate pays an app for this exam).
3. Small base: the body's own count is under 50,000 candidates a year (a G2 gap or no count never fires this).
4. Clone wave (<span title="Our written method for finding and grading ideas, revised for issue #41.">method-v2</span> <span title="How many apps on the shelf already sell the same thing.">Crowding</span>, provisional): 10 or more of the top 25 are New and no New row is paid with over 100 ratings. Recorded, <span title="A measured fact that holds a grade below a letter, however good the rest is.">caps</span> at C+, does not remove the row.

**Concepts.** At most three, from kept candidates only. Each gets the method-v2 author row (`| Concept | Route | Pull | Novelty | Depth | Gate 1 | Crowding | Fit | Day 30 | Day 90 | Overall | Why |`), author letters, <span title="The final letter, after the caps.">Overall</span> left to the chair, the Why cell carrying "T1: input X; output Y; top 10 read DATE (file)". The exam, phrase and candidate tables carry no letters. No candidate kept, no concept: the lane says so.

**What would make this lane wrong.** Candidates may prepare with books and courses bought outside the App Store, so a store phrase's top 10 may measure only the app-buying share. The US store under-reads exams taken mostly in Asia or Europe (JLPT, HSK, TOPIK, DELE); a US top 10 is the store we would launch in, but the buyer count is world-wide. The exam list and phrases are mine and the board's; an exam whose candidates search by level ('n3 japanese', 'b1 german') rather than by exam name is under-read.

## 5. What this lane shows, and what is open

- **The money is real, and it is not on the store.** FEI alone counts 310,436 TCF and 513,435 DELF/DALF candidates in 2025, each paying a fee. Under the exam names, the US store shows almost no app a candidate visibly pays for: eight of eleven exams fail kill 2, and the shelves that pass hold one paid row with 0 ratings (HSK) and subscriptions with 138 to 1,057 ratings. Exam fees are proof of spending on the exam, not on an app.
- **The computation is already sold.** Score and band prediction, per-section gap to target, mock tests and spaced review are on the shelf for every exam with traffic. The one output no top 10 names (C1, a dated pace forecast against the HSK list) sits on the thinnest shelf of the three.
- **Clone wave.** Nine of 22 phrases have ten or more 2025-2026 entrants in their top 25 and none of them paid with over 100 ratings: the exam-prep shelf is where AI-built apps are landing now.
- **Others the search finds** (named in two or more top-10 rows, not in the list): EPS-TOPIK (Korea's Employment Permit System test; four 'topik' top-10 rows name it) and TCF Canada / TEF Canada (immigration versions; most 'tcf' and 'tef' rows). Both are immigration-linked; a check against Commandment 7's legal-advice line comes before any lane on them.
- **Open.** (1) Chair: does jlpt.jp's robots.txt, which names other AI agents and not us, make jlpt.jp a G2 gap (I treated it as one)? (2) Every fee and seven of nine bodies' counts are G2 gaps; a person's browser read could fill them, though no kill or keep here turns on them. (3) JLPT's kill 2 rests on description codes; Migii JLPT's and Kanji Study's IAP lists are unread (G2a). (4) C1's word-list licence is unread. (5) The US store only; JLPT, HSK and TOPIK candidates are mostly in Asia.
