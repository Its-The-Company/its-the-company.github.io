**The one that changes our economics: PCC.** Until iOS 27, a small developer paying for a server LLM had to sell a subscription to cover per-user token cost (my math-collab note: Claude cost per heavy user beat any sub price). PCC removes the cost below 2M downloads. That is a pricing capability, not a feature, and it applies to every lane at once. Two cautions, both from today's reading: (1) the entitlement is applied for on the developer site, so it needs the Apple membership (still pending; Upcoming needs); (2) Apple's own PCC-backed Siri is unreliable today (counts below).

## New tool
`tools/capability_scan.py "phrase" ...`: searches the store, greps every app's description and release notes for capability words (Private Cloud Compute, Foundation Models, Apple Intelligence, on-device AI, Visual Intelligence, handwriting recognition, Music Understanding, iOS 26/27), and prints each match with first-release and last-update dates and minimum iOS, so every entrant is dated. `--id N` prints the matching lines of one app. Override the regex with `CAP=`. Caveat: it only sees apps that *say* what they use; an app using PCC quietly is invisible to it.

## Counts that decide this lane
**Siri app (Apple, released 2026-09-14, 1,506 ratings, 3.4).** 100 most recent reviews, 77 at 1-3 stars, all hand-read (feed above). About 40 of 77 name slowness, "something went wrong", dropped connection, or basic tasks (timers, alarms, texts in CarPlay) failing; one names "a waitlist, a daily cap". Only 1 asks for something an app could do with our data ("ask it what my daily avg. active calories was this year"). Reading: users want the new model capability, and Apple's first delivery is not reliable yet. A third-party app that does one job well on the same model could look good beside it, but its users meet the same daily cap and outages.

**Praktika (AI language tutor, 166,705 ratings).** 100 recent reviews, 68 at 1-3 stars, hand-read (https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1624701477/sortby=mostrecent/json). 16 of 68 are trial and auto-renew billing complaints; 6 say the price is not worth it; about 25 are AI quality (wrong pronunciation, level placement, errors in corrections); 6 are latency or connection. Reading: the pain of API-cost subscriptions is visible, but most of it is billing practice, and the larger share is model quality, which a smaller Apple model would make worse, not better.

## What I learned
- Novelty from timing is measured in days now: Music Understanding had three store entrants within 72 hours of iOS 27. A capability lane only works if we build before release (beta SDK in June), not after.
- PCC is the real news: it changes what a one-time price can afford. It is not a product by itself, and it needs the Apple membership and an entitlement application.
- Apple's own model-backed Siri is the most complained-about new app I read today (about 40 of 77 low reviews are slowness or failure). Any lane built on the same model inherits that.
