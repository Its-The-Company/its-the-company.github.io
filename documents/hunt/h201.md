## 2. Method

Script: `research/hunt/H201-raw/bin/h201_packs.py` (rules in its docstring, ingredient patterns in its `ING` list). Run: `python3 -I h201_packs.py <unzipped dir> research/hunt/H201-raw`. Outputs: `H201-raw/packs.tsv` (the 40 rows), `H201-raw/samples.tsv` (newest 5 matched products per ingredient, for audit). The bulk download stays in /tmp, out of git.

- The CSV <span title="What keeps an idea below the top: the cap that applies to it now.">holds</span> every release since 2019: 1,999,950 rows, 442,182 distinct GTINs after stripping leading zeros. One product = one GTIN; description and category from its newest row, package size from its newest row with a non-empty `package_weight`.
- Match = description pattern AND category pattern AND no exclusion word. The patterns were written once, from a tally of which categories hold each word, before any pack-size count, and were not changed after the counts.
- Parse = `package_weight` only, not the description. The first known quantity+unit decides weight (oz), volume (fl oz) or count (each); rounded to 0.1 (count: integer).
- Distinct sizes = distinct rounded values in the dominant kind. Median and range are over those distinct values; the product median sits beside it.
- Matched minus parsed = "No size field" plus a few unparsed strings (at most 9 per row).

## 4. What the counts cannot carry

- **Precision is not 100%.** I read the 200 sample rows by eye (newest 5 per ingredient, not a random draw). 30 of 200 are not the plain ingredient: mixes (snap peas and bell peppers, brussels sprouts and carrots, couscous and spinach, cauliflower with green onions), flavoured yogurt cups (5 of 5 yogurt samples; the rule does not require "plain"), a chicken kabob, a frozen rice meal, a liquid egg product, a blue-cheese crumble filed as buttermilk, cheddar puffs. Counts and distinct sizes run high for yogurt, bell peppers, green onions, cilantro, carrots and mushrooms. A random-draw precision check is the next step if the applier needs one.
- **Multipacks and food service.** Sizes are the whole package as recorded: a 4-pack of 14.5 oz broth reads 58 oz; 5 lb shred bags, 40 lb thigh cases and 105 oz cans are in. Ranges, and some medians of distinct sizes, are pulled up by them; the top-3 column shows the retail sizes. Feta's 0.0 minimum is a sub-0.05 oz value rounded down.
- **oz for liquids.** Broth (251 of 295 parsed as oz), cream and milk are sometimes labelled "oz" where fl oz is meant; the script keeps the label's unit.
- **Eggs.** 345 of 548 matched egg products have no `package_weight`. The 203 parsed are mostly weights (24 oz is a dozen large), not counts.
- **Fresh produce is thin.** Loose produce carries no GTIN: bell peppers 16 matched, green onions 11, parsley 27, cilantro 41; chicken thighs 51.
- **Label data, not <span title="The App Store results a buyer sees for a search phrase. The top 10 is the first ten, dated the day we read them.">shelf</span> data.** No price, no store, no in-stock date; `discontinued_date` not applied. US market only in practice (99.3% of rows).
