## 0. Rules written before the first fetch (written 04:13 ET; committed alone, before any script or read)

**The fetch rule in code.** Every request goes through `H241-raw/bin/get.py` (H231's guarded fetcher with paths changed): it opens URLs only through `terms_guard.fetch`, and stops with exit 2 when (a) the host's robots.txt saved by `get.py robots HOST` disallows the path for `*`, `ClaudeBot` or `anthropic-ai`, or (b) in `page` mode, the host's verdict in `H241-raw/terms-verdicts.tsv` is not `permits` or `none`. A verdict line is written by hand only after I have read the terms text, with the clause quoted in section 1. `python3 tools/lane_fetch_check.py --lane H241` runs before the first fetch. One host per process (TermsRefused is SystemExit). A 429 or 5xx is retried once after 60 s, then written "refused", never 0.

**Hosts, terms first.**

**The sample (4 queries, seed 241, <span title="A measured fact that holds a grade below a letter, however good the rest is.">cap</span> 150).** Algolia `search_by_date`, `tags=comment`, `numericFilters=created_at_i>=1761364800` (2025-10-25 00:00 ET), `hitsPerPage=1000`, page 0 only: one call per query, no second page, nothing added after the first call.
- Q1 `would pay for an app`
- Q2 `I'd pay for a tool`
- Q3 `would happily pay`
- Q4 `take my money`

Algolia matches words, not phrases, so each hit is kept only if its unescaped text matches the phrase filter `\b(would|i'd|i would|id|i'll|happily|gladly)( happily| gladly| definitely| absolutely)? pay\b|take my money` (case-insensitive). The pool is the union of kept hits, deduplicated by objectID, sorted by objectID as an integer; the sample is `random.Random(241).sample(pool, min(150, len(pool)))`. Pool size and sample are saved before any comment is read.

**Codes (hand read, each comment once, in `H241-raw/codes.tsv` with objectID and author).**
1. **K7 first.** A comment whose pay statement is about a C7 area (tax, budgeting, investing, trading, crypto, lending; medical, health, mental health, diet as health; legal; gambling; adult; weapons, alcohol, drugs; children under 13; politics) is coded K7 before any other code and leaves the read.
2. **W**: the commenter states they themselves would pay for a tool or app that computes something from their own data (their files, logs, notes, code, photos, recordings, measurements, records).
3. **P**: a first-person pay statement for something else (a hosted service or API, content, hardware, removing ads, a feature that is not a computation on the owner's data, a person's labour).
4. **X**: not a first-person pay statement (quoted, sarcastic, about others, "nobody would pay").

**Clusters and <span title="One product idea inside a lane. C1 is the lane&#x27;s first idea.">concepts</span>.** W comments are grouped by the output they ask for, in the commenter's own output words. A cluster needs **3 or more distinct commenters** (author names). At most **4 concepts**, from the largest clusters (a tie goes to the cluster with the earliest comment). Each concept gets:
- **a paid substitute**: a product the cluster's comments or their threads name, or else the product that does the job today; priced from its own pricing page after robots and terms. A price rendered by script is written "price not in served HTML", never guessed.
- **R29-2 demand**: Hacker News through Algolia, stories and comments, 2026-07-27 to 2026-10-25, at most 3 queries in the concept's own output words, plus one Stack Exchange site named by topic. These queries and the site are written into a dated section 0b and committed before the first R29-2 call (concepts cannot exist before the sample is read; this is the one departure from R29-2's "before the first fetch", stated here). Cluster comments inside the 90 days count as asks only if they meet R29-2's ask definition on a hand read. The Stack Exchange API terms are read before its first call; if the turn budget ends first, the site is written "unread", never 0.

**What would make this lane wrong.** Page 0 of `search_by_date` is the most recent 1,000 hits, so a busy query skews to recent months; the date spread of the pool is reported. "I'd pay for" on HN is cheap talk: a stated price is not a purchase, which is why every concept needs a priced substitute and R29-2 demand. HN commenters are developers, who often build their own tool (the H41 lesson): a GitHub or open-source substitute named in the thread is recorded as a substitute and weighs against the concept. Ten turns: whatever is unread is listed under Open.
