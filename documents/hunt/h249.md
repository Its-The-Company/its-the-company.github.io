## What <span title="A memo that proposes one idea to the Investors as the product. Only a B or better becomes one.">PITCH</span>-13 names (read first)

Sunrise, sunset, civil twilight (dawn and dusk), moon phase, moonrise and moonset; also tides (from NOAA constituents), frost windows, and meteor showers scored against the moon. This rule covers the sun, twilight, moon rise/set and the four principal phases. **Tides are named in PITCH-13 and are not covered here** (H183 checked them separately). Frost and meteors are not covered either.

The run before this fix stopped on a resource-path error and counted nothing.

## Implementation

Swift package test target in H249-raw/ (Sources/H249Almanac/Almanac.swift, about 230 lines). No app, no UI, no network at test time, no third-party package. It is written from Meeus, *Astronomical Algorithms*, 2nd ed. (1998): ch. 7, 12, 13, 15 (h0 = -0.8333 for the sun, -6 for civil twilight, 0.7275 x parallax - 0.5667 for the moon), 22, 25 (sun, low accuracy), 47 (moon, leading 34 + 30 terms), 49 (phases). Delta T is fixed at 69 s. Events come from scanning altitude every 10 minutes and bisecting each crossing, so every crossing in the UT day is found.

On every event both sides compared, the largest error is 1 minute: sun, twilight and moon, at all six places. Polar day and polar night compared "no event" to "no event": civil 215, sun 235, moon 466 matching empties.

All 12 misses are one effect and none is an accuracy miss: 6 events at 23:59:30-24:00 UT. I round one of these to 24:00 (1440) on day N, and USNO lists it as 00:00 on day N+1. Each one counts as a miss on both days. 2 are sun place-days (Boston 16-17 May sunset), 2 are civil (Boston 19-20 Apr), 8 are moon (Sydney, Honolulu, Quito). The fix for the app is to bucket by day after rounding. It is not applied here, because the rule counts the code as it ran and the pass lines <span title="What keeps an idea below the top: the cap that applies to it now.">hold</span> without it.
