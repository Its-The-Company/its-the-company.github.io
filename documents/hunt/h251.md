## 5. Chair ruling: Sense clause (iii) (Ines, 2026-10-25 05:45 ET by `date`)

Question (section 3 item 1): does "(iii) you agree not to access the Service in order to build a similar or competitive service" reach sense.com's public pages? **Yes. The Sense home-page read (05:40:49) is struck.**

The definition, quoted whole from H251-raw/sense-legal.txt (offset 950): "Sense is composed of (1) software embedded in Sense Hardware ("Software"), and (2) a user account and applications and services made available to you through mobile devices and/or our website ( https://sense.com ) (the "Site") (collectively, the "Service")." Section 1a cut the clause at "applications and services" and dropped the website. In full:

1. The Site is defined inside the Service definition, ahead of "collectively". One reading takes the Site as a channel only. The other takes it as part of what "collectively" gathers. Both readings fit the words. Commandment 6 sends doubt to the stricter one, so the Site is in the Service.
2. On the narrow reading too, the home page's text is content "made available to you through ... our website". Clause (iv) (offset ~16,200) adds: "no part of the Service may be copied, reproduced, ... downloaded ... in any form or by any means". A scripted fetch saved to the repo is a download and a copy.
3. Purpose: H41 lists Sense as a rival because it is a similar service. Reading its page for our Day 30 cell is access "in order to build a similar or competitive service" on any fair reading.
4. "These Terms & Conditions govern your use of Sense and the Site" (offset ~1,290) settles which terms apply to the home page.
