## 0. Plan (committed with the labels, before the engine exists)

- **Question.** Can v1 mark mutations (word, type, trigger) in pasted Irish with only rules and word lists the company writes, no BuNaMo (ODbL) data?
- **Text.** The first 145 sentences of `H230-raw/sentences.json` (Irish Wikipedia, dump on disk since H182; text under CC BY-SA 4.0 / GFDL, read in H230 from dumps.wikimedia.org/legal.html; title and revid kept per sentence for attribution; test input only, never shipped). The <span title="A memo that proposes one idea to the Investors as the product. Only a B or better becomes one.">PITCH</span>-14-mockups/check_words.py fixtures are not used: their claims are checked against BuNaMo, and a truth set drawn from them would carry BuNaMo into the labels.
- **Truth set.** `H259-raw/bin/candidates.py` lists every token whose spelling could carry a mutation (surface patterns only: consonant+h, the eclipsis pairs in both cases, n- and nV, h-/hV, t-/tS). It is a recall aid; a mutation that spelling does not show (l, n, r, sc, sp, st, vowels under lenition) is not marked by writers either, so no hidden mark is missed. Each of the 284 candidates was labelled by me in `H259-raw/labels-compact.txt`, expanded by `bin/expand.py` into `H259-raw/labels.tsv`: 254 marked (173 lenition, 64 eclipsis, 11 h-prefix, 6 t-prefix), 30 not (native h-initial words such as chomh, chun, thiar, dhá; foreign names). Trigger is the token index of the word that causes the mutation, or TENSE for a lenited past, imperfect or conditional verb with no particle. Base form is the headword; gender for nouns only, "-" where not a noun or not known to me.
- **Rule codes** (the rule each label applies): LEN-PREP simple preposition or preposition+article (de, do, ó, ar, mar, trí, faoi, sa, den, don, ón, fán) lenites; LEN-ART-F article before a feminine noun, nominative singular; LEN-ART-MGEN article before a masculine noun, genitive singular; LEN-ART-PREP preposition+article lenites (northern and Caighdeán-allowed usage, "ar an ghrian"); LEN-POSS possessive a (his), mo, do, ina; LEN-REL direct relative a before a verb; LEN-VN a before a verbal noun; LEN-TENSE past, imperfect, conditional; LEN-ADJ adjective after a feminine singular or slender-plural noun; LEN-GEN genitive (proper names, definite genitives) after a noun; LEN-NUM aon, dhá, dá, trí to sé, an chéad; LEN-PART ní, ba, gur, Uí, Ní in surnames; LEN-PREFIX iar; LEN-IDIOM le chéile, bean chéile; ECL-I after i; ECL-ART-PREP preposition+article; ECL-GENPL na before a genitive plural; ECL-POSS a (their); ECL-REL indirect relative a, ina; ECL-PART nach, go; ECL-IDIOM ar dtús, go dtí, mar an gcéanna; H-POSS a (her); H-ART na before a vowel; H-PREP le; H-NUM séú, a hAon; T-ART-M an t- before a masculine vowel noun; T-ART-S an tS before a feminine s noun; NONE-NATIVE, NONE-FOREIGN.
- **Engine (to be written after this commit).** Python, `H259-raw/bin/engine.py`, no data file: spelling detects the mutation; company-written lists only (trigger words per mutation, a short native h-word list, a short irregular-verb list, a foreign-letter test j k q v w x y z). No network, no app target, no Xcode.
- **Pass.** Marks right on all three of word, type, trigger: right / truth marks 95% or more, and right / engine marks 95% or more; and no engine mark needs BuNaMo (true by construction: the engine opens no file but the sentences). Base form share: de-mutated spelling equals the headword. Gender share: nouns whose gender the engine states from spelling and article alone, right, over labelled nouns with a gender.
- **One run.** The first run is the result. No rule is changed after it.

## 1. Run (05:53 ET, one run, no rule changed after it)

`python3 -I H259-raw/bin/engine.py H230-raw/sentences.json H259-raw/out/marks.tsv`, then `bin/score.py`; output in `H259-raw/out/score.txt`. The engine has one `open(` (the output file); sentences come through `common.py`. No BuNaMo, no Gramadán, no network.

- **Marks right (word, type, trigger).** 250 of 254 truth marks (98.4%); 250 of 263 engine marks (95.1%). Both at or over 95%: **pass**, narrowly on the engine-marks side.
- **The 4 truth marks missed** (all trigger, none type): `tharla`, `chosain` (regular past verbs after a non-trigger word; the engine gave the previous word, the truth TENSE); `Chumann` (lenited by `de` across `agus`); `Mhic` (genitive governed two words back).
- **The 13 extra or wrong engine marks:** 8 foreign names spelled like lenition (Shelburne, Chengdu twice, Thompson, Championnat, Chamaeleonidae, Chelsea, and English `that`), which the letter test (j k q v w x y z) does not catch; the 4 above; and `tSín` (s085, "sa tSín"), a true t-prefix my candidate finder missed (its pattern had `ts` but not `tS`). That last one is a truth-set fault, not an engine fault; counted as scored (wrong) here. Counted as right it gives 251 of 255 and 251 of 263 (95.4%); the verdict is the same.
- **Base form.** De-mutated spelling equals the headword for 199 of 254 marks (78.3%). The other 55 (21.7%) are inflected (genitives such as `compáis`, `Móinéir`; plurals; verb forms such as `bhfuil`, `bheith`, `tháinig`): rules give the base form for about four in five, and the rest need an inflection table.
- **Gender.** Of 168 marked nouns with a labelled gender, the engine states 6 from spelling and article alone (an t- before a vowel; an tS), 5 right and 1 wrong (`an tséasúir`, masculine genitive, read as feminine). Rules give gender for 3.0%; 97% need a lexicon or a separate download.
- **Limits, stated.** One author for labels and engine; 145 encyclopaedic sentences, heavy on place names (`i gContae`, `sa bhuirg`), not learners' own text; the trigger field is a token index, so "why" (the rule) is not scored, only "which word".

**Result:** pass. Rules-only marking meets 95% on word, type and trigger on this set (98.4% of truth marks, 95.1% of engine marks), with no BuNaMo data; base form from rules 78.3%, gender from rules 3.0%. Per R32-4, K163-1 can be "met by design" for a v1 that marks mutations by rules and drops gender or downloads it separately; the applier records the two v1 options here and on the PITCH-14 board line. No letter moves (<span title="Can a buyer find it? Yes if the phrase is a store search suggestion or a top-10 app under it has over 100 ratings.">Pull</span> unread under #53; K163-5 open). No <span title="The worker who reads both graders and decides. The decision, a ruling, is one row in the rulings file.">rulings</span>-v2.md row.
