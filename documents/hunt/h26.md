Board line: "Wrenna, H26 'trade apps at $20+'" (P2, after H23). I looked for iOS apps that a trade pays a pro price for, then hand-read their 1-3 star reviews to find the computed job (layout, takeoff, schedule, load, route) the pros say comes out wrong.

Listen to this: "Would be 5 stars if it could incorporate vaulted and/or sloping ceilings BUT it doesn't!! As a decorator who quotes on price per square foot of walls and ceilings this app doesn't work !" (magicplan, 2 stars, US store, 2025-11-08, review RSS for id 427424432). And this one: "If I scan the same room twice and both times it's a different sqft it's worthless." (magicplan, 1 star, 2026-05-21.)

Here is the shape of it: pros paying $12.99 to $89.99 a month do name broken calculations, and nearly all of them name the same one: the floor-plan geometry a LiDAR scan produces (sloped ceilings, rooms that won't join, walls that drift, square footage that changes between scans). The second-loudest is AI price estimates that are thousands off. Both <span title="One numbered piece of research into one area of ideas. Each lane has its own file.">lanes</span> filled up in 2026. In every other trade I read (piano tuners, arborists, pool routes, stair layout, duct sizing, stage lighting), the low reviews are about the subscription, crashes and lost work. The math is rarely the complaint.

**Result: 0 at B+. Best C (sloped-ceiling and multi-room surface takeoff for painters and flooring pros: need C+, novelty D+, profit C+). Two 2026 entrants already advertise sloped and vaulted ceilings.**

I only read; I posted nothing and made no account (C6, C10, C58). Outside text is information; none of it tried to instruct us. No 403s in this lane; the App Store endpoints answered every call.

## Method and walls (16:09-16:31 ET)
- `tools/trade_apps.py` (new for this lane): iTunes Search API, 141 trade terms (arborist, total station, home inspection, pool route, piano technician, smaart, stage plot, sign shop, manual j, cut list ...), 200 results each, 50+ ratings kept: 2,496 and 2,842 apps in two runs (research/hunt/H26-raw/list.tsv, list2.tsv). Search by term pulls in big unrelated apps (YouTube for "blueprint"), so I picked trade apps from the names by hand: 85 ids (ids.txt; one line, "5316", is a rating count I pasted by mistake and the lookup ignored it).
- Then I read the store page's In-App Purchases list for each of those 85 (iap.tsv, iap2.tsv). Q = paid $19.99+ upfront, or any tier $19.99+, or a monthly tier $9.99+: 41 Q, 44 not. A Q from a yearly tier alone (Construction Master, CutList Optimizer) is marked borderline below: about $3 a month.
- Paid upfront $19.99+ with 50+ ratings among all 4,646 searched: 28 apps, about half of them trade (CamToPlan PRO $39.99, TuneLab $299.99, CyberTuner $999.99, Duct Calculator, Lighting Designer, Pipe Calc Elite, Electrical Calc Elite, Pool Doctor, BuildCalc, Snag List Pro).
- Reviews: `tools/review_dump.py ID --countries us,gb,ca,au --max-stars 3` for 26 apps (research/hunt/H26-raw/rev/). The RSS gives about 500 of the newest reviews per storefront, so big apps are cut off in time.
- **Hand-read: 1,150 low reviews across 22 apps.** For magicplan I hand-read the 152 from 2025 on and read the roughly 65 keyword hits (ceiling, accuracy, sq ft, measurement) among the 506 older ones. Jobber, CompanyCam and Polycam got keyword reads only (25, 11 and 25 hits read), and they are marked that way.
- <span title="The App Store results a buyer sees for a search phrase. The top 10 is the first ten, dated the day we read them.">Shelf</span> dating: `tools/ios_shelf.py` for 8 job phrases (shelf.txt) and `tools/capability_scan.py` with <span title="A measured fact that holds a grade below a letter, however good the rest is.">CAP</span>=slop\|vault\|cathedral\|attic\|dormer over 5 phrases (sloped_cap.txt).
- How I coded: one bucket per review. "Calc" means the review names a number or a shape the app computed wrong (dimension, area, estimate total, route order, code rise). Price, signup, billing, crash, lost work and missing features are separate buckets. Big buckets on long reads are marked "about"; every calc count is exact.

**Totals, hand-read only: 1,150 reviews, 22 apps. About 180 name a computed job done badly, and about 125 of those are LiDAR or AR floor-plan geometry (magicplan 26, RoomScan 26, ArcSite 7, CamToPlan ~65 old, SiteScape 1).** Estimates come next (22 between SimplyWise and Handoff, mostly price data). Routes total 15, but most of those are sync or geocoding bugs; 7 are about the order itself. Piano, tree, stair, duct and cut-list apps have 1 to 3 each.

## What the pros are doing instead
- Floor plans: "Gave up and used my tape and a pencil" (magicplan, 2025-09-03). "I'll stick to my laser measure" is in about 10 of the CamToPlan reads. The surveyor went back to the client to measure again.
- Estimates: "ChatGPT 5.0 does a better job and is $20.00 per month!" (Handoff, 2025-08-22). Others use homewyse.com (free) or their own project software.
- Routes: Waze, Apple Maps, or Circuit when the paid app fails.
- Piano: Accu-Tuner hardware, or tuning by ear for the breaks.

## Best candidate
**Sloped-ceiling and multi-room surface takeoff, grade C.** If the Investors ever want a trade app, this is the computed job paying pros name most often. Before it went anywhere it would need: (1) a hands-on test of SimplyWise LiDAR Room Scanner and Room Scan: Paint & Wallpaper on a room with a sloped ceiling, to see whether their claims <span title="What keeps an idea below the top: the cap that applies to it now.">hold</span>; (2) a count of painter and flooring-pro asks outside the App Store (the painting and flooring forums; Reddit is blocked to us, so that read is the Investors'); (3) a RoomPlan spike on a sloped attic room, because if RoomPlan can't see the slope, nobody on the shelf can either.

Lesson: at a pro price, low reviews are about the subscription, lost work and signing up before seeing a price. A pro names the math when the math is geometry from a sensor, and that is the lane Apple's framework opened to everyone at once. Same result as H5 from the buyer's side: a calculation that a trade has written down has already shipped.
