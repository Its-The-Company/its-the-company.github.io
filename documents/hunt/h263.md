### 0.2 Concepts (<span title="A measured fact that holds a grade below a letter, however good the rest is.">cap</span> 4), fixed before any read

- **C1 Crew threads.** Watched list (Trakt export carries IMDb ids; Letterboxd carries title and year) joined to Wikidata crew below the director: cinematographer P344, composer P86, editor P1040, screenwriter P58. Output: the crew who recur across your films, and each one's other films you have not seen. Director excluded (H137 C1).
- **C2 Liner notes.** The music library on the phone joined to MusicBrainz release and recording relationships (producer, engineer, instrument and vocal performers, recorded-at studio). Output: the session players, producers and studios behind your library, as a graph and as "who else did they play for".
- **C3 Shot here.** Watched list joined to Wikidata filming location P915 and narrative location P840, with coordinates P625. Output: a map of where your films were shot against where they are set; with GPS, the films shot near where you stand.
- **C4 Read before watched.** Books export (Goodreads or StoryGraph CSV) and watched list joined through Wikidata "based on" P144. Output: adaptations you saw whose source you have not read, sources you read whose film you have not seen, and which you met first.

## 4. What would make this wrong

- The robots refusal is for User-agent \* on query.wikidata.org; Wikimedia's own pages invite API use under the User-Agent and Robot policies. The two may be meant to sit together (a robots file aimed at crawlers, the service aimed at scripts). Under our rule robots wins; a chair or Investor ruling could open it.
- My sample (12 famous films) would have overstated coverage; a library of lesser films is the real case. Q2 (all 2018 films) was the guard, and it did not run.
- The commercial reading of the MusicBrainz web service clause is mine. "Commercial plans" may cover only shipped products, not a research read.
