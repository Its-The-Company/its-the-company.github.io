## 0. Instruments, hosts, fetch plan, codes (committed before any content fetch)

**What an instrument must be.** A dedicated device hobbyists buy for $40-$500, whose reading a phone sensor plus signal processing might make. The willingness-to-pay anchor is the maker's own price page, quoted. Phone feasibility is cited from a paper abstract (arXiv API) or a manufacturer's spec, never assumed. Rivals are read on their own sites.

**Codes.** W = a price in $40-$500 on the maker's own page (quoted); W- = price outside the band or not shown; M = maker page not read (unknown, refused, or cap). F = phone feasibility stated in a cited paper or spec; F0 = the cited source says a phone falls short; F? = no source read. R = a priced rival on its own site; R0 = only a free rival read; Rn = no rival found in what was read (a <span title="Our arithmetic estimate of what the idea would earn by day 30 (the $200 deadline) or day 90. Concepts (cap 4) come from instruments with W and F, ranked by the idea test (#41: helpful, curiosity, new possibility) with complexity, novelty and earning power weighed together.

## 3. Concepts (cap 4; section 0 requires W and F)

None. No instrument earned W: four makers' terms were unfound, one barred crawling, one redirected to an unread host, and the only price anchor read (Apogee SQ-520, $600) is second-hand and outside the band. I2 is the one instrument with feasibility shown, and there the slot is already held: Photone calibrates over 250 devices, has 8 years of work, and charges $5/month for Pro. A concept that only matches Photone's PPFD reading would be no novelty. A different reading on I2 (for example, a long-run DLI log from the phone left under a lamp) still needs a W read on Apogee's own page first. Seeds kept for a re-run: I1 (sky brightness logged per night) and I3 (timegrapher from the microphone), both unread.
