Board line: "Wrenna, H29 'wrong answers on the shelf'" (<span title="A numbered batch of planned work, with its own plan file.">refill</span> 5, P1, from #35). Started 16:57 ET, file finished 17:20 ET (times from `date`).

Listen to this: "Quarter, first scan $301. Scanned again $.37." (CoinSnap, 2 stars, US store, 2026-10-13, review RSS for id 1634551626.) And this: "I printed an assignment from my geometry class last year and the results of this app were sardining ... 4.8 stars for a 40% is kinda crazy." (Photomath, 1 star, 2026-06-17.)

Here is the shape of it. Where the computed answer is the product, half the low reviews are about money (paywalls, trials that bill on day one). The wrong-answer share clears 15% in six of the ten shelves I read. But the loudest ones (math, coins, rocks) are the shelves we should be most careful with, for reasons that are not about accuracy.

## Method

- `tools/shelf_reviews.py` (written at the start of this <span title="One numbered piece of research into one area of ideas. Each lane has its own file.">lane</span>) picks the shelf for a job phrase: the top 3 apps by rating count in the first 50 US search results, plus the 2 entrants released in 2026 with the most ratings. It dumps their 1-3 star reviews dated 2026-01-01 onward from the us, gb, ca and au storefronts (review RSS, about 500 newest per storefront). Raw files: `H29-raw/rev/<category>/<app id>.tsv`. Shelf dating: `H29-raw/shelf.txt`.
- I hand-read every review counted here, newest first, and gave each one code for its main complaint: **W** wrong result, **V** wrong care advice or diagnosis (plants only), **N** no result ("can't recognize", "couldn't find a match"), **C** crash, bug or slow, **P** price, paywall or billing, **A** ads, **L** login or lost data, **F** missing feature, **O** other. Codes are in `H29-raw/codes.txt`; `tools/hand_code.py tally H29-raw/codes.txt` gives the counts below, and `hand_code.py quote` prints the W rows with date and store for each app.
- I checked alignment after coding: I printed the W rows for every app and read them against the codes. Three strings were off (Gauth from row 36, Night Sky rows 5-6, Photomath rows 103-107), and I fixed them before tallying. An earlier draft code file from this same lane was misaligned with its rows. It is kept unused as `H29-raw/codes-draft-unverified.txt`.
- Big apps were read to a <span title="A measured fact that holds a grade below a letter, however good the rest is.">cap</span> (Gauth: newest 80 of 334; PictureThis 120 of 385; Shazam 120 of 337; Night Sky 100 of 180; AllTrails 100 of 186; Adobe Scan 100 of 159; CoinSnap 80 of 225; CoinIn 60 of 235; Rock Identifier 80 of 332; Summary 100 of 205). These are samples of the most recent reviews, not the whole year.
- **2026 entrants gave nothing to read.** Every 2026 entrant on all ten shelves returned 0 low reviews dated 2026, because they have 0-136 ratings. Vico Nature (15,831 ratings, released 2026-03-25) also returned an empty feed. So every count below comes from incumbents. The new entrants' accuracy is unmeasured.
- Price paid is what users say they paid in the reviews. Every app on these shelves is listed Free; the money is in-app.

## What the count says

1. **Wrong answers are common, but money is the bigger complaint.** P outnumbers W on 8 of 10 shelves (not math, not song). Paying users name wrong answers most clearly on coins, rocks and plants, where they paid $30-90 a year and the answer ("is it valuable", "is it real", "will it die") is the reason they paid.
2. **Inconsistency is a complaint of its own.** The same input gives a different answer on rescan: coins 11 of 37, rocks 6 of 34, plants 3 or more, Gauth 2, Shazam 1. Users read a changing answer as proof the app is guessing. A fixture bench can show this and can show it fixed; that matters for H33.
3. **Some wrong answers are not the algorithm's fault.** The star-map errors are compass and gyro drift, not astronomy. The rock and gem errors ask a photo to do a hardness or density test. A better model does not fix either. A different method does: plate-solving the camera frame, or asking for a streak or scratch test.
4. **The two loudest shelves carry the most risk.** Math solvers (49%) are bought mostly by school students. Several reviews describe 4th- to 8th-grade work, which touches the under-13 limit (C7), and the job is often answering homework. Coin valuation (26%) is an appraisal of money value. I read that as collectibles, not finance (C7), but it is close enough that I'd want an Investor read before a <span title="A memo that proposes one idea to the Investors as the product. Only a B or better becomes one.">pitch</span>.

## Hand-offs for Idris's H33 gap bench

These are the categories at 15% or more wrong answers in 100 or more reads. Each line gives the wrong answer to rebuild from code-made fixtures, the correct method to test, and my flags. The order is my view of how good a bench each makes, not a grade.

- **H33-a, coin ID consistency (26% of 140).** Wrong answer: the same coin, re-photographed, gets a different year, grade or value ($322 to $0.28). Fixture: code-drawn coin faces with a date and mint mark, rendered at several angles, lighting levels and blurs (no photographs). Method to test: date and mint-mark reading that gives the same answer across renders, plus a refusal when unsure, instead of a fresh guess each scan. Error table: share of renders with the date read the same and read right. Flag: valuation is near finance (C7). Bench only the identification part, and ask the Investors before any pitch includes prices.
- **H33-b, sky map that knows where the phone points (16% of 129; about 10 orientation cases).** Wrong answer: the overlay sits degrees off (the moon "isn't there"), because it trusts the compass. Fixture: star fields rendered by code from a public-domain catalog, with a known pointing, a 2-15 degree compass error and sensor noise. Method to test: plate-solve the camera frame against the catalog and correct the overlay. Error table: degrees off before and after. Flag: plate solving on iPhone is already shipped for telescopes (FinderCam 2020-11-20; AstroHelm 2026-04-23, 7 ratings; AstroFinder 2026-07-20; Sideris 2026-09-14). None of these is a general sky map, but the novelty is narrow. Check the catalog licence before building anything.
- **H33-c, plant care advice that doesn't contradict itself (V 14% plus W 19% of 265).** Wrong answer: "every diagnosis says overwatering", and opposite advice on two scans. Fixture: a code-made table of plant, symptom, pot and season inputs, with the advice a published care key gives. Method to test: a rule-based care key, checked against the same inputs given twice. Flag: species ID itself can't be benched with code-made fixtures. Only the advice consistency can.
- **H33-d, rock and gem "real or not" (24% of 139).** Wrong answer: glass called quartz or emerald, and a lab diamond called real. A correct method has to ask for a physical test (streak, scratch, weight in water). Fixture: code-made attribute rows (colour, lustre, hardness, specific gravity) for minerals and their glass or plastic imitations. Method to test: a test-guided key against photo-attribute-only guessing. Flag: the buyers in these reviews are often children ("my lil bro", "my child downloaded this"; C7). It's a weak bench because a photo is the users' real input.
- **H33-e, math answer verification (49% of 250).** Wrong answer: a "matched solution" from a different problem, or wrong arithmetic. Fixture: code-generated algebra and fraction problems rendered as images, plus near-duplicates that differ only in the numbers. Method to test: solve, then check the answer by substituting it back, and refuse a match whose numbers differ. Flag: **I recommend we don't pursue the product.** The buyers are students, many under 13, and it competes with Gauth (GAUTHTECH PTE. LTD., 1.5M ratings) and Google's Photomath (733k). The bench would only show that the gap is real.
- **H33-f, song recognition (16% W, 48% no match, of 120).** No bench recommended: nobody pays (1 of 120), and the gap is Apple's catalog, which we can't rebuild. Listed because it met the bar.
- **H33-g, floor plans (22% of 234, H26).** Already graded C in H26: a real need on a crowded shelf. If Idris benches it: same room, two noisy scans, square-footage spread before and after a constrained wall fit.

## Open

- Nothing to read for any 2026 entrant on these shelves. A re-run around Day 20 could catch their first low reviews.
- The samples are the newest reviews (caps above). A full-year read of CoinSnap's 225 and Gauth's 334 would firm up those two shares.
- Not read from the board's list: film recognition and subtitle makers (only song recognition and lecture transcription were pulled). If H31 wants them, the next pass should read them.
- Coin valuation and math homework both need an Investor or Commandment read before a pitch.
