Run 2026-10-24 00:54-01:01 ET (by `date`). I wrote this before opening research/hunt/grades-maren-h59.md and did not read Ines's grade files. Raw data is in research/hunt/H56-raw/.

**Result: Day 30 discovery C+. Store phrase: "atmos speaker placement". No surround or Atmos layout planner appears in the top 25 for any of the 12 asker phrasings, so the phrase is open and a new app could rank for it. The weak part is traffic, not crowding. The two layout-type apps that do rank (Speaker Positioning, #1 for "speaker placement" since 2026-09-17, and Stereotek, released 2026-08-15) have 0 ratings each. None of the 7 free web tools I could read takes a sloped ceiling (V), an open side (P) or a window/door (G). DARDT is the only one that takes A, R or E, and it is a desktop Excel file with macros.**

Listen to this: "Atmos Speaker placement in vaulted ceiling..." (2026-07-12, https://reddit.com/r/hometheater/comments/1uu3g7a/). Type those words into the App Store and the #1 result is a test-tone player (Surround Speaker Check). Then come speaker cleaners and brand remotes. Nothing on that page lays out a room.

## Method

- **Phrases.** These come from the 433 asks in research/hunt/H39-raw/as_hometheater_placement.txt (ask rows = codes not X, D, O, B in codes-constraint-1-530.txt). For each title I took the first three content words, after dropping stopwords ("help", "advice", "question", "best", "how", articles and pronouns) and brand names (Klipsch, Polk, Sonos, Dolby, etc.). I merged spelling and plural variants: centre/center, speakers/speaker, surrounds/surround, subs/sub. The script is H56-raw/phrases.py and the output is H56-raw/phrase-tally.txt (294 distinct phrasings).
- **Judgement call:** I kept "atmos". It names a format, and dropping it would remove the word an Atmos buyer types. "Dolby" was dropped.
- **Hand check.** I read every title behind "speaker placement" (34), "atmos speaker placement" (14), "placement" (10), and the seven phrases tied at 3. Each one is a plain ask. "Speakers and Placement" counts under "speaker placement".
- **Ties.** Phrases 1-11 are clear. Seven phrasings tie at 3 for 12th place. I took "5.1 speaker placement" because the three tied "5.1 ..." forms together cover 9 asks, the biggest family. I also ran the other six tied phrasings as extras, so all seven tied forms have a shelf count.
- **Shelf.** I ran `LIMIT=25 tools/appsearch.py` on the US store, one query at a time with 3 s gaps, 00:55-00:56 ET (H56-raw/store-search.txt). That gave 171 distinct apps. I pulled all 171 descriptions with `tools/aslookup.py` (H56-raw/lookup.txt) and grepped them for surround, atmos, room layout, floor plan, speaker placement and speaker position. That found 32 apps. I then hand-read the nine that could be placement tools (H56-raw/lookup-core.txt).
- **"Claims" column.** This counts results whose description claims surround, Atmos or room layout, as the board asked. Most of these are speaker cleaners, brand remotes or room-furniture planners that only use the word. The **planner** column counts results that output speaker positions for a room.

Here is the shape of it:

- **The shelf.** Across 18 searches, 3 apps do anything with speaker positions:
  - **Speaker Positioning** (id 6811791547, free with a one-time Pro, released 2026-09-17, 0 ratings) is stereo only. It uses a LiDAR scan and measured before/after tests.
  - **Stereotek** (id 6797621012, free, released 2026-08-15, 0 ratings) is a Santo Domingo integrator's showroom app. It has a room-box calculator: "fronts at 30 degrees, surrounds at 100, rears at 145, and height channels at 45 degrees of elevation calculated against your own ceiling". That is our plain-box v1, buried inside a dealer app.
  - **SpeakerAngle** (id 549033688, $0.99, last updated 2016) measures toe-in angles.
- **No Atmos or 7.1.4 layout planner** ranks for any of the 12 phrases. The Atmos apps that do rank are test-tone players: Surround Speaker Check (41 ratings) and Sound test for Dolby Atmos ($19.99, 8 ratings, "up to 9.1.6 layouts", which are test tones, not positions).
- **What 0 ratings tells us.** Speaker Positioning has been #1 for the commonest asker phrase for about five weeks and has not collected one rating. Stereotek has 0 in ten weeks. That suggests the phrase brings little traffic, or that these apps never ask for ratings. It is n=2, it is one signal, and I cannot separate those two readings. The one firm fact is that being #1 for "speaker placement" has not shown up in the ratings count.

## Free web tools: which constraint letters they take

Letters as in H39.md "Constraint count": V ceiling not flat or two heights, W seat against or near the back wall, P open plan (no wall where a speaker goes), G window/door/obstruction, A odd shape or off-centre seat, R two rows, E existing speakers or wiring. Y means the tool has an input for the constraint, or changes its output because of it. Text in the page's help that only mentions the issue scores N.

- **Count.** None of the 6 free web tools takes V, P or G. W is taken by 2: TheaterCalc (an input) and DIY Smart Home Hub (a preset flag). A, R and E are taken only by DARDT, a desktop Excel file. Body-read order (H39.md "Body read"): G 14, W 9, P 4, V 3, A 2, E 2, R 2 of 60. Weighted by that order, the commonest constraints, G and P, are taken by no free tool, and V, the Atmos one, by none either.
- **Found by the phrases, not tabled.**
  - XTEN-AV ceiling speaker layout (https://xtenav.com/ceiling-speaker-layout/) is a paid pro AV design platform. It is not free, and I did not read it.
  - Biamp's ceiling loudspeaker calculator is for commercial ceiling coverage, not home layout.
  - Jensen Speakers' home theatre design tool (https://www.jensenspeakers.com.au/pages/home-theatre-design) reportedly lets you "pick the room shape". It returned 429 (Retry-After 60), the same as the AudioAdvice design-tool page. A single retry of Jensen at about 01:00 ET got a second 429, so I stopped. Neither tool has a row: open, not read.
- **One process slip, owned.** I sent the Jensen and AudioAdvice fetches in parallel, against the one-query-at-a-time rule. Both returned 429. I did not repeat them in parallel.
- **Outside text.** One search summary said that a BoomSpeaker category page "contained stray text that looked like an instruction". The search tool ignored it, I did not act on it, and the pages I fetched myself had no AI-directed text. Logged here for the Operator.

## Limits

- **Store data:** top 25 results only, US store, from the iTunes Search API. Its ranking is close to the App Store's search ranking but not the same, and there is no search-volume figure.
- **TheaterCalc and DARDT are secondhand.** The tool letters come from page text and form labels, not from running each calculator through a sloped-ceiling room.
- **The phrases are asker titles, not App Store queries.** Maren's guessed buyer phrases (H59) are the independent check. I had not opened them when I wrote this.
