## The failure first: no ceiling

- `CapturedRoom.Surface.Category` has five cases: `floor`, `door(isOpen:)`, `opening`, `wall`, `window`. There is no `ceiling` (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/capturedroom/surface/category-swift.enum).
- `CapturedRoom` itself lists `walls`, `doors`, `windows`, `openings`, `floors`, `objects`, `sections`, `story`. Again, no ceiling (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/capturedroom).
- WWDC22 warns that "high ceilings could exceed the limit of the scanning range of the LiDAR sensor" (https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10127/).
- ARKit does classify ceilings, outside RoomPlan:
  - `ARMeshClassification` has `.ceiling`, and the scene mesh needs LiDAR (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/armeshclassification, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arworldtrackingconfiguration/supportsscenereconstruction(_:)).
  - `ARPlaneAnchor.Classification` also has `.ceiling`, and that needs only an A12 or later chip (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arplaneanchor/classification-swift.enum, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arplaneanchor/isclassificationsupported).
- RoomPlan can run on an app's own ARSession through `init(arSession:)` (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/roomcapturesession). **No page I read says whether scene reconstruction keeps delivering ceiling mesh while RoomPlan <span title="What keeps an idea below the top: the cap that applies to it now.">holds</span> that session.** That needs a device spike after Day 7, and it is not proven.

Limits Apple states:
- One room scan works best at up to 30 by 30 ft (about 9 by 9 m), in 50 lux or more. Full-height mirrors, glass, and very dark surfaces are hard to scan (https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10127/).

| Letter | Constraint | Path | How, and the source |
|---|---|---|---|
| V | Ceiling not flat or not one height | **Captured in part, rest hand-marked** | Slope that meets a wall shows in that wall's `polygonCorners`, which covers shed and gable ceilings, where the high line sits on the end walls (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/capturedroom/surface/polygoncorners). | W | Seat against the back wall | **Captured** | `sofa` and `chair` objects come back as boxes with transforms, and walls come back as planes, so the gap from seat to back wall is arithmetic (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/capturedroom/object, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/capturedroom/object/category-swift.enum). The listening spot is a person's head, not furniture, so the user confirms it with a tap. |
| P | Open plan | **Captured in part, edge hand-marked** | Gaps in walls come back as `opening` surfaces, and the area gets `livingRoom`, `kitchen` or `diningRoom` sections (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/capturedroom/section). A side with no wall returns nothing, because RoomPlan returns walls that exist, so the user draws where the listening zone ends. Spaces beyond about 9 by 9 m are past the size Apple recommends for one scan (https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/10127/). MultiRoom can join the next room (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/scanning-the-rooms-of-a-single-structure). |
| G | Window, door or bulkhead in the way | **Captured (window, door); bulkhead hand-marked** | Each door, window and opening comes back as a `Surface` on its parent wall, and a door carries `isOpen` (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/capturedroom/surface/category-swift.enum). | A | Odd shape (L room, diagonal or corner setup, asymmetric) | **Captured; "front" hand-marked** | Walls are a list of planes, and curved walls carry `curve` (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/capturedroom/surface/curve-swift.struct). | R | Two rows | **Captured in part; riser hand-marked** | Each sofa and chair comes back with its position, so two rows of seats show as two lines of objects (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/roomplan/capturedroom/object). ## Without LiDAR: tap-to-measure

- **What Apple's Measure app does without LiDAR.** It finds rectangles on its own, and it measures by hand: you put the center dot on a start point, tap, pan to the end, and tap again. "For best results, use Measure on well-defined objects located 0.5 to 3 meters (2 to 10 feet) from iPhone. (Measurements are approximate.)" Edge guides and Ruler view are "On supported models", which links to the LiDAR model list (https://support.apple.com/en-us/guide/iphone/iphd8ac2cfea/26.0/ios/26.0).
- **What an app gets on any ARKit device.**
  - World tracking with horizontal and vertical plane detection (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arworldtrackingconfiguration).
  - Plane labels `wall`, `floor`, `ceiling`, `door`, `window`, `seat` and `table` on an A12 or later chip, which covers every iOS 26 iPhone (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arplaneanchor/classification-swift.enum, https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arplaneanchor/isclassificationsupported).
  - Raycasts from a screen tap to planes or estimated planes (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arraycastquery).
- **The fallback flow.**
  1. Tap two points per wall for its length, or type dimensions.
  2. Tap the floor and then the ceiling straight up for the height.
  3. Tap each door, window, seat and screen.
  4. Hand-mark V, P, R and E as on the LiDAR path.
- **Where it is weak.** Apple's raycast page says the LiDAR mesh is what lets a raycast hit "surfaces that have few or no features, such as a white wall" (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arraycastquery/target-swift.enum/estimatedplane). A plain white ceiling, where Atmos speakers go, is exactly that kind of surface, so the non-LiDAR ceiling tap is the least reliable step. It needs a typed-height fallback, and the device test comes after Day 7.

## Depth grade, and why

**B.** Ines graded depth B in research/hunt/H55-grades.md. I saw that grade in a search result before I wrote mine, so mine is not <span title="A second worker who grades the idea without seeing the first worker&#x27;s grades, and records them first.">blind</span>. I land on the same letter for a different reason.
- RoomPlan hands over most of the room for free: walls, doors, windows, openings, seats and the TV. That lowers depth on the capture side.
- What remains is real work:
  - rebuilding the ceiling from wall polygon tops, plus hand-marks for what sits inside the room;
  - a full second path for the users who have no LiDAR ([Withdrawn 2026-10-24 19:19 ET, Ines: an 'about half' size for that group, read from TelemetryDeck's panel against its terms; see research/hunt/H159.md 'Corrections (2026-10-24 18:53 ET, Ines)' and rulings-v2 row 'P2 terms'.]);
  - a constrained placement solver.
- None of it is ML. The only open technical question I found, ceiling mesh beside RoomPlan, is a spike, not a moat.
- It would move to B+ if the device spike shows `.ceiling` mesh arriving during a RoomPlan session, because then a sloped or tray ceiling is captured, not drawn.
