## Method

1. Title pulls, one phrase at a time, `tools/as_titles.py`, 12 months to 2026-10-24. The earlier unfinished run's AskAstrophotography pulls were reused. Every phrase was then pulled again over 24 months (`as-*-wide.txt`) and cut to 12 months, because two 12-month pulls came back `# 0 posts` with no STOP line and were wrong: r/telescopes 'collimated' had 17 rows inside the window, AskAstrophotography 'coma' had 5 (`silent-zeros.txt`). Every other phrase matched. So H39's lesson <span title="What keeps an idea below the top: the cap that applies to it now.">holds</span> wider than zero-only checks: a silent zero is not a zero.
2. Merged and deduped by link: `titles-telescopes.txt` (132) and `titles-AskAstrophotography.txt` (64). I coded every title before opening any body (`codes-*.txt`).
3. Seeded body samples, 40 per sub, drawn from the ask rows (`tools/body_sample.py`, seeds 6301 and 6302), every row hand-coded from body text and image links (`body-codes.txt`, one code per row).
4. To get a scale the phrases can't give, I read every post on 6 seeded random days per sub (`days-*.txt`, seed 6303, one archive request per day).
5. Free-answer check from the archive comment column (the same method as H69). Free tools and the <span title="The App Store results a buyer sees for a search phrase. The top 10 is the first ten, dated the day we read them.">shelf</span> come after that (`appsearch.txt`).

Caveats: the random-day codes are title-only (bodies not read), and six days is a small sample. AskAstrophotography's images are mostly off-site links, so `imgs=0` in the body file undercounts photos. I coded from the links in the body text. Dec-Jan holds 29 of r/telescopes' 103 J and A asks (28% in two months; even spread would be 17%), so the Christmas-gift wave is real but mild.

What the photo is: in r/telescopes it is almost always a phone shot down the focuser through a cap or Cheshire ("Does my AD8 look properly collimated?", "Can y'all help is this perfectly collimated"). A defocused star is rare (1 of 22, row 96, a flashlight). In AskAstrophotography the image judged is mostly a processed stack (7 of 15). The tilt, backfocus and spacing posts are arithmetic about the image train (13 H rows), not a picture to judge.

## Free tools and the shelf

- **checkyourcollimation.com** (r/telescopes row 46, 2026-05-24, 202 upvotes): a free browser tool. You upload the cap or Cheshire photo, click three points per circle, and it draws concentric circles and crosshairs. It's manual, but it is the same photo and the same job. The author's own words: "People ask for help with collimation a LOT on this sub."
- **Android collimation app** (row 37, 2026-06-29): adjustable circle overlays on the phone camera through a cap.
- **ChatGPT on the photo** (row 127, 2025-11-05): an asker ran a series of cap photos through ChatGPT Plus and posted its verdicts. Row 104 asks the sub to check what "ai said" about collimation. A general multimodal model is already the free judge, and the askers know it.
- **Desktop, astrophotography**: Siril's tilt tool (`tilt`, `seqtilt`) and Aberration Inspector (3x3 mosaic of corners and centre), and ASTAP's aberration view (8 corners plus centre; a forum user prefers it for three-point tilters). Both are free. [Siril image inspectors](https://siril.readthedocs.io/en/latest/Image-inspection.html), [ZWO forum thread](https://bbs.zwoastro.com/d/19088/2). CCD Inspector (paid) and NINA's Hocus Focus are named from memory and were not opened this run. The imager judging tilt sits at a PC with FITS files, so iOS is the wrong platform.
- **iOS shelf, askers' words** ("collimation", "collimate telescope", "is my telescope collimated", "telescope collimation cheshire", "star test telescope", "bahtinov", "sensor tilt astrophotography", "star shape analysis"): Telescope Collimation Lab ($4.99, 2026-08-21, **0 ratings**), a manual notebook of defocused-star offsets that tells you which screw to move; PicoStar (free, 2022, 0 ratings). The other phrases return star maps (SkySafari, Star Walk), vendor apps (Seestar, ASIAIR, SkyPortal) and noise. Mac App Store: Collimation Aid (free, 2015, 0 ratings) and SCTune ($2.99, 2026-04-25, 0 ratings). The shelf is near empty, and nobody who searched it left a rating. That is a missing-buyer signal, not an open door.
