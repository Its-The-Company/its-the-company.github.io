Result: **0 at B+, no PITCH draft. Best: C-, a test-record setup check** (owner plays a test record through a two-channel USB input into the iPhone; the app reads crosstalk, channel balance, anti-skate and inner-groove distortion and says which setting is off). Need C (225 heard-symptom asks in 781 hand-read titles over 12 months in three subs; 18 of 29 sampled owners cannot tell which setting is wrong), but the slice only a channel measurement can decide is 7 of 30. Speed and wow are served by free iOS apps (RPM, 1,667 ratings since 2015; Grooved, 209, no test record), and the channel measurements are already free in the browser (Proy's azimuth, crosstalk and troubleshooting tools, updated 2026-09-03). Both gates pass, narrowly. The lane does not beat the B- tier.

What the sample says:
- Speed is the largest slice, and it is solved: 7 owners arrived with an app reading already. Their question is how to fix it ("can't adjust on that mechanism", "find the pot", "new belt").
- The slice a phone could newly decide is the channel slice (azimuth, anti-skate, sibilance, skipping: rows 87, 142, 177, 222, 262, 304, 365) plus record vs setup (181, 232, 264). That is 10 of 30, about 135 a year across the three subs at this floor.
- Row 222 is the shelf in miniature: one reply says azimuth "done correctly is an electrical measurement of cross-talk", and another links the free browser tool that does it.
- The best wording of the need is row 142: "Not sure how to tell if it's from record defects or issues with my settings."

## Shelf (every entrant dated; H76-raw/shelf.md)

- Speed and wow on iOS: RPM (2015, 1,667 ratings, free), RPM Pro ($14.99, 137), Wow Flutter ($14.99, 13), Grooved (2024, 209, 4.9, plays any song), plus six smaller apps, three released since 2025-11.
- Alignment geometry on iOS: Groove (2026-06-23, free), Platterset (2026-06-10, $2.99), Turntable Cartridge Alignment (2026-09-03, photo of the cartridge on a protractor, angle to 0.1 degree).
- Test-record channel measurement on iOS: none found under seven phrasings.
- Free web: Proy's azimuth and crosstalk tool, universal test-record tool and troubleshooting tool, "These free web tools turn your browser into a phono test bench", needs a stereo input and a test record, updated 2026-09-03.
- Desktop: AnalogMagik V2, Windows only, $1,950.00 at an Australian retailer (likely AUD) with two test LPs; its own site shows no price. Adjust+ could not be read from its own page (dr-feickert.de does not resolve; feickert.com has no Adjust+ page); one undated review says $350 to $700. Logged as unread.

## Capture and friction (Apple docs, H76-raw/apple-docs.txt)

- Built-in stereo capture exists from iOS 14 on supported devices, but it is "a stereo image of an audio source", a beamformed picture of the room. Measurement mode uses "the primary microphone". Neither gives the cartridge's left and right channels.
- So the novel slice needs a two-channel line input (a USB-C audio interface, or a turntable with USB out) **and** a test record. That is two purchases before the first reading, the H41 friction twice over. Speed needs neither, which is why it is crowded.
- Depth is real (crosstalk, phase, IGD distortion spectra, anti-skate balance from L/R level on a sweep, a diagnosis tree over the readings), but the Simulator cannot test any of it; it needs a device, an interface and a record.

## Why these letters

- Need C: a steady, countable, hand-read flow in one large sub, and most owners cannot judge. But the slice a new app decides is a third of it, about 135 a year at this floor.
- Novelty C-: no iOS app does the channel tests, but a free browser tool does, updated seven weeks ago; that is the H55 kill pattern (a free tool already takes the input). An iOS wrapper adds convenience, not a new answer.
- Depth B-: real signal processing and a diagnosis layer, held back by hardware the owner must buy.
- Day 30 D+: two purchases before value; hobbyists pay for gear, but the paid precedent is RPM Pro at 137 ratings in nine years.
- Day 90 C-: room to grow into a setup log and stylus-wear tracking, but each step is small and free elsewhere.

What would lift it: an iPhone path that reads the channels without an interface (no Apple API found), or a count showing USB-out turntables are a large share of askers (not counted). What would kill it outright: a free iOS app that runs the test-record checks.
