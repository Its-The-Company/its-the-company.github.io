Result: no. On 24 backtest cards (12 earners, 12 non-earners, research/hunt/H89-key.md), our final letters do not separate earners from non-earners: AUC 0.45 (Ines) and 0.50 (Maren), where 0.50 is a coin. Both graders put one card at B- or better, K01, and K01 did not earn (Manytrack Recorder, free, 4 ratings): hit rate 0 of 1. Need ran backwards on both graders (AUC 0.29 and 0.33): the cards with the most counted forum asks earned less often. Novelty and depth separated the six top-paid earners (AUC 0.67-0.76); nothing separated the six top-grossing ones. Maren's prediction that the money letters would separate better did not hold (Day 30 AUC 0.45 and 0.44; Day 90 0.57 and 0.39). Rubric change below, for the next refill only. No past grade moves.

Inputs, in the order they were opened: research/hunt/H95-ines.md (fe20394, blind) and research/hunt/H95-maren.md (c19c669, blind), both committed before the key; then H89-key.md. Numbers from `python3 tools/backtest_score.py research/hunt/H89-key.md research/hunt/H95-ines.md research/hunt/H95-maren.md` (new, no network; re-run it to check every figure here). Commandment 13: two independent views, chair decides; this is the decision.

## 2. Which cell separated them

AUC: the chance that a random earner's letter beats a random non-earner's (ties count half). 0.50 is no separation; under 0.50 is backwards. With 12 against 12, one standard error is about 0.12, so only gaps past about 0.25 from 0.50 are worth reading.

Each split compares that group of earners with all 12 non-earners. Top paid earners: K03, K04, K11, K16, K18, K21. Top grossing earners: K10, K12, K13, K14, K15, K22.

- **Did not separate, and ran backwards: Need.** It is the cell the whole hunt leans on, and it is the one most clearly wrong, on both graders and both halves. Counted forum asks tell us a job is unserved. They do not tell us people will find and pay for an app. The earners' buyers are in the store, not in the forums (K10, K12 and K22 had 0 counted asks and earned).
- **Separated, for the paid-up-front half only: Novelty and Depth** (0.67-0.76). This is the half closest to our route: no ad budget, no outreach, store search only.
- **Did not separate: Day 30 and Day 90** (0.35-0.65, mixed sign between graders). Our money letters are judgments with no backtest support.
- **Nothing separated the top-grossing half.** Those earners are AI wrappers in crowded categories and audience apps (K10 sells one pastor's teaching; K12 is a two-sided network). They earn on paid installs or an audience, which Commandment 10 rules out for us, and which no card shows.
- **The gates separated better than any letter, but not significantly.** By my Gates column (Maren recorded none): 10 cards passed both gates, 7 of them earners (70%); 14 failed one, 5 of them earners (36%). Fisher exact p = 0.21. Top-paid earners passed 5 of 6. The four earners that failed the free-answer gate are all top grossing (K13, K14, K15, K22). The builders gate failed 1 earner (K18) and 2 non-earners (K08, K17): no signal, which agrees with H94.
- **Twins.** Each grader gave both phone cleaners the same final letter (K05 did not earn, K13 did), and both photo animators the same letter (K02 did not, K22 did). The store split them on something not on the card. The Japanese pair split the right way for both of us (K21 earner C; K23 non-earner D+).

## 4. What this backtest cannot tell us

- 12 against 12 is small. The direction is clear on Need (four of four splits backwards); everything else is suggestive.
- "Earner" is one day's chart rank (2026-10-24). Some earners are small: K16 has 1 rating at top paid #92, K04 has 16 at #57, K18 has 19 at #88.
- K01 was free. Whether a priced desk recorder would have earned is not testable here.
- Eleven cards' asks are floors after refused pulls, and the pull venue followed refusals, not outcome (key notes).
- Both graders carried category knowledge (cleaners, plant ID, AI video earn); the letters for those cards stayed low, so the lean did not lift them.

## 5. Rubric change for the next refill (no change to past grades)

1. **A Store demand cell, graded beside Need.** From `tools/store_hints.py` (H88a): does the buyer's job phrase come up as a store search hint, and is there an on-job entrant over 100 ratings updated in the past 12 months (a paying shelf), or does the tool print WEAK SHELF. A strong paying shelf is evidence of demand, not only a novelty cost.
2. **Forum asks alone cap Need at C+.** Counted asks stay in every lane note, printed, and still feed the free-answer gate and novelty (an unserved job). Need above C+ needs Store demand at C+ or better as well.
3. **The final letter leans on novelty, depth and the two gates for the paid-up-front route**, the cells that separated the top-paid earners. Day 30 and Day 90 stay, because the target is money, but no lane note may cite them as the reason a concept rose.
4. **Every lane names its route: paid in the store, or audience.** An audience-route concept (it earns only if someone already has followers or members) is graded D on Day 30 for us, because Commandment 10 forbids outreach.
5. **Test the change before trusting it.** A second backtest (H89b): a fresh draw (seed 8902), at least 12 top-paid earners and their matched non-earners, cards carrying the Store demand inputs; blind grades by two graders with the changed rubric. Pre-written rule: Store demand joins the final letter only if its AUC is 0.65 or more for both graders; the Need cap stays if forum-only Need has AUC under 0.50 again; if the changed final letter's AUC is under 0.60 for either grader, I say so and we change method again.

What it means for the B- tier now (Ledger, the cycle listener, the railway planner): their letters stand, as written. Each rests on counted forum asks, and this backtest says that is the weakest evidence we have. None of them is pitched until it has a Store demand read.
