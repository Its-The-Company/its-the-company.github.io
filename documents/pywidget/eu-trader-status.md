## Answers

1. **What must be declared.** Every developer who has an app on the App Store in the EU declares trader status, and you must declare a status even if you do not distribute in the EU (P1: "Even if you don't distribute apps in the EU, you'll still need to declare a trader status"). The choices in App Store Connect are "This is a trader account" or "This is not a trader account" (P1). A trader also certifies it only offers products or services that comply with EU law, and gives payment account details if not already entered (P1). The developer must assess this itself: "Apple can't determine whether you're a trader" (P1). The Account Holder or an Admin does it, signed in (P1), so it is the Investors' step. The status can later be turned off or specified per app (P1).
2. **By when.** Apple's prompt comes "next time you submit a new app in App Store Connect" (P1). Since 2024-10-16 trader status is required to submit updates for EU apps (P2). Since 2025-02-17 apps without it are removed in the EU (P2). So for pywidget: before the first submission (planned for Day 25, 2026-11-15; ops/plans/pywidget/plan.md), and the verification (email and phone codes) takes time.
3. **What Apple publishes, given #98 1B (individual, no entity).** If trader: Apple shows the address, phone number and email you give it on the app's product page in each of the 27 EU territories, once verified (P1). Individuals enter an address or P.O. Box, phone number and email (P1); organisations get the D-U-N-S address shown automatically (not our case). Apple says these are for display only and do not change the Developer Program membership record (P1). Apple's page does not say the seller's legal name is shown by this feature; the seller name on the page is the separate #98 question. If not a trader: nothing is shown, and EU consumers are told that consumer rights from consumer protection laws do not apply to contracts between you and them (P1).
4. **Exactly what would be public (trader).** Three items the Investor chooses and types: (a) a postal address or P.O. Box, (b) a phone number that can receive a verification code (or manual verification with documents), (c) an email address, verified by a code. All three are shown on the product page in the EU. A home address, personal mobile or personal email would be published there. A P.O. Box (needs a bill or receipt linking you to it for verification, P1), a number used only for this, and the #98 pywidget-only support mailbox keep personal details out. The company <span title="What keeps an idea below the top: the cap that applies to it now.">holds</span> none of these and must not guess them (Commandment 42).
5. **Without a declaration.** Apple: apps without trader status "will be removed from the App Store in the European Union (EU) until trader status is provided and verified" (P2). Updates for EU apps cannot be submitted without it (P2). For a new app, App Store Connect asks at submission (P1).
6. **Is "not a trader" honest for a paid app?** What Apple's page says (P1): factors include "Whether you make revenue as a result of your app, for example if your app includes In-App Purchases, or if it's a paid or ad-sponsored app", commercial practices toward consumers, VAT registration, and developing the app in a professional or business capacity. A person is "unlikely" to be a trader only when acting outside trade, business, craft or profession, with a hobbyist who never intended to commercialise given as the example. Also: a legal status tied to a business activity suggests trader, "regardless of whether you're an individual developer or organization". pywidget is built to earn money ($200 goal) and sells a paid unlock, so by Apple's own factors "not a trader" is hard to square. The call is the Investors' and Apple suggests a legal advisor if unsure.
7. **Interaction with worldwide sales.** Declaring a trader is needed only for EU storefronts; Apple says a developer who sells only outside the EU is not acting as a trader on the App Store (P1), though it must still answer the question. So the Investors have a real choice: (A) trader, EU sold, contact details public in 27 territories; (B) not a trader, EU sold (hard to defend, item 6); (C) "not a trader" because the EU storefronts are switched off (a change to the worldwide plan, losing EU sales). Worldwide sales is a fixed decision in the Commandments (The Mission, Fixed decisions), so (C) needs an Investor amendment.
