Direction C, screen 1: Projects

Direction C is creative-first. The screen opens on making and on finished things: one large card that starts the Builder, then the person's projects as tiles, each showing the thing it makes. The programmer is one tap from code everywhere: "Write Python" on the start row, and a code button on every tile. C stands on Paper's colours in light and Studio's in dark, and adds its own tile tints, a rounded title face and a larger type scale. Sample data throughout. Project, Builder and Gallery are placeholder names.

iPhone, 390 wide

What to look at:

  • The first third of the screen is for starting. "Make a widget" opens the Builder and is the largest thing on the screen. "Ready-made" opens the Gallery. "Write Python" opens a new project with the editor and the preview: one tap from home to code. These are 03's three ways to start, laid out as tiles in place of a menu under New.
  • Each project is a tile holding the thing it makes, at 77% of its real size. The line under the name says how it was made.
  • The round button on each tile opens that project's Python: its own code, or the read-only Python the blocks produce.
  • The cost, measured: this screen shows 4 projects where direction A's list shows 7 rows. The second row runs under the tab bar and scrolls.

iPad

The same parts with more room. A tile is as wide as the thing it makes: the Weather project has a medium widget, so its tile spans two columns and shows it at real size. The last tile offers one finished widget from the Gallery, with Get and Make it mine. The tabs are the system's top bar on iPad.
