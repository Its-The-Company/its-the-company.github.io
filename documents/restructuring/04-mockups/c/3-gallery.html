Proposal, not approved

Direction C, screen 4: the Gallery

The Gallery is where a marketplace would live. In direction C it is shelves of tiles, and every tile holds a finished thing drawn live by the app. The three shelves are named for the act: get a widget, make a starter your own in the Builder, add a part to a project. A part is drawn as the same block card the Builder uses. The programmer is one tap from the Python on every widget tile, before anything is installed. Drawn as 1.0 would have it under 03's working assumptions: every item is the company's own and ships inside the app, with no account, no rating and no price. Sample data throughout. Gallery, Builder and Project are placeholder names.

iPhone, 390 wide

What to look at:

  • Three shelves, each named for what a person does there, each scrolling sideways. Finished widgets come first and are the largest things on the screen.
  • Every picture is the app's own live drawing of the widget, at 77% of its real size on the first shelf. No picture comes from an item's maker (03 question 8).
  • The line under each name says what the item touches, before a tap.
  • One filled button on each widget tile: Get. The round button beside it opens the item's Python, read only, before anything is installed.
  • A starter opens in the Builder. A part is the same card, with the same tint by kind, as the block it becomes there.
  • The cost, measured: 4 items are whole on this screen (2 widgets, 1 starter, 1 part) with 3 more peeking. Direction A's list shows 5 rows and B's shows 10.

iPad

The shelves keep the left 60% of the screen. The chosen item opens on the right, in the place where the Builder and the editor keep their stage: the widget at real size, 03's three choices, what it touches, who made it and its version, then what is inside it as blocks. Its Python is one tap away, through the round button.

What to look at:

  • The ring marks the tile that is open on the right, as it marks the chosen block in the Builder.
  • The widget on the stage is at real size. The switch beside its name shows the other size, and is the same control as Blocks and Code.
  • The three choices sit in one row: Get keeps a copy, Make it mine opens that copy in the Builder, Add to project copies one part.
  • A newer version is offered and never installed for the person (03 question 3).
  • Directions A and B show the item's Python on this screen. C shows its blocks and keeps the Python one tap away. That is C's choice everywhere, and here it has a cost: a person must tap once to read the code before they install.

Open, for the Investors

  • No tile carries a price or a Pro mark. The approved Pro purchase holds 7 of the premades (#87), and phase 2's question 8 recommends that installing stays free. How a Pro premade should look on a shelf is not a layout choice, so C draws neither, as A and B do not.
  • No Python is drawn on this screen. Where the round button leads, the sample Python on screens 2 and 3 is placeholder, not the app's API.
  • Names, versions, counts and the maker line are sample content. No licence is named because none is chosen.

Where this screen departs, and what it keeps

  • Kept: the Gallery as the second of the four approved tabs, the approved widget card inside every picture, every approved colour. The full table is on screen 1.
  • Departs from the approved drawings: shelves of tinted tiles that scroll sideways. The Gallery tab and an item's page have no approved drawing; the approved "gallery" drawing is the sheet of widgets.
  • Departs from 03: the item's blocks stand where 03 allows "its blocks or code"; the code is behind one tap.
  • New here and in no token file: the six tile tints, used for widget tiles and, by kind, for parts.
