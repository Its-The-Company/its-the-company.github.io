Direction C, screen 5: the preview

In direction C the preview is a showroom, not a report. The widget stands on a stage that looks like the place it is going to, a Home Screen, in each of its sizes at real size. A run is told in plain words: three steps and a tick. The Python is one tap away, through the same round code button that sits on every project tile. Sample data throughout. No Python is drawn on this page, and the file name on it is a placeholder. Project, Page and Builder are placeholder names.

iPhone, 390 wide

What to look at:

  • The stage is the top 510 px of an 844 px screen. It stands in for a Home Screen: plain squares where app icons would be. It is not the person's own Home Screen; the app cannot see that.
  • Both sizes at real size, 158 and 338 px wide. The small widget takes the room of four icons and the medium of eight, as they will. The line under each says whether it has been added. That line assumes the app can ask iOS which sizes are placed; I recall that it can and have not checked it this round.
  • One switch for the look: Light, Dark, Tinted, Clear. It is the same control as Blocks and Code. Tinted and Clear are named, not drawn in this round.
  • The last run is a card in plain words: what was fetched, what was drawn, when it was saved. The round arrow runs it again. Nothing here says "run 14" or "0.21 s"; those are in the console.
  • Code is one tap away: the round button at the top right opens this widget's Python. It is the button on every project tile, in a fixed place.
  • The title is a menu. It offers exactly the project's outputs: Today, Refresh and, marked as planned, Forecast.
  • One large button, where "Add a block" and "Run" sit in the tools. It shows the steps. The person places a widget; the app cannot, and the line above the button says so.

iPad

With more room the stage is drawn twice, once in each look, so nobody flips a switch to compare them. The left column lists what the project makes and what this widget is made with: the blocks and the Python are one tap each. The Forecast page is listed and marked "planned for 1.1". The switch keeps three looks, because Light and Dark are both on the screen.

A run, as the person sees it

Three moments of one run, for a person who never opens the editor. Each state has its own shape and its own words, so colour is not the only signal: a turning ring, a tick, a cross. When a run fails, the card says what happened, says the widget still shows its last good result, and offers two buttons. "See what happened" opens the console at the line that failed: one tap from the failure to the code.

  • The steps are sample content. For a widget made with blocks the first step can take its name from the data block. For hand-written Python it could only say "Ran your Python".
  • Whether the app can tell the three steps apart today, and name the step that failed, is for Idris and Pernille to say. Direction B's preview asks for the same facts as numbers.

Where this screen departs, and what it keeps

  • Kept: the widget itself, drawn as approved in both looks and at real size; every approved colour; 03's one preview for every output, every size, light and dark; one filled button. The full table is on screen 1.
  • Departs from 03: on iPhone the preview is a whole screen opened from the floating picture or the Builder's stage, not a sheet pulled up from the bottom. The project's outputs are a list on iPad and a menu under the title on iPhone, where 03 has a switch.
  • Departs from directions A and B: no data table, no timing and no counted checks on this screen. The run is three plain lines, and the numbers stay in the console.
  • New here and in no token file: the sky tint as a stage, and the mint, sun and rose tints as the three states of a run.
  • Not drawn: Tinted and Clear, a Page, the Add to Home Screen steps, larger text sizes, Increase Contrast.

Measured, and not

  • Contrast by script: 298 pieces of text in the six frames, 36 colour pairs, and the page's own 6. None is under 4.5:1. The lowest is 4.77:1, the approved label on the approved dark card. The lowest of C's own is 5.52:1, the secondary text on the rose tint in light.
  • The same script tried C's six text colours on all six tints in both appearances: 5 of 72 pairs are under 4.5:1. They are the tertiary text on rose and lilac in light and on mint and sun in dark, and the success green on lilac in light. This page uses none of the five.
  • Rendered in Chromium 148 only: 6 of 6 frames read by eye, no console errors, no sideways scroll at 390. Text fit was judged on the renders, not by script. Not opened in Safari or Firefox. Nothing ran on a device.
  • The script is in a scratch folder, not in the repo.
