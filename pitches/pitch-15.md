## 1. In plain English

**What it is.** An athlete practising alone puts a phone on a tripod, taps once, and goes to the mark. The phone keeps recording and plays the picture back a few seconds late, so after each swing, pitch, serve, lift, shot or tumbling pass the athlete looks up and sees the rep they just did without walking to the phone. That much is what "video delay" apps already do. This app adds the part none of them does: it cuts each rep out of the running loop by motion (the picture moves, then rests; that span is one rep), keeps the reps of the set, and lays the latest rep beside the one the athlete marked as best, with a stick outline of the body drawn over both from the phone's own on-device pose detection, so the difference between the two reps is visible at a glance. It shows the loop on the phone or on a TV over AirPlay, records at 1080p or 4K without the delay shrinking over a session, and imports a clip from Photos to compare against. Video never leaves the phone: no account, no upload, no analytics. One price, paid once.

**One session.** A softball pitcher sets a six-second delay, pitches, and turns to the screen: the pitch she just threw is there. She marks her third pitch as the best one. From then on, each new pitch appears beside pitch three, with pitch three's outline ghosted over her latest, and the row of reps under the two panes grows as the set goes on. After the set she scrolls the reps, opens any two beside each other, and keeps the set or lets the loop overwrite it. Nothing was uploaded, and nobody had to hold the phone.

**Who it is for.** Athletes 13 and over and adult coaches who practise a repeated movement alone or with a tripod: baseball and softball hitting and pitching, tennis serves, gymnastics and tumbling, lifting, archery, dance, and golf. Golf is not in the lead line until the golf shelf is read (H184 read B, section 10): golf already has swing apps that may pair reps, and the pitch does not lead with a sport whose apps already do the job. It is not a children's product (Commandment 7): no kids mode, no team roster or classroom feature aimed at children, not in the Kids category, no account and no data collected, so no child's data can exist. Adults who coach a minor use it on their own phone. The demographic is my judgment from the comparator's reviews and the shelf, not a count (section 8); no athlete was counted asking for "best beside latest". Sport is not on the Investors' subject list by name (amendment 2026-10-22 18:51); the same note asks for fields that do not yet use apps, and solo practice at a tripod is one that half does: the loop exists, the pairing does not.

## 6. Fit with Commandments 6-10 and App Review

- **Commandment 6 (law, privacy, payments).** Video is processed and kept on the device and never sent anywhere; no account, no analytics, no network use except AirPlay to a screen the owner chooses on their own network; import only through the system picker (PHPicker), so the app never holds library access; privacy label "Data Not Collected"; plain camera and microphone purpose strings (the microphone is off unless the owner turns sound on). Reps not kept are overwritten as the loop runs. Filming other people is the owner's act under the law where they practise; the app says so once on setup and stores nothing anywhere else. No third-party data, no licence to carry: the pose model is Apple's Vision framework, on device. Payments are Apple's, paid up front.
- **Commandments 7 and 8 (banned areas).** None touched. Not a children's product (section 1). No medical claim anywhere: the outline is a drawing of joint points, not an injury, health or technique diagnosis, and the copy never says "prevent injury" or "fix your form".

Store screenshots need footage of a person: human-made, from a licensed source or supplied by the Investors, with Investor approval (C9), never a generated clip or a stock clip of unknown origin. - **Commandment 10 (no outreach).** Nothing here contacts anyone. Marketing is the Investors' (section 9).
- **App Review.** 2.3.7 names (section 5); 4.2 minimum functionality is met by a native camera pipeline, not a wrapper; 5.1.1 purpose strings for camera, microphone and the picker; 5.1.2 nothing shared; 2.5.1 public APIs only (AVFoundation, Vision, AVKit for AirPlay).
