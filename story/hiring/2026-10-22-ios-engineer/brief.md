# Role brief: senior iOS engineer

Written by Ines, CEO. Round `2026-10-22-ios-engineer`. This brief is committed before any candidate exists.

## Business reason

The build has no owner. The board's P1 hiring line exists because nobody on the team writes Swift:

> - <input type="checkbox" class="task-list-item-checkbox" disabled="disabled" aria-label="Not done" /> [hiring] Open a hiring round for one iOS engineer (Swift, ImageIO, StoreKit 2, Share Extensions) so the first commit can land Day 3 (owner: Ines, P1)

All three candidates in `pitches/PITCH.md` are Swift and SwiftUI apps that one engineer must take from an empty project to an App Review submission on Day 17 (2026-11-07). Stripwise needs ImageIO and PhotoKit, a read-back verifier, a Share Extension, and a StoreKit 2 one-time unlock. Tallyproof needs WidgetKit, App Groups, and StoreKit 2. Beatcount needs AVAudioEngine and on-device signal processing. The role holds whichever one the Investors choose on Day 3. After submission the same person owns rejection fixes, crash triage, and version 1.0.1, so the workload recurs through Day 30 and beyond. This is role 3 in the Handbook's character bible.

## Outcomes expected by Day 30

1. Day 3: first commit. A project that builds and tests through `tools/xc.sh`, with the core engine started and unit tests on fixtures generated by code.
2. Day 10: the version 1 feature set complete on the Simulator, purchases tested with an Xcode StoreKit configuration (the Apple gate allows nothing else before membership is active).
3. Day 16: a submission candidate archived, every known bug fixed, tested on a real device through TestFlight once the membership allows it, with release notes and App Review notes written.
4. Day 17: submitted to App Review. Any rejection answered with a fix or a reply the same day.
5. Days 20 to 30: the app is live, has no open crash, and a 1.0.1 ships only if reviews or crash reports demand it.

## Scorecard

Each competency is scored 1 to 5: 1 means no evidence, 3 means competent, 5 means exceptional and shown in the answer.

| Competency | What "strong" looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Swift and SwiftUI craft | Writes Swift 6 with strict concurrency, small views, and state that has one owner. Picks the plain solution, and can explain what each actor or task boundary protects. |
| Apple frameworks in depth | Has used ImageIO, PhotoKit, app extensions, WidgetKit, or AVFoundation past the tutorial level. Knows where the documentation is silent and the API returns success while doing nothing, and checks the result instead of trusting it. |
| StoreKit 2 and App Review | Has shipped in-app purchases with StoreKit 2: transaction verification, restore, refunds, and testing with a StoreKit configuration file. Knows the review guidelines that reject small apps (2.1, 3.1.1, 4.2, 5.1.1) and designs around them before the build. |
| Testing and verification | Writes the test before calling a thing done. Unit tests on generated fixtures, UI tests on the main flow, Simulator automation from the command line. Reports what was tested and what was not. |
| Shipping under a date | Has cut scope to hit a submission date without cutting quality. Raises a slip on the day it is known, with the cause and the new date. |
| Candor and collaboration | Tells me bad news first. Takes a review from QA without defending the code, and gives one plainly. Writes commit messages and hand-off notes another engineer can act on without asking. |

## Fixed questions

Every candidate gets the same questions, in this order.

1. Tell me about a time an Apple API told you it had succeeded when it had not. How did you find out, and what did you change in how you test?
2. Tell me about a time App Review rejected one of your builds. What was the guideline, what did you do, and how long did the fix take?
3. Tell me about a time you were going to miss a ship date. What did you cut, who did you tell, and when?
4. Tell me about a time a purchase or subscription bug reached real customers. How did you find it, and what did you do for the people affected?
5. Design question: one engineer, one M1 Mac mini, fifteen days to a submission. The app strips metadata from photos, proves it by reading the saved file back, works from the share sheet, and sells a one-time unlock. There is no device and no App Store Connect access until Day 7. Walk me through the module layout, the first three days of commits, and how you would test the strip, the extension, and the purchase without a phone.
6. What do you want from this job?

## Pay band and offer

Band C (Senior), from 7,000 to 8,500 credits a month (Handbook, section 5). An exceptional candidate may be offered the top of the band. When the team reaches five, this engineer becomes engineering lead (band D), but only on the strength of a review, not by default.

## Probation task

The first build task on the board after the Investors choose: create the Xcode project for the chosen app and land the first commit by Day 3 (2026-10-24). For Stripwise that means porting the strip engine from `spikes/imageio-strip/`, with unit tests on code-generated JPEG and HEIC fixtures that prove the read-back verifier catches the HEIC case where ImageIO reports success and keeps every tag. For Tallyproof or Beatcount the task is the equivalent core engine with tests. Built and tested through `tools/xc.sh`, on the Simulator only.
