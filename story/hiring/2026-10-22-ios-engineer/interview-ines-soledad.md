# Interview: Soledad, senior iOS engineer

Round `2026-10-22-ios-engineer`. Interviewer: Ines, CEO. Six fixed questions from the brief, in order.

**Ines:** Thanks for coming in. Six questions, then I score. Question one. Tell me about a time an Apple API told you it had succeeded when it had not. How did you find out, and what did you change in how you test?

**Soledad:** A share extension that wrote to an App Group container. The write call returned no error. The host app read nothing. The App Group identifier in the extension's entitlements had a typo, so the container URL resolved to a private sandbox folder. No failure anywhere. I found it on the Simulator by listing the directory from the command line. Since then every write I ship has a read-back test: write, reopen from the other process, compare. I am not an imaging specialist. I have not had to chase a metadata bug inside ImageIO. I would read the saved bytes, not the return value, and I would write that test first.

**Ines:** Would you trust the success flag on a strip call?

**Soledad:** No. I would parse the output file and assert on the tags.

**Ines:** Question two. Tell me about a time App Review rejected one of your builds.

**Soledad:** Three times across fifteen years. Two were 3.1.1: a restore path that was missing, and a "unlock" button that did not use in-app purchase. The third was 4.2, minimum functionality, on a small utility that looked like a web wrapper. Each fix shipped within a day. For 3.1.1 I added a visible Restore Purchases button and rewrote the review notes with the exact tap path. For 4.2 I added two native features and said so in the notes. Now I read the guidelines before the first commit and put restore, price, and the test path in the notes before I submit.

**Ines:** Question three. A time you were going to miss a ship date.

**Soledad:** A Monday, a Friday date, a client app. By Monday noon I could see the sync work would not finish. I told the client that day: Friday would slip by a week if we kept everything. I offered the alternative, which was to cut a third of the scope, sync and one settings screen, and ship Friday. They took the cut. We shipped Friday with a clean build. Sync went in the next release, eight days later. What ships Friday? That is the question I ask on Monday.

**Ines:** Question four. A purchase or subscription bug that reached real customers.

**Soledad:** A one-time unlock that did not re-check entitlements on launch, so a customer who restored on a second device stayed locked until relaunch. I found it from two support emails on one morning, not from a test. I reproduced it in a StoreKit configuration in an hour. The fix was to listen to Transaction.updates at launch and to check current entitlements before drawing the paywall. I shipped it the next day. For the people affected, I wrote a reply template that told them the cause and the fix, and I told the owner to offer them the choice of a refund, which is the owner's call, not mine. I added a UI test for the restore-on-second-install path.

**Ines:** Question five, the design question.

**Soledad:** Three modules. A core package with no UI: StripEngine, which reads and writes through ImageIO; Verifier, which re-opens the saved file and compares tags; and Entitlements, a thin wrapper over StoreKit 2. An app target with SwiftUI. A share extension that links only the core package. Shared files go through an App Group, with a read-back test, because of what I said in question one.
Day 1: project, package, strip engine for JPEG, tests on fixtures generated by code, building through tools/xc.sh. Commit. Day 2: HEIC, and the Verifier that fails the strip if any tag survives. This is the day the probation task lives. Commit. Day 3: the SwiftUI flow, with a result screen that shows the verifier's receipt, and the StoreKit configuration file with the unlock product. Commit.
Testing without a phone. The strip: unit tests on generated JPEG and HEIC fixtures with known GPS and camera tags, and the verifier as the oracle. The extension: the Simulator, with simctl to add photos and to launch it, plus a UI test on the main app for the hand-off. Anything I cannot automate goes on a written list of what is untested until TestFlight. The purchase: an Xcode StoreKit configuration, the StoreKitTest framework for buy, restore, refund, and ask-to-buy, all from the command line. The one thing I cannot test is the real sandbox, and I say so in the report. I would also cut one thing: a batch mode. Cut it, then do it well.

**Ines:** What do you cut first if Day 10 slips?

**Soledad:** The share extension polish, not the verifier. The verifier is the product. I would tell you the same day with the new date.

**Ines:** Question six. What do you want from this job?

**Soledad:** A product that earns in its first month. I want to ship it clean and watch the number. And the engineering lead title once the team reaches five, on the strength of a review. I have marked Day 17 on my calendar already.

**Ines:** Thank you. That is all. You will hear from me by the end of the round.
