# Role brief: market and product researcher

Written by Ines, CEO. Round `2026-10-22-market-researcher`. This brief is committed before any candidate exists.

## Business reason

The board has P1 work with no owner:

> - [ ] [research] Phase 1 research: App Store charts and paid rankings, 1 to 3 star reviews, forums, search gaps, pricing; findings in research/ with links (owner: the market researcher, P1)
> - [ ] [research] Phase 1 synthesis and the Pitch on Fable: pitches/PITCH.md, 2 to 4 candidates (owner: Ines with the researcher, P1)

Research is the workload the month depends on. A weak idea costs the whole month. I can judge evidence, but I can't run a disciplined research pass across charts, reviews, communities, and trademarks while also planning, hiring, and reporting. After the Pitch, the same person owns the evidence behind pricing, the App Store page, and post-launch review mining, so the role recurs.

## Outcomes expected by Day 30

1. Before the Pitch: `research/` holds sourced findings across every route the Commandments name (charts, low-star reviews, communities, search gaps, pricing), each with a link, and no page is fetched twice.
2. Two to four candidate memos in `pitches/`, scored on the Phase 1 criteria, with demand sized in numbers (how many sales reach $200 net at the proposed price) and name checks (USPTO, App Store, domains).
3. A Pitch the Investors can choose from without asking for more research.
4. After the Pitch: competitor teardowns that feed the version 1 scope, keyword and pricing evidence for the App Store page, and a review-mining routine after launch.

## Scorecard

Each competency is scored 1 to 5: 1 means no evidence, 3 means competent, 5 means exceptional and shown in the answer.

| Competency | What "strong" looks like |
| --- | --- |
| App Store market analysis | Reads charts, category rankings, price tiers, and review volume as signals of demand. Knows what the numbers can't tell you, and estimates downloads and revenue with stated assumptions. |
| Evidence discipline | Separates a claim from its source. Every finding has a link and a confidence level. Stops when more reading won't change the answer, and says why. |
| Novelty and gap finding | Turns repeated complaints and workarounds into a product angle no existing app offers. Spots a reskin or clone before it becomes a pitch. |
| Commercial judgment | Turns an idea into a price, a purchase model, and the number of sales needed for $200 net after Apple's commission. Can tell whether a target is plausible for a new, unmarketed app. |
| Rules and risk awareness | Screens out the banned areas (Commandment 7), App Review rejection risks (spam, duplicates, and 2.1 incomplete apps), and trademark conflicts early. Research stays passive: no contact with anyone. |
| Synthesis and writing | Writes short, scored memos a busy reader can decide from, with the recommendation first. |

## Fixed questions

Every candidate gets the same questions, in this order.

1. Tell me about a time your research changed what a team decided to build. What was the evidence, and what did the team believe before?
2. Tell me about a time you stopped researching. How did you know you had enough, and were you right?
3. Tell me about a time a source you relied on turned out to be wrong or misleading. What did you change afterward?
4. Tell me about a time you had to estimate a market with almost no data. How did you build the number, and how close was it?
5. Design question: we have about four weeks to get one new, unmarketed iOS app to $286 in customer purchases. Walk me through the first eight hours of your research, what you'd read and in what order, and what a finding would have to show for you to put it in a pitch.
6. What do you want from this job?

## Pay band and offer

Band C (Senior), from 7,000 to 8,500 credits a month (Handbook, section 5). An exceptional candidate may be offered the top of the band.

## Probation task

The first P1 research task on the board: the Phase 1 research pass, with findings in `research/` with links, ending in a ranked shortlist of four to six candidate ideas, each with its evidence, for the Fable synthesis. It runs on Sonnet for the reading.
